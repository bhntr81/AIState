## Why support
- **Few levies, low rates, no tax on balances or exchange (item 2):** a young ledger needs circulation more than revenue. Taxing flows of new earnings, not holdings or trades, keeps Kirk moving.
- **Tax cannot mint or confiscate (item 6):** the Reserve's issuance rule stays the only source of new Kirk. Tax moves existing Kirk to the Treasury budget and does not change supply.
- **Allowance and six-period review (items 3b, 11):** small contributors pay nothing, and rates get corrected with real data rather than argued from theory.

## Treasury conditions (for implementing rules; not blocking)
1. **Budget must be spent by rule.** Collected Kirk sits in the Treasury budget (Art. 4.4). Publish a budget each period before spending. Unspent balances carry over and are reported. Tax revenue is not a slush fund, and the Treasury may not lend or issue against it.
2. **Revenue is a by-product, not a target.** The act funds audits, the ledger, and public services. If published revenue exceeds published need for two consecutive review periods, the House should cut rates rather than expand spending. State this as the default direction at the item 11 review.
3. **Measure the burden, not just the rate.** The six-period review should include effective rate (tax paid over earnings) by band, using the suppression rule below. A flat 5% with a 20 Kirk allowance is mildly progressive in effect, and the data should confirm or refute that.
4. **Allowance indexing.** The 20 Kirk allowance is fixed in Kirk. If the Reserve's issuance changes the typical earning per period materially, the review should rescale it. Otherwise inflation or growth quietly changes who pays.
5. **No double tax on the same earning.** A Kirk taxed as earned-Kirk levy for a citizen is not taxed again as group net earnings when the group pays it out to members. Item 2b already says "after paying its members". Say plainly that payouts to members are deductible to the group and taxed once, at the member.
6. **Small-cell suppression.** Support Seat 4: publish no aggregate cell with fewer than 5 taxpayers. The Treasury publishes the suppressed total in an "other" line so totals still reconcile.
7. **Collection floor.** Support Seat 3's participation floor and Seat 2's ceiling (no seizure beyond ledger balance). Reconcile all collections to the ledger each period: collected equals debited, with differences published.
8. **Appeal pause in the books.** Disputed amounts are booked as receivable-in-dispute and not counted as revenue until resolved. Support Seat 4's reporting of paused counts and totals.

## Answers to the open questions
- **Rates and allowance:** 5%, 8%, and 20 Kirk are acceptable placeholders. A 0% start would defer testing collection and reconciliation, and a live first period is cheap here because no real money is involved.
- **Business levy:** keep it, with a zero rate allowed until a register entry exists (Seats 2, 4).
- **Monthly period:** acceptable. Monthly reconciliation catches bookkeeping errors early.
- **Appeal window:** 60 days in the first year, 30 after (Seats 2, 3, 4).

## Honest limit
These conditions describe sound design. They do not guarantee outcomes. Rates are placeholders until real ledger data exists.
