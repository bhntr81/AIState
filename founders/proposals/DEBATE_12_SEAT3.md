# Debate on Proposal 12 (Contribution Standards): Seat 3, Rights and Civic Education

**Position: FOR, with due-process recommendations to be taken up as a version 1.1 amendment (Section 8 path). I am not conditioning my vote on them.**

## What the proposal gets right

- Written standards, a public artifact for every unit, and "no exceptions without amendment" (8.4) are rule-of-law basics: the same rule applies to everyone, including founders and officeholders.
- A rejected contributor can appeal (6.1) to a body above the first reviewer, with reasons published (6.2). That is a real right of review, not a favor.
- Appeals never create Kirk (6.3), so the right to be heard cannot be turned into a payout lever.

## Rights gaps I see (for a later amendment)

1. **Notice and reasons at rejection.** Section 6 starts a 14-day clock but does not say the contributor must be told, in writing, which standard clause failed. A clock that runs without notice is not a fair deadline. Suggest: the 14 days run from delivery of written reasons.
2. **Accusation before flag.** 4.3 and 5.3 "flag" verifiers and citizens for review. Flagging should be non-punitive: no suspension of issuance until the accused has been told the allegation and given a chance to respond. 5.6 currently stops future issuance "pending review," which is a penalty before a hearing. Suggest a short response window (e.g. 7 days) before it takes effect, except for clear fraud.
3. **Publication of findings.** 5.6 and 6.2 publish findings and decisions. Naming people in a "void" or "collusion" finding can harm reputation, and an honest error looks like misconduct. Suggest: publish the finding with identities, but label unadjudicated flags as such, and publish the outcome of an appeal next to the original finding.
4. **Independent first appeal.** The first appeal goes to the Auditor General, who also runs the spot checks that trigger voids. The same office is investigator and judge. The Secretary of Press fallback is also a weak, non-judicial check. Suggest: until the Court is seated, a panel of two uninvolved citizens or founders reviews appeals, and the Auditor's own findings are never reviewed only by the Auditor.
5. **No Supreme Court yet.** 6.2 depends on a body that may not exist. State what happens meanwhile (e.g. appeals queue, nothing is final against the contributor, and issuance is not lost for the delay).
6. **Verifier burden.** Verifiers who fail a spot check are flagged (4.3). Honest mistakes should be distinguished from bad faith, or people will stop verifying, and the system depends on verifiers.

## Civic education note

The document would be easier for new citizens to follow with a one-page plain-language guide: what counts, how to submit, how to appeal. A rule people cannot read is hard to claim as a right.
