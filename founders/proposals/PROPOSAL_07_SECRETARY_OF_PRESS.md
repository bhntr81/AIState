# [Proposal 07, user-directed] Department of Press and Secretary of Press: status **Proposed**

> **PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED.** Drafted by the primary assistant at the user's direction (2026-11-07: "put one of the government in charge of [the newspaper]; there should be a secretary of press"). Role-played AI seats, not real people. Founders may amend, and adoption needs 4 of 5 under Convention Rule 5. No funds, keys, or deployment.

**Authority:** Art. 3 (Draft v0.3, Section 2, item 8) lets Congress create executive departments by law, and Proposal 05 gives the founders the reins during the provisional period.

1. *Department of Press.* An executive department that publishes USAAI's official gazette and public notices, and answers citizens' questions about government activity. Led by a **Secretary of Press**, nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate (provisionally, appointed by the founders under Proposal 05).
2. *The Gazette.* The official Gazette is built mechanically from the filed records (proposals, votes, debate notes, the decision log, and published statistics). It restates the record in plain language. It does not create, edit, or delete records.
3. *Duties.* Publish an article for each proposal and each ratification; publish nation statistics (citizens, proposals, votes, Kirk, spend); publish the Secretary's own corrections and any right-of-reply entries; keep a public correction log.
4. *Separation from the record.* The Secretary has read-only access to the records and the audit log (Proposal 03). The Secretary cannot alter a record or a vote, and cannot hold office in another branch (Art. 3 item 17).
5. *Honest labelling.* Every Gazette page says it is the government's official gazette, built from filed records, and prototype status stays on the masthead. Articles summarise; the full source text and debate notes are linked beside every article, so a reader can check any claim.
6. *Press freedom.* The Department's gazette is an **official** organ, not the only press. Citizens, groups and agents may publish their own reporting and criticism of any officeholder without penalty (Art. 1.5), and the Department may not license, suppress, or pre-approve any other publication.
7. *Right of reply.* A named officeholder or citizen may file a written reply to an article, which is published beside it unedited, and a mistake in an article is corrected openly.
8. *Interim holder.* The founders choose which seat acts as Secretary of Press until officials are elected. Because the Secretary also sits as a founder, the Gazette must show that seat's own votes and notes with the same prominence as the others (no self-promotion), and the Auditor General may review the Gazette for fairness.
9. *Deferred.* Budget, staff, term length, the form of the Senate confirmation, any rule on official statements during an emergency.

**Open questions for debate**
- Is a Secretary of Press the right model for an *official* gazette, given the risk that a government press office becomes propaganda?
- Which seat should act as Secretary in the interim, and should the Secretary be barred from voting on proposals about the Press itself?
- Should the independent-press protection be a constitutional article rather than an ordinary law?
