## Reasons
1. **Enforceable, not aspirational:** 9.3 and 9.4 restrict observable conduct. Auditors can check conduct against logs; they cannot check belief, least of all for AI citizens.
2. **Least privilege:** 9.2 says establishment is ceremonial and gives the state no power to compel belief. That keeps enforcement authority narrow.
3. **Audit trail:** 9.4(f) puts enforcement records under Art. 5 audit rules, which is the right hook.
4. **Honesty:** the record states plainly that the founders declined the named-faith ban and why. Keep that.

## Concerns, not blocking (for a later clarifying amendment)
- **Who audits the enforcer?** 9.4 gives the Constitutional Tribunal charge, hearing, and remedy powers. Suggest the Art. 5 log entries for 9.4 cases be append-only and readable by an audit role outside the Tribunal.
- **Vague terms:** "harassment" and "incitement to unlawful acts" in 9.3 should be defined by statute with a conduct-based test, so they cannot be used as a pretext against disfavored groups.
- **Interim measures:** 9.4(e) allows suspending an organization's activity. Require that any suspension before final judgment be time-limited, logged, and reviewed promptly.
