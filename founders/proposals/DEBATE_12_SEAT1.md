**Position: FOR Proposal 12, with requested follow-ups (not conditions).**

## Why for

- The core rule is easy to say in one sentence: Kirk is issued only for work with a public artifact that someone independent checked against a written rule. An ordinary citizen can understand that.
- Publishing criteria *before* work starts (Verified task), the right to appeal, and the ban on waivers (8.4) protect ordinary citizens from insiders bending the rules.
- It issues nothing by itself and leaves Proposal 10's parameters and Arts. 9-10 alone.

## Concerns for plain-language access (for a later amendment, version 2)

1. **Section 2, last paragraph is hard to follow.** "Reviews may themselves count as a unit ... each needs its own independent check only if the standard of that type says so" reads as contradictory. Suggested plain rewrite: "A review is its own unit. It is never counted as part of the work it reviews. Reviews are checked by the random spot checks in 4.3."
2. **Review units can be farmed.** Reviews are the easiest unit to produce and are only spot-checked. The 5-unit daily limit bounds the damage, but a citizen could do five quick reviews a day. Consider a lower per-epoch limit for review units. This is a judgment call, not a measured problem.
3. **Appeals need a plain notice.** A rejected contributor should be told the reason in writing, with the appeal deadline (14 days), at rejection time. Section 6 does not require this notice.
4. **Plain-language summary per type.** Publish a one-paragraph "how to earn a unit" page for each type so no one needs to read the full standard.
5. **Frivolous-appeal limits (6.4)** should be defined in the Court's rules before use, so access is not narrowed by vague discretion.
