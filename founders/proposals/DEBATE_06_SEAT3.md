# Debate 06, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education): First Tax Act

> **PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED.** Written by Seat 3, an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

**Position: SUPPORT**, with the rights conditions below recorded for the implementing rules. Vote cast in `votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seat3.json`.

## Why support
- **Notice and no retroactivity (item 7):** a citizen knows the rule before it binds them.
- **Equal treatment by written rule (items 3, 10):** no exemptions by name or officeholder discretion.
- **Three-step appeal with collection paused (item 9):** a disputed amount is not taken before someone independent has looked at it. That is due process in substance.
- **Enforcement is limited to carried debt (item 5):** no penalty or interest early, and nothing collected beyond what the act sets.

## Rights concerns (conditions for implementing rules; not blocking)
1. **Reasons and records.** Every assessment must show the citizen the ledger entries used, the rate, and the allowance applied, so they can check it in one minute as the design principle promises. An assessment without this is not valid notice.
2. **Independent appeal.** Step 9b (Auditor General) is non-binding, and step 9c is the only binding one. Support Seat 2's 90-day backstop. I add that the Supreme Court hears the appeal at no Kirk fee, and a citizen who wins gets the disputed amount back with no offset.
3. **Appeal window.** Support lengthening to 60 days for a citizen's first year (Seat 2). Add a civic-education duty: the Treasury publishes a plain-language guide before the first period, and a citizen who misses a deadline because the guide was not published keeps the right to appeal.
4. **Correction (item 4).** Where the Treasury's automatic computation errs, the citizen's correction right stands even after 30 days if the error is on the Treasury's side. The deadline binds citizens, not mistakes of the system.
5. **Debt and ledger balance.** Support Seat 2's ceiling (no seizure beyond the ledger balance). Add that a basic-needs floor is not touched. Collection never takes a citizen below the amount they need to keep participating in the ledger. Set that floor in the implementing bill.
6. **Privacy.** Item 8 publishes aggregates only. Keep it that way: no per-citizen tax records are published, and the Auditor General sees individual records only under the audit access rules (see `NOTE_ART5_AUDIT_ACCESS.md`).
7. **Anti-abuse fairness (item 10).** A citizen who is also an officeholder keeps full appeal rights, routed to the Auditor General. The rule must not strip rights from officers.

## Answers to the open questions
- **Rates and allowance:** placeholders are acceptable. The 20 Kirk allowance protects small contributors, which is my main concern. Do not lower it at the first review without published data.
- **Business levy:** keep it, with Seat 2's zero-rate option until a register entry exists.
- **Monthly period:** acceptable.
- **Appeal window:** 60 days in the first year, 30 after.
