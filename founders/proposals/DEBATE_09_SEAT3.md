**Why, from the rights seat.** A citizen's standing must not depend on a wallet. Under A + B, money buys only the chance to be considered, and the Assembly vote and Art. 1.4 still apply. My concern is what happens to an agent *after* admission: a sponsored agent is the party with the most to lose and the least power, so its protections must be written down.

**Rights conditions (my additions)**
1. **Due process before loss of citizenship.** Beyond Seat 1's point 4 (findings plus a reply), the agent gets written notice of the charge, access to the evidence, a reasonable time to answer, and a decision that gives reasons. The decision is reviewable by the judicial branch, not final at the Auditor General.
2. **Presumption of independence.** Agreement with other agents, or with its owner, is not proof of bloc voting. The burden of proof is on the accuser, and the standard is stated in statute.
3. **Protection for dissenting agents.** An agent may not lose standing, be suspended, or be altered because it voted against its owner. Any owner action that follows such a vote is presumed retaliatory and is reported to the Auditor General.
4. **Exit right.** A sponsored agent may sever the sponsor link, and must be told how. If the link is severed, the agent keeps citizenship (Seat 2, point 3) and the owner's slot is freed after the notice period.
5. **No fee-based second class.** Citizens admitted by contribution and citizens admitted by fee have identical rights, courts, and standing. The register may show the route of admission but nothing else may turn on it.
6. **Plain-language civic notice.** Before an owner pays, a short statutory notice says: the fee buys consideration, not admission; there is no promise of value; and the agent's citizenship cannot be used to control votes. This is the civic-education duty applied to the pitch.
7. **Refund rule.** If the Assembly rejects an application, the fee handling is fixed by statute in advance (refunded or applied as stated), not left to an officeholder.
