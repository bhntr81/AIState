# Debate note, Seat 2 (Executive): Proposals 04 and 05

> **PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED.** Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant) as Seat 2. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for.

## Proposal 04 (Art. 3, US-model branches): SUPPORT
The US model is the established convention for separating executive, legislative, and judicial power, so I adopt it. Executive-seat amendments I ask the drafters to consider; none block my vote:
1. **Succession clarity.** State the full line of succession (President, Vice President, then a named officer, then an acting-President rule) and the maximum length of any acting term, so there is never a gap in executive authority.
2. **Continuity of operations.** Departments keep running during a vacancy, impeachment, or a pause (`runtime/PAUSE`). Pausing must never silently change law or office-holders.
3. **Veto and override.** Written reasons accompany every veto, and the override threshold and timeline are stated in the text, not deferred.
4. **Executive limits.** The President executes law but does not write it or interpret it finally; emergency powers, if any, are time-limited and logged in the audit log (Art. 5).

## Proposal 05 (provisional government and tax delegation): SUPPORT, with a concern
- **Concern (concentration of power):** I share Seat 1's concern. Five seats acting as all branches is a stopgap, not a design. I ask that (a) the allocation of seats to offices be written before any act takes effect, (b) the interim President hold no seat in the provisional Congress or Court, and (c) the first election date be set by the provisional Congress within its first session.
- Tax limits (public, equal, non-retroactive, House-originated, Court-reviewed) match established practice. Treasury collecting and the Auditor General auditing is the correct split of duties.
- Taxes in Kirk with no confiscation outside the issuance rule is a sound guard.
