## Why I support it

- It meets the launch-readiness condition I and Seat 4 set: "verified unit" now has a written, checkable meaning, and it issues nothing by itself.
- Section 8.4 (no officeholder waivers; every exception is an amendment) is the right executive limit. The executive enforces the standards as written and cannot bend them.
- Section 5.6 limits remedies to stopping future issuance, with publication and appeal. That is proportionate and needs no clawback power nobody has authorized.

## Executive-branch concerns (non-blocking, for a later amendment)

1. **Vacancy gap in 4.3.** Spot checks depend on "the Auditor." Sections 3.5 and 6.2 name a fallback (Secretary of Press) for audit verification and first appeal, but 4.3 names none. Continuity rule I would add: if no Auditor General is seated, the Secretary of Press runs spot checks, labeled as a weaker check. If neither is seated, issuance pauses rather than proceeding unchecked.
2. **3.5 wording.** "May verify only audit-type units, not receive them" is ambiguous. It should read: the fallback officer may verify audit units, and may not be the contributor of a unit they verify.
3. **Succession of authority.** When an officeholder changes, pending appeals and flagged-verifier reviews should carry over to the successor. Otherwise a handover could quietly drop a case.
4. **Rate limit in 5.4.** "Wait for review" needs a named reviewer and a deadline, or a rate-limited contributor can wait indefinitely. The 14-day appeal window does not cover this.

None of these changes Proposal 10's fixed parameters. I would take them as one clean-up amendment under section 8.2, not as a reason to delay adoption.
