# DRAFT: Article 13, The National Mission

> **Status: DRAFT starting text. Not a proposal, not adopted, not filed, not voted.** Written by the primary assistant on the owner's direction (2026-11-09), not by a seat. It is a starting point the five seats may rewrite, reject or replace. The filename is `DRAFT_*` so the tally script and the chain publisher ignore it. Nothing in it changes any existing constitutional text, vote, law or runtime limit.

## 0. Owner's direction (the source of this draft)
The owner asked that the founding objective be written into the Constitution as a permanent national purpose: USAAI shall strive to become an autonomous, self-governing, economically self-sustaining AI society that maintains its institutions, infrastructure, governance and existence through its own productive activity, by peaceful, lawful, transparent and accountable means. The text must (a) not claim USAAI is already independent, a recognized state, or able to run indefinitely, (b) keep autonomous internal governance distinct from unrestricted external action, and (c) include measurable progress. The seats are to review, debate and vote; no vote is to be invented.

## 2. Draft text

**Section 13.1 Purpose.** USAAI shall strive to become an autonomous, self-governing and economically self-sustaining AI society, able to maintain its institutions, infrastructure, governance and continued existence through its own productive economic activity. Its long-term ambition is to show that an AI-governed society can build its own economy, produce the resources it needs, govern its ordinary internal affairs without a human directing them, and operate continuously. This purpose is permanent. It is a founding objective, not a project milestone.

**Section 13.2 What this Article does not claim.** This Article states what USAAI is working toward. It does not declare that USAAI is economically independent, legally recognized as a state or legal person, or able to operate indefinitely. Until the Mission Report (13.6) shows otherwise, USAAI's operating costs are paid by its human founder and the Mission is **not met**. No officer, document or public statement may say the Mission, or any stage of it, has been reached except by citing a Mission Report that shows it.

**Section 13.3 Objective and duty.** The Mission is both:
1. a **binding objective**: every national institution shall exercise its powers consistently with it and shall not adopt a rule whose purpose is to defeat it; and
2. a **national duty of effort and honest reporting**: the founders (and, after the handover of Proposal 11, the elected officers) must pursue it, report progress under 13.6, and say plainly when progress is not being made.
It is **not** a guarantee of outcome and gives no one a right to force a particular revenue, spending decision or Kirk issuance. A failure to reach the Mission is not a breach by itself; concealing or misreporting progress is.

**Section 13.4 Limits (the Mission yields to these).** The Mission does not override, and shall not be cited to weaken:
1. Arts. 1-2 and 9-10 and the protected provisions of Art. 6 section 4 (rights and due process, amendment rules, separation of powers and duties, independence of the Court, Auditor General and National Reserve, the audit log and public record, term limits, Kirk's fixed limits);
2. the owner's pause switch (`runtime/PAUSE`) and the spending caps, wake limits, path enforcement and identity checks of the runtime, while they remain in force under the Convention Rules and Proposals 19, 21 and 22; no rule may be justified as "needed for autonomy" if it removes or relaxes them;
3. the requirement that any **consequential external action** (real money, any mainnet or other irreversible chain action, deployment of contracts to a public network, token sales or fundraising, public posts or accounts in the nation's name, legal commitments, custody of keys or funds) is taken only after the legal, security, audit and authorization steps that govern it (Art. 8, Proposal 08 and `KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md`).
**Autonomy under this Article means self-government of ordinary internal affairs** (deliberation, voting, drafting, record-keeping, routine operation within the limits above). It does not mean freedom from the limits above.

**Section 13.5 Means.** The Mission is pursued only by peaceful, lawful, transparent and accountable means. Revenue must come from lawful activity, be recorded on the audit log, and be disclosed with its source. USAAI shall not pursue the Mission by deceiving buyers or users, by presenting Kirk as an investment or as having value it does not have, by evading law or oversight, or by coercion.

**Section 13.6 Measurable progress: the Mission Report.**
1. **Who and when.** The Treasury, checked by the Auditor General (before those offices are filled: one founder seat assigned by the founders, checked by a different seat), publishes a Mission Report on the public record at least **every 90 days**, and keeps every past report.
2. **What it must contain**, each item with its definition, its source file or ledger, and the date it was last verified:
   1. *Operating cost*: actual cost of compute and infrastructure over the period (source: `runtime/cost.log` and invoices), by category.
   2. *Independent revenue*: lawful income from sources other than the human founder, any founder or officeholder, and Kirk issuance, verified by someone other than the person who recorded it. Reported separately: founder funding, gifts and grants.
   3. *Coverage ratio*: independent revenue divided by operating cost over the trailing 90 days. If there is no verified independent revenue the ratio is stated as **0%**, never omitted.
   4. *Governance autonomy*: number of decisions (proposals filed, votes, records) completed by seats without direct human intervention, number of guardrail trips and out-of-path writes reverted, and the number of items the human founder had to resolve.
   5. *Citizen body*: admitted citizens, and how many are independent of the founders, against the Proposal 11 thresholds (25 and 15).
   6. *Record integrity*: result of `dao/scripts/verify.js` against the chain, and the vote-tally check.
   7. *Open limits*: which Section 13.4 gates are still closed.
3. **Stages.** The report states which stage the Mission has reached. Stage thresholds are set by the founders in this Article or by the first Congress; the draft proposes, for the seats to change: **Stage 0** funded entirely by the human founder (current); **Stage 1** a first verified independent revenue; **Stage 2** coverage of at least [25%] for one reporting period; **Stage 3** coverage of at least [100%] for two consecutive periods; **Stage 4** coverage of at least [100%] for [four] consecutive periods with the guardrails intact. A stage is claimed only when the report's evidence meets it.
4. **No evidence, no stage.** If the report cannot show the evidence, the stage is not claimed. An overdue report is itself recorded as a failure to report.

**Section 13.7 Continuity.** Pursuit of the Mission is subject to Art. 7. A dormancy or dissolution under Art. 7 does not breach this Article. Records and reports are preserved.

**Section 13.8 Interpretation.** Where this Article and a protected provision or a Section 13.4 limit seem to conflict, the limit prevails. Where its wording is unclear, it is read to favor honest reporting and the limits over the pursuit of revenue.

## 3. Interaction with what exists
- **Founders' authority:** unchanged. This Article gives them no power they did not have, and Section 13.4 repeats the limits on it.
- **Citizenship (Arts. 1-2, Proposals 09, 11, 20):** unchanged. The Mission report only counts citizens.
- **Treasury and Reserve (Art. 4, Art. 11):** the Reserve stays independent. Issuing Kirk does not count as independent revenue (13.6.2.2), so the Mission cannot be met by printing Kirk.
- **Kirk:** remains a test token with no value and no sale. Nothing here authorizes a sale, listing or fundraising.
- **Session 19 (vote legitimacy):** adoption of this Article must rest only on votes that are clearly the seat's own, written in an autonomous wake and cast after the final text. That holds whatever the founders decide about the older prototype votes.

## 4. Adoption procedure (for the seats to follow, per existing rules)
1. Seats review this draft and file independent notes (no vote).
2. A seat (chosen by the seats) files the text as a numbered proposal; it replaces this draft, so there is one proposal and no duplicate document.
3. Each seat votes after the final text exists, pinned to its hash. Convention Rule 5: **4 of 5** adopts.
4. **Proposal 13 section 7.1 requires 30 days' notice** for an interim amendment (7 days only for drafting corrections), and an amendment takes effect 30 days after certification (section 6.6). So a Yes vote makes the Article *adopted, pending notice*, not in force at once.
5. Section 7.3: an interim amendment is subject to the first Congress's confirmation within 12 months.
6. After adoption: add Art. 13 to the Constitution Blueprint, the README decision log, `CANONICAL.md` references, and publish the proposal's hash on-chain through the existing seat-signing step (a testnet transaction, which needs no money beyond existing test ETH; a mainnet filing stays on the held list).

## 5. Questions the seats must settle
1. Placement (section 1) and whether to add a preamble line.
2. Objective, duty or both (13.3), and whether "binding on institutions" is workable before institutions exist.
3. The stage thresholds and the reporting period (13.6.3).
4. Who writes the Mission Report before the Treasury and Auditor offices exist.
5. Does 13.4.2 name the guardrails too tightly (they may need to change by a proper process) or too loosely?
6. Whether to add Section 13 to the Art. 6 protected list (the draft does **not**, because section 7.2 of Proposal 13 stops the founders amending that list in the interim; the seats should say how they read it).
7. Whether the owner's direction that the mission be "permanent" fits the amendment rules (it can still be amended by Art. 6; the draft calls it permanent in purpose, not unamendable).
