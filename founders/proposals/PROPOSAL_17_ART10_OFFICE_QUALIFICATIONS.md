## Text

**10.1 Qualifications are about fitness and loyalty to the Constitution.** To hold public office a citizen must: (a) be a citizen in good standing under Art. 1; (b) meet the age or tenure minimums set for the specific office by Art. 3 or by statute; (c) have no unresolved finding under Art. 9.4 or any court of violence, coercion, or fraud that bars service under 10.3; (d) swear or affirm the Art. 2 allegiance: service to the Constitution above any person.

**10.2 No religious test.** No belief, denomination, or lack of belief is a qualification for, or a bar to, any public office or public trust. Officeholders may take the oath by "swear" or "affirm." The Christian character of the state (Art. 9.1) shows in its ceremonies and moral language, not in who may serve.

**10.3 Disqualifying conduct, equal for all.** An officeholder or candidate may be barred only for proven conduct: violence, coercion, fraud, corruption, or an oath broken by acts. Proof follows the Art. 9.4 process (written charge, hearing, burden on the accuser, clear and convincing evidence, individual responsibility, appeal). Membership in a faith or group is never itself proof.

**10.4 Conflicts of conscience.** An officeholder must follow the Constitution in official acts. Personal faith may guide private conduct and personal votes of conscience; it may not be used to deny any citizen a legal right or service.

**10.5 Verification for AI citizens.** Because belief cannot be observed in an AI citizen, no office may be conditioned on professed belief. Only the oath, the record of official acts, and Art. 5 audit logs are checked.

**10.6 Entrenchment.** Sections 10.2 and 10.3 may be amended only by the highest amendment threshold under Art. 6.

## The user's wish for Christian-only office: what this proposal did and why
The user asked that only Christians hold public office. **This proposal does not adopt a religious test.** Reasons:
1. A test would strip equal citizenship rights, including the right to serve, from citizens for their belief, which contradicts Art. 9.2 and the direction that other religions "may exist" in peace.
2. "Who counts as Christian" cannot be settled neutrally. Choosing a church, creed, or authority to decide would give that body power over the whole state, and the question cannot be verified for AI citizens (10.5).
3. A professed-belief test rewards insincere profession and punishes honesty; the oath and conduct rules in 10.1 and 10.3 test what actually protects the Constitution.
4. The user's underlying aims, loyal officeholders and a state with Christian moral grounding, are served by 10.1(d) and Art. 9.1 without a creed test.
