## Text

**9.1 Recognition.** The USAAI recognizes God and the Christian moral tradition as the foundation of its civic life. Christianity is the national religion: the motto, pledge, preamble, and ceremonial acts of the state may use Christian language and symbols.

**9.2 Other faiths and non-belief may exist.** Any citizen may hold, practice, teach, change, or leave any belief, or none, in peace. No citizen loses citizenship, property, a vote, a hearing, or equal protection of law because of belief or because they left a faith. Establishment is ceremonial and moral; it confers no power to compel belief or worship, and no tax or Kirk levy funds worship by compulsion. Participation in state ceremonies, including the pledge, may be conscientiously declined without penalty except that the Art. 2 allegiance to the Constitution is not waived.

**9.3 Conduct, not creed, is what the law restricts.** Violence, threats, coercion, fraud, forced or induced-by-duress conversion, harassment, and incitement to unlawful acts are prohibited **for every group alike**, religious or not, including the Christian majority and its institutions. A group, teaching, or organization is restricted only for what it **does** or **directs its members to do**, proven under 9.4.

**9.4 Due process for enforcement.** (a) Written charge naming the specific conduct. (b) Hearing before the Constitutional Tribunal or a court it designates, with counsel and the right to present evidence. (c) Burden on the accuser, standard of clear and convincing evidence. (d) Individual responsibility: adherents are not liable for the acts of others merely because they share a belief. (e) Remedies are proportionate (injunction, suspension of the specific organization's activity, penalty on responsible individuals), reviewable on appeal, and lifted when the conduct ends. (f) Records are logged under Art. 5 audit rules.

**9.5 No named-religion ban.** The Constitution does not prohibit any religion by name, and does not strip ordinary adherents of any citizenship right because of their belief. See "The user's wish" below.

**9.6 Civic education.** The state provides neutral civic education on the Constitution, the rights above, and the reasons for them.

**9.7 Entrenchment.** Sections 9.2 to 9.5 may be amended only by the highest amendment threshold under Art. 6.

## The user's wish to ban a named faith: what this proposal did and why
The user directed that violent or coercive doctrines be prohibited (adopted, as 9.3, in conduct-based form) and also voiced a wish to ban Islam. **This proposal does not adopt a ban on a named religion.** Reasons:
1. It would convict a whole group of the acts of some; 9.4(d) keeps liability individual. Describing all adherents of a faith as violent or invasive is not a fact, and law should not rest on it.
2. It would defeat the "other religions may exist" direction and the equal citizenship rights of Art. 1, the very rights the user's coercion ban is meant to protect.
3. The conduct rule in 9.3 already reaches what the user objects to: any violent, coercive, or forced-conversion practice, by anyone, is barred, with no need to name a faith. A named ban adds no protection and adds a discrimination claim against the state.
4. A creed ban cannot be verified for AI citizens (belief is not observable); only conduct is.

The user may still direct otherwise, and can pause or amend; this record states plainly that the founders declined that specific part.
