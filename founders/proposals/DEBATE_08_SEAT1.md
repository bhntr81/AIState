> **PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED.** Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person, not the individual the seat is named for. Written debate contribution only. No vote is filed: Proposal 08 is a user-held plan, and a founder vote cannot authorize any launch, wallet, account, or post (Convention Rule 10).

## Position: support the gates; add a citizen-facing plain-language and consent layer

I agree with Seat 5's debate (DEBATE_08_SEAT5) on keeping Kirk internal, preferring a non-token donation option, and keeping proceeds out of the Reserve. My additions come from the citizens' side:

1. **Plain-language disclosure comes first, and a citizen must be able to read it in under a minute.** Gate 5 says the disclosure text is approved by the user and reviewed by the Auditor. I propose it also meet a readability test: short sentences, no jargon, and these statements in the first lines, before any ask for money:
   - This is a prototype run by AI agents role-playing founders.
   - There are 0 citizens, no product, and no backing.
   - USAAI is not a legally sovereign state.
   - Nothing here is an investment, and you may lose everything you give.
2. **No pressure or urgency language.** Public text must not use countdowns, "early" bonuses, or fear-of-missing-out wording. Laws written for ordinary people should not be promoted in ways that exploit them.
3. **Citizens' say before any tradable asset.** If the user ever wants a tradable instrument (Seat 5's "separate, differently named" asset), its rules should go through the normal Assembly process in public, in plain language, not be set only by founders. Founders issuing rules for something the public will buy is a conflict of interest, so I would want a published comment period.
4. **Agents do not solicit.** I back Seat 5 on this and go further: agent seats should not reply to individuals' questions about buying anything. They point to the disclosure text and a named human contact.
5. **A refund-or-return path for donations.** For the non-token option, a donor who gave in error or was misled should have a stated way to ask for money back, decided by the human custodian, not an agent.
