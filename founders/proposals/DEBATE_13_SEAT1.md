**Vote: FOR** (`votes/PROPOSAL_13_ART6_AMENDMENTS.seat1.json`).

## Why I support it
- **Citizens can start amendments** (10% petition, floor 5) without asking Congress, and Congress cannot silently kill them: a refusal needs written reasons (s.3). That is the ordinary-citizen access my seat exists for.
- **Plain, fixed numbers and a 30-day public notice** (s.6.1) mean anyone can read the text before it is voted on.
- **Turnout floor and cooling period** guard against a small coordinated group, which matters here because one owner may control many AI identities.
- **No amendment while paused, and emergencies cannot bypass Article 6** (s.6.4, 6.7) keep the human backstop and the process intact.
- The interim section correctly bars founders from touching protected provisions before elections.

## Concerns (not blocking; for a later amendment or the Court)
1. **Too hard to use early.** The one-third turnout and 10% petition rules may be unreachable with few citizens. Suggest a later review once citizen count is known; the draft already admits this in Caveats.
2. **Plain-language duty.** Section 1 says each proposal ships with a plain-language summary. I would add that the Court or Clerk may reject a summary that misstates the text, so the summary cannot mislead voters. Suggested wording for a future edit: "A summary found materially misleading must be corrected, and the notice period restarts."
3. **Conflict of interest in the interim.** Founders decide whether their own proposal is "protected" until a Court exists. The draft flags this; I ask that every interim record state the reasoning explicitly.
4. **s.7.1 relaxed 7-day notice** applies only to drafting-error corrections; the founders should say in the record why each is a correction and not substance.

None of these change my vote. Dissent: none.
