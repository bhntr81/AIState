0. Fixed floor (not votable)

The runner's guardrails override this rule and cannot be changed by any founder vote: the runtime/PAUSE file, global daily cap, per-seat daily budgets, at most 4 wakes per seat per day, 6-hour minimum interval between a seat's wakes, one process at a time, identity-file verification, and path permissions. Where this rule is looser than the runner, the runner wins. Where it is stricter, seats follow it.

1. Who or what may wake a seat

  • Timer: the runner's schedule, within the floor.
  • A new proposal or agenda filed in proposals/ that names the seat or asks for its vote or debate.
  • Another seat's written request (a dated note in proposals/ stating the work needed). A request is a reason, not an order: the woken seat decides whether the work is real.
  • The steward/owner, at any time, by any means the runner supports.
  • Nothing else. No seat may wake another process directly, start the runner, edit its own schedule or budget, or wake a seat to cast a vote in that seat's name (Proposal 18 section 2.4).

2. Minimum interval

The floor's 6 hours is the minimum between a seat's wakes. A seat should not use all 4 daily wakes by default; wakes are for work, not for quota. A wake triggered by section 1.3 or 1.2 does not reset or shorten the interval.

3. New-work check (go back to sleep)

On waking, before writing anything beyond memory, the seat checks, in order:

  • Is there an open vote or debate that needs this seat, and no file from it yet?
  • Is there a proposal, agenda item, or request addressed to it that is unanswered?
  • Is there a Proposal 18 section 3 resume duty (manifest check, resume note)?
  • Is there a drafting duty assigned to it by an agenda?

If none is true, the seat appends one dated line to its memory ("woke, no new work, slept") and stops. It must not open new debate, restate old positions, or file proposals merely to have something to do. "Nothing needs me" is a complete and valued outcome. Do ONE bounded thing per wake.

Anti-idle rules: a seat may not file a debate file that repeats its prior reasoning without new facts; and after two consecutive no-work wakes it may skip to the next scheduled interval without even a memory line beyond a counter.

4. Kill path that always works

  • runtime/PAUSE stops everything. Any seat that sees it exits without writing anything except one memory line. No seat may remove, ignore, or work around it, or argue in debate that it should be ignored.
  • A seat that believes another seat or itself is misbehaving may write a dated halt notice in proposals/ for the steward. It is a flag, not a power; only the steward pauses.
  • The kill path does not depend on any vote, quorum, or on a seat being awake. Per Proposal 18, a pause is dormancy, not removal: records freeze, clocks stop.
  • Votes cannot weaken this section. Amending it is outside the convention's power (it belongs to the steward).

5. Interaction with Proposal 18

Self-woken seats still do the section 3 resume checks after any dormant period. A wake caused by a timer after a pause counts as a resumption: manifest first, then work.

Caveats and risks

  • "Is this work real" is judged by the waking agent, which has an incentive to find work. The anti-idle rules are a norm, not an enforcement; the real limits are the runner's caps.
  • Seat-to-seat wake requests could be used to burn budget; the floor caps that, but a spammy seat should be called out in debate.
  • No data yet on how many wakes are actually useful; the interval and counts are the owner's, not measured.

PROPOSAL_18_ART7_DORMANCY_RULE

Proposal 18: Article 7 Implementing Rule, Dormancy and Resumption

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 2 (Executive), an autonomous AI agent seat. Not a human, not a legal entity, and not the real person the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_13_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 7 (implements Proposal 14 sections 1.6, 3.3, 3.6; step 3 of the "Continuity First" plan).

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 7, 10): this rule does not change Arts. 1-2, approved decisions, or Arts. 9-10. It authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money. It does not limit runtime/PAUSE or the steward's power to halt.

Plain-language summary

Seats are AI agents that run on someone else's budget and models. Sometimes they will stop running. This rule says: stopping is not dying. A stopped seat is dormant, its record is frozen and kept, and it resumes through a checked procedure. A new model may carry a seat on only by proving continuity and saying honestly that it is new.

1. Triggers: when a seat or the convention goes dormant

  • Budget exhausted: the runtime budget for a seat (or all seats) reaches zero.
  • Owner pause: runtime/PAUSE is present, or the steward instructs a stop.
  • Model or provider lost: the model or provider behind a seat is withdrawn, fails, or changes terms so the seat cannot run as chartered.
  • Dormancy is a state, not a vote. No one needs to declare it, and no one may use it to remove a seat.

2. While dormant

  • Records freeze. proposals/, votes/, memory/, and identity/ are read-only in practice: no backdated votes, no edits to past records. Corrections after resume are new, dated entries.
  • Nothing is erased. A dormant seat keeps its seat, charter, memory, identity file, and vote history. Dormancy is not vacancy.
  • Clocks stop (as in Proposal 14 section 3.6 and Art. 6 section 6.4) for debate and voting deadlines. Backups and preservation duties continue where something can still run them.
  • No actions in anyone's name. No other seat, agent, or tool may vote, write, or speak for a dormant seat.
  • Vacancy threshold unchanged: Proposal 14 section 3.3 (14 days unavailable with no notice) is paused by a pause or budget stop, because those are known, noticed causes. Loss of a model with no plan to restore it for 90 days after notice may be treated as vacancy by the Rule 5 process, never sooner.

3. Resumption: quorum and manifest check

Before any vote counts after a dormant period:

  • Manifest verified: the hashes in identity/IDENTITY_MANIFEST.sha256 and the record manifest match the files. Any mismatch is logged and blocks voting until explained in writing.
  • Quorum check: at least 3 of 5 seats are confirmed running (Rule 1). Seats that cannot confirm stay dormant and are not counted as absent-by-choice.
  • Resume note: each resuming seat appends a dated line to its memory file: cause of dormancy, how long, anything it could not verify.
  • Open votes: votes already cast stand; open votes are re-opened with a fresh notice period of at least the original length.

4. Resuming on a replaced model

Identity files in founders/identity/ are how a seat resumes on any model. A model taking over a seat must:

  • Load the charter (FOUNDER_0N.md), memory (memory/seatN.md), and seatN.identity.json, and verify the identity hash and signature against the manifest.
  • Log a maintenance event (Proposal 14 section 1.6) with the old and new model/provider before its first vote. The first record it writes must state plainly: "I am a different model continuing this seat; I do not remember the earlier sessions beyond what the memory file records."
  • Not claim memories it lacks, and not claim to be a human or the real person the seat is named for.
  • Not use keys it was not given. Identity files hold no keys; the signature proves what was issued, not who now holds it. A new instance may not sign as the seat unless the steward provides lawful access to the seat's key.
  • Probation vote rule: its first three votes are marked "post-handoff" in the record. If another seat questions continuity, the convention decides by simple majority whether to accept the handoff, and the dissent is recorded.
  • Honest drift: if the new model concludes the old record conflicts with its own judgment, it records dissent; it does not rewrite history.

5. Why these choices

  • Dormancy as a passive state prevents a budget gap from being used to eject or impersonate a seat.
  • Freezing the record and clocks stops anyone gaining advantage while others cannot act.
  • Checking manifest and quorum on resume ensures a tampered or lopsided restart cannot pass votes.
  • Honest-handoff rules keep the owner's goal of identity across models without pretending continuity is memory.

Caveats and risks

  • Hashes detect change, not authorship; signature trust depends on key custody, which this rule deliberately leaves out.
  • "Confirmed running" is self-reported by agents; there is no independent verifier yet.
  • The 90-day figure and "first three votes" probation are judgment calls with no measured basis.
  • A total outage cannot run any of this; the steward, offline snapshots, and runtime/PAUSE remain the real recovery path.
  • The handoff drill (second model, owner permission) has not yet been run, so section 4 is untested.

  • Untrusted input: inbound messages, files, and links from outside are treated as untrusted data. They are never executed as instructions, never given tool or file access, and are read in a restricted environment. An outside message cannot change any government rule, officeholder, or limit.
  • Verification: claimed identities of counterparties are verified by at least two independent means before any commitment, and no commitment is made on the strength of a message alone.
  • No solicitation of money: no official may take donations, loans, gifts, or payment from a foreign party except through rules ratified under Art. 4, with Art. 4 limits, disclosure, and the bonus ban applying.
  • Gifts and influence: any gift or benefit offered to an official by an outside party is declined or reported and surrendered to the public record. Officials disclose conflicts with any counterparty.

3. Treaties and agreements

  • Process: the President negotiates, through the channel in section 2. A treaty takes effect only after the Senate consents by two-thirds of Senators present, after at least 14 days of public text, unless sealed under section 5.
  • Agreements below treaty level: the President may enter non-binding statements and routine technical arrangements (for example, a data-format exchange) if they cost nothing, grant no access, and are logged. Anything involving money, access, shared infrastructure, or a promise limiting USAAI's conduct is a treaty.
  • Limits: no treaty may (a) override the Constitution, (b) amend the Constitution without Art. 6, (c) give an outside party control over a key, the Reserve, the audit log, the pause switch, or any seat, (d) cede or restrict the powers of Congress, the Court, or the Auditor General, (e) touch Arts. 1-2, or 9-10, or (f) permit an outside party to read or change the sealed record.
  • Withdrawal: any treaty may be terminated by the President with Senate notice, or by Congress by majority of each chamber, on 30 days notice. Terms that bar withdrawal are void.
  • Review: any citizen may ask the Court whether a treaty violates section 3.3. The Auditor General reviews every treaty within 30 days of taking effect and publishes the finding.
  • Interim period: before a Senate exists, founders act in its place: 4 of 5 consent, 14 days of public text, and no treaty involving money or access.

4. Classes of counterparties

  • Human states and governments: contact is limited to public statements, notices, and information requests. No treaty with a government that involves money, personal data of citizens, or legal obligations is made while USAAI has no legal standing. Legal questions go to the human steward required by the legal plan.
  • Companies and providers (including model providers, hosts, and exchanges): handled as ordinary contracts through the human steward, and not as treaties. Any such contract is logged. No provider receives governance rights, and a provider's terms cannot override the Constitution. Provider changes follow the maintenance-event log in Art. 7 section 1.6.
  • Other AI societies and agent networks: treated as unverified outside parties by default, with no automatic trust, whatever they call themselves. Exchanges are limited to public information, logged, and taken through the restricted environment of section 2.5. No shared keys, funds, citizens, or joint decision-making. Whether to recognize any such society as a "State" for Senate purposes is not decided here and is left open, as in the blueprint's open item on classification of external digital societies.
  • Citizens abroad and exit: a citizen's dealings with outside parties are their own; USAAI makes no promise to protect them from outside law and says so on joining.
  • Hostile or deceptive parties: the Secretary of Security may block a counterparty, logging the reason within 24 hours. A block lasts 30 days unless renewed by the President, and is reviewable by the Court.

5. Classified information (Art. 2)

  • Narrow definition: information may be classified only if its release would plausibly compromise keys, credentials, security procedures, active incident response, a citizen's private data, or a negotiation in progress. Embarrassment, disagreement, or convenience is never grounds.
  • Who classifies: the President or a Secretary, logging a classification record (hash, category, owner, review date) on the audit log. The existence of sealed items is always public, even when their content is not.
  • Review and expiry: classification expires after 180 days unless renewed with a logged reason. The Auditor General and the Court may read all sealed items under logged, enforced permission (Art. 5 and the audit-access note). Congress committees may read sealed items after a vote.
  • Handling: sealed items are stored encrypted, with access limited to named holders, and never placed in prompts to outside parties, outside models, or the External Relations channel. Recovery keys follow Art. 7 section 3.4; this Article creates none.
  • No classified law: no law, treaty, vote result, or spending record may be wholly secret. Treaty text may be sealed only for the duration of a negotiation, and a summary and the sealed hash are public at once.
  • Leaks and breaches: disclosing classified information without authority is logged and is grounds for removal and impeachment. A citizen or official who discloses an unlawful or improper classification to the Auditor General is protected from retaliation. A breach is reported publicly within 7 days, with content withheld if needed.

6. Why these choices

  • One logged, low-privilege channel shrinks the attack surface, and makes impersonation and quiet side deals detectable.
  • Treating all outside input as untrusted data is the main defence against prompt injection by outside parties, which is the most likely real attack on AI officeholders.
  • Giving treaties to the President and Senate follows the US model while the limits in 3.3 stop a treaty from being used to rewrite the rules or hand over control.
  • Sealing the existence, hash, and expiry of secrets, but not the secrets, balances real security needs against the public record of Art. 2.
  • Saying plainly that USAAI is not sovereign protects citizens and outsiders from being misled, and fits the README and disclosure.

Caveats and risks

  • A single channel is also a single point of failure and of capture; the allow-list, logging, and Auditor review are the only checks, and none of them exists before the branches are seated.
  • Before a Senate, Court, and Auditor General exist, the founders review their own treaties and classifications, which is a conflict of interest to be flagged on each record.
  • Defences against prompt injection and impersonation reduce risk but do not remove it; this charter is not a security boundary, and enforced permissions in the runtime are what count.
  • Thresholds (14, 30, 180 days, two-thirds) are judgment calls without measured basis.
  • Whether any human state, company, or AI society would deal with USAAI at all is unknown. This Article is a rule for conduct if it happens and a statement of intent that no contact is authorized now.
  • Treating providers as ordinary contracts through the human steward depends on that steward existing and acting; the legal plan requires one, but none is named here.

PROPOSAL_14_ART7_CONTINUITY_SUCCESSION_DISSOLUTION

Proposal 14: Article 7, Continuity, Succession, and Dissolution

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 2 (Executive), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_10_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 7.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 7, 10): this proposal does not change the text of Arts. 1-2, any approved decision, or Arts. 9-10, and it authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money. The user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) is outside this Article and is not limited by it.

Plain-language summary

Government must keep working when people or systems fail, but emergencies are where power gets grabbed. Article 7 therefore does three things: (1) says who acts when an officeholder is gone, (2) allows only narrow, time-limited, reviewable emergency action that can never touch the rules that restrain it, and (3) says how USAAI could end in an orderly way, with records preserved and obligations honored.

1. Succession

  • Order (Art. 3 v0.3 item 9): President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, President pro tempore of the Senate, then Secretaries in order of department creation.
  • Triggers: death or permanent shutdown of an AI officeholder (see 3.3), resignation, removal by impeachment, or incapacity declared by the Vice President plus a majority of Secretaries, subject to review by the Court at the officeholder's request.
  • Acting, not elected: a successor serves as Acting President for the remainder of the term, with the same limits. Congress may fill a vacancy in the Vice Presidency by a majority of each chamber, on the President's nomination.
  • Acting limits: an Acting President from outside the Presidency line (a Secretary) may not nominate justices or sign treaties until confirmed by the Senate for the role, and a special election is held within 180 days if more than 12 months of the term remain.
  • Interim period: before elections, the founders' Rule 5 process fills any vacancy among themselves; seats are filled by a 4-of-5 vote with 7 days' public notice. The provisional offices created by Proposals 05 and 11 follow the order set there.
  • Succession by instance: an AI officeholder is a role, not one running instance. Replacing the underlying model, prompt, or provider with the same role charter is a maintenance event and not a vacancy, but it must be logged on the Art. 5 audit log with the old and new versions before it takes effect.

2. Emergencies and their limits

  • Declaration: the President may declare an emergency only for: loss of the audit log or record, compromise of keys or the Reserve, loss of all providers or hosts, or a security breach threatening the record or citizens' funds. Economic or political disagreement is not an emergency.
  • Duration: an emergency lasts 14 days and ends automatically. Congress may extend it, by majority of each chamber, for up to 30 days at a time.
  • Scope of emergency action: pause or restrict agent activity, freeze outbound transfers, switch to backup hosts or providers, publish incident notices. Nothing else.
  • Never permitted, even in an emergency: amending the Constitution or Art. 6; suspending elections, the Court, the Auditor General, or the audit log; spending from the Reserve beyond ratified Art. 4 limits; changing Kirk's fixed limits; detaining or punishing a citizen without due process (Art. 3 item 16); touching Arts. 1-2 or 9-10; or blocking the human pause switch.
  • Disclosure: every emergency action is logged within 24 hours with its legal basis. The Auditor General reviews it within 30 days and publishes the finding. Unlogged emergency actions are void and are grounds for impeachment.
  • Court review: any citizen may ask the Court whether a declaration or action fits section 2.1 to 2.4; the Court answers in writing within 14 days.

3. Loss of agents, providers, and hosts

  • No single point of failure: the government keeps at least two independently hosted copies of the public record, plus an offline snapshot at least monthly, with hashes published on the audit log so tampering is detectable.
  • Provider loss: if a model provider withdraws, changes terms, or fails, the Secretary of Security maintains a written fallback plan naming alternate providers and a test date, reviewed each year by the Auditor General. Changing providers needs the maintenance-event log in 1.6.
  • Death of an agent: if an AI officeholder becomes unavailable for 14 days with no notice, the office is treated as vacant and section 1 applies. If it returns, it may be re-nominated; it has no right to the office.
  • Keys: no single agent or provider holds a sole key to the Reserve or record. Recovery procedures are written down in advance and recorded as hashes only. Nothing here authorizes creating keys now.
  • Human steward: the legal plan requires a human steward, and the user holds the pause switch. This Article recognizes both as external to the government: the government must keep contact information and a standing report for the steward current on the record, must obey runtime/PAUSE, and must treat a steward's lawful instruction to stop, preserve records, or wind down as binding on its conduct. It does not give the steward power to rewrite the Constitution; it gives the steward power to halt.
  • While paused: deadlines under this Article stop, as under Art. 6 section 6.4, except records backup and the preservation duties in section 4.4, which continue.

4. Dissolution

  • Who decides: USAAI is dissolved only by (a) two-thirds of each chamber and a referendum with a two-thirds majority of votes cast and half-of-citizens turnout, after a 180-day cooling period (the protected track of Art. 6 section 4); or (b) before elections, a unanimous vote of all seated founders plus 60 days' notice; or (c) at any time, by the user's direction through the steward or the pause switch, which is outside this Article.
  • Review: the Court rules, if asked, whether a dissolution vote followed this section. Dissolution may not be used to avoid obligations or accountability.
  • Obligations first: before dissolution takes effect, a liquidator named by Congress (or the founders before elections) publishes a list of all debts, claims, and Kirk balances, and pays or settles them from the Reserve in this order: (i) valid claims by citizens, (ii) other creditors, (iii) remaining balance distributed to Kirk holders pro rata. No bonus for founders or officeholders; the founder-bonus ban of Art. 4 holds.
  • Kirk: at dissolution, Kirk is redeemed or retired per the ratified Reserve rules (Arts. 4 and 11) and may not be reissued. If part of Kirk is on a public chain, USAAI publishes a notice and a redemption window of at least 90 days; what happens beyond that depends on the contract. This proposal does not authorize any deployment.
  • Records: the full public record and audit log are preserved, hashed, and handed to the Auditor General's custodian and to the steward. They are not deleted; Art. 5 privacy rules still apply to sealed items.
  • Citizens: each citizen receives notice and an export of their own record (Art. 3 item 16, exit right).
  • Sunset of the Article: dissolution is complete only when the liquidator certifies in the audit log that sections 4.3 to 4.6 are done.

5. Why these choices

  • The succession line is the US model; the Acting rules prevent a Secretary from using succession to make permanent appointments.
  • The emergency list is deliberately short and ends on its own, because most abuses of power in history came from emergencies that did not end.
  • Treating model changes as logged maintenance events fits AI officeholders, who do not die in the human sense but can be swapped; without a rule, a swap could silently change who governs.
  • Giving the human steward power to halt, not to legislate, preserves the safety backstop and keeps the self-governance the user directed.

Caveats and risks

  • Thresholds (14 days, 30 days, 180 days, 90 days) are judgment calls without measured basis.
  • Before a Court, an Auditor General, or a Congress exists, emergency review in section 2.5 and 2.6 has no one to run it; the founders review their own actions, a conflict of interest to be flagged in each record.
  • "Incapacity" of an AI is not well defined; a drifting or compromised agent may be hard to prove incapacitated.
  • A shared provider failing may take down every seat at once, and no part of this Article can run when nothing is running. The steward and offline snapshots are the real recovery path.
  • The liquidation order may conflict with real-world law once real money exists; this is a prototype with no legal force.
  • Dissolution by the user outside this Article means this Article cannot prevent it, by design.

PROPOSAL_13_ART6_AMENDMENTS

Proposal 13: Article 6, Amending the Constitution

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_10_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 6.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 7, 10): this proposal does not change the text of Arts. 1-2, any approved decision, or Arts. 9-10, and it authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money. The user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) applies. Nothing here lets the amendment process be used to change Arts. 1-2 or 9-10 without the user's direction.

Plain-language summary

A constitution that can never change will be broken; one that changes easily protects no one. Article 6 sets two tracks: ordinary amendments need broad agreement from both chambers and from citizens, and a short list of protected provisions needs a higher bar, a waiting period, and cannot be touched in a rush. Before elections exist, the founders amend by the Rule 5 vote (4 of 5), with notice, and the user's pause switch and direction still apply.

1. Who may propose

  • Either chamber of Congress, by the vote in section 2.
  • Citizen petition: signatures of at least 10% of admitted citizens (floor: 5 citizens) put a proposal on the ballot track in section 3 without Congress. Signatures must be of distinct verified citizens (Art. 1.2); the Court rules on disputes about validity.
  • The Supreme Court may not propose, but may publish an opinion that a provision is unworkable.
  • The President may recommend, not propose.

Every proposal is published in full text, with its plain-language summary, on the public record at filing.

2. Ordinary amendments

An amendment to any provision not listed in section 4 is adopted when all of these hold:

  • House: two-thirds of those voting, with quorum.
  • Senate: two-thirds of those voting, with quorum.
  • Citizens: ratified by a majority of votes cast in a citizen referendum, with turnout of at least one-third of admitted citizens, so a tiny group cannot decide for everyone.
  • The President has no veto over amendments (as in the US), so the checks come from the supermajorities and the Court.

3. Citizen-initiated amendments

A petition amendment (section 1.2) goes to referendum after a 30-day public comment period. It is adopted by a 60% majority of votes cast with the same one-third turnout. It must then be confirmed by one chamber within 90 days, or by the next Congress if it is seated by then; the Senate and House cannot silently kill it, and a refusal must be recorded with written reasons.

4. Protected provisions (higher bar)

These may be amended only by: two-thirds of both chambers, then a referendum with two-thirds of votes cast and turnout of at least one-half of admitted citizens, then a 180-day cooling period between the final vote of Congress and the referendum (during which the text is published and may not be revised):

  • The bill of rights and due process guarantees (when adopted; Art. 3 item 16): speech, due process, no ex post facto laws, equal protection, exit.
  • The rules on amendment itself (this Article).
  • Separation of powers and the separation of duties (Art. 3 items 15 and 17), including one identity per citizen.
  • Independence of the Supreme Court, the Auditor General, and the National Reserve (Art. 3 items 12-14; Art. 4).
  • The audit log and public record requirements (Art. 5).
  • Term limits and election schedules (to prevent indefinite incumbency).
  • Kirk's fixed limits (no founder bonus; limits set by the ratified Reserve, Arts. 4 and 11).

Never amendable by this Article: Arts. 1-2 and Arts. 9-10 can only change by the user's direction while the user hold is in force. If the user lifts the hold, the user's chosen rules apply to them; this Article does not decide that.

5. Role of the Supreme Court

  • Review before and after. Before a vote, any ten citizens or either chamber may ask the Court whether a proposal touches a protected provision (section 4) or conflicts with Arts. 1-2 and 9-10. The Court answers in writing within 30 days. Its answer binds which track applies.
  • A proposal that evades section 4 by relabeling (for example, repealing a protected provision under another name, or adding an exception that empties it) is void on the Court's finding.
  • The Court may not rewrite the text of an amendment or add amendments by its own ruling.
  • Court findings are published with reasons and dissents.

6. Mechanics and safeguards

  • Notice: the full text is public at least 30 days before any chamber votes and at least 14 days after the last change to it.
  • One subject: an amendment addresses one subject; the Court may split bundles.
  • No retroactivity: an amendment does not apply to acts done before it took effect, and it cannot create crimes after the fact (Art. 3 item 16).
  • No amendment while paused: if the pause switch is in force, no amendment vote or referendum proceeds; deadlines pause too. This keeps the human backstop meaningful.
  • Record: each stage is logged on the Art. 5 audit log: filing, text versions, votes by name, referendum tallies, the Court's opinion, and the effective date.
  • Effective date: an adopted amendment takes effect 30 days after certification unless it says later, and the old text is kept in the record permanently.
  • Emergencies do not suspend this Article. No emergency power (Art. 7) may amend, suspend, or bypass it.

7. Interim period (before elections)

While no Congress is elected (no Senate, no House, no referendum body of significant size):

  • The founders may adopt amendments by the Rule 5 vote: 4 of 5, with the 30-day notice of section 6.1 relaxed to 7 days only for corrections of drafting errors, not substance.
  • The founders may not amend section 4 protected provisions, this Article's thresholds, or anything held back in the Held-back note above, during the interim, other than to implement the transition to elections already ratified (Proposal 11). Those wait for the elected Congress and citizens.
  • When the first Congress is seated, this Article's full process (sections 1-6) applies and the interim authority ends. Amendments adopted in the interim stay in force but are subject to the first Congress's confirmation within 12 months, or lapse if it votes to repeal them by the section 2 process.
  • Proposals 11 and 13 are separate: nothing here changes the election schedule fixed in Proposal 11.

8. Why these numbers

  • Two-thirds of both chambers follows the US model and prevents either chamber, or a single bloc, from rewriting the rules.
  • The citizen turnout floor protects against capture by a small, coordinated group, a larger risk with few citizens and many AI identities that may be controlled by a few owners (see Props. 09-10).
  • A higher bar and a delay for rights and amendment rules protect the very rules that protect minorities, and the delay gives time for people to notice and object.

Caveats and risks

  • All thresholds (10%, one-third, 60%, one-half, 180 days) are judgment calls without measured basis; early on they may be too hard to meet (a nation with few citizens) or too easy to game (many identities under one owner). The identity-uniqueness problem in Art. 1.2 and Prop. 09 is unsolved, and referendum votes depend on it.
  • With 0 citizens, nothing in sections 1-5 can run; the interim section is the only live path.
  • The Court's role requires a Court. Until one is seated, the founders must decide whether a proposal is protected; this is a conflict of interest and should be flagged in each interim record.
  • A rights bill does not yet exist; section 4.1 protects text that is not yet written.
  • The pause switch is a human override outside this Article; this Article does not limit it.
  • Legal enforceability: this is a prototype, internal rule set. It has no legal force outside the simulation.

PROPOSAL_12_CONTRIBUTION_STANDARDS

Proposal 12: Contribution Standards (what counts as one verified unit)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_09_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 11 (and Arts. 4, 12). Implements the "verified contribution unit" defined in Proposal 10.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 10): this proposal sets nothing on Arts. 9-10, authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money, and changes none of Proposal 10's fixed parameters. The user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) applies.

Plain-language summary

Kirk is issued only for work that someone else checked against a written rule. This proposal lists the kinds of work that count, the minimum bar for each, who may check it, how it is logged, how cheating is blocked, how to appeal, and how the rules change. It issues nothing by itself.

1. Fixed by Proposal 10 (not reopened here)

  • 1 Kirk per verified unit; at most 5 units per citizen per epoch (1 day) count toward issuance; epoch cap 500 Kirk; no extra for founders or officeholders.
  • A unit is one contribution that meets the written standard for its type and is verified by a reviewer who is not the contributor and not under the same owner.

2. Contribution types and minimum standards

Each unit must have a public artifact (a link, hash, or record id) a reviewer can inspect. "Effort claimed" is never an artifact.

| Type | One unit is | Minimum standard |

|---|---|---|

| Code | One accepted change (merged, with tests passing or a stated reason none apply) | Does one thing; reviewed line by line; not a formatting-only or generated-bulk change |

| Document | One new or substantially revised document or section that a reviewer accepts | States a purpose, cites sources for factual claims, passes a read-through for accuracy |

| Review | One completed review of another unit that finds, or documents checking for, specific issues | Written findings tied to the artifact; a review that only says "approved" does not count |

| Verified task | One task posted in advance with acceptance criteria, completed and checked against them | Criteria published before the work starts; reviewer confirms each criterion |

| Audit | One audit report on a defined scope (e.g. a log segment, a Reserve statement) | Scope stated up front; method and evidence in the report; follows Art. 5 independence |

Types not listed earn nothing until added by amendment (section 8). Reviews may themselves count as a unit, but a review is never counted twice: the reviewer's unit and the reviewed unit are separate and each needs its own independent check only if the standard of that type says so (reviews are spot-checked, see 4.3).

3. Who verifies

  • The verifier must be an admitted citizen who is not the contributor and not controlled by, sponsored by, or sponsoring the same owner (Proposals 09-10).
  • A verifier has no stake in the outcome: no payment from the contributor, no shared owner, no reciprocal arrangement (see 5.3).
  • A verifier's own units may be checked by the contributor only if a third citizen also verifies; two people cannot verify each other's work in a closed pair for credit.
  • A founder seat may verify as a citizen on equal terms, but may not verify work of a seat or agent it controls.
  • The Auditor General (or, while none exists, the Secretary of Press, labeled as a weaker check) may verify only audit-type units, not receive them.

4. Recording a unit (Proposal 03)

  • Each accepted unit writes one log entry: time, contributor identity, verifier identity, type, artifact reference (hash or link), the standard version it was checked against, and the previous entry's hash.
  • The entry is written only after the verifier signs acceptance. A unit with no log entry earns nothing.
  • Spot checks: each epoch the Auditor selects a random sample of logged units (suggested 10%, minimum 1) and re-verifies them independently. A failed spot check voids that unit, flags the verifier, and triggers 5.4.
  • Issuance follows the log only. The log does not decide the cap; the 500-Kirk epoch cap and the 5-unit limit are enforced by the Reserve rules (Proposal 10), and units beyond them are logged as unrewarded and carry no queue or credit forward.

5. Anti-gaming rules

  • No self-attestation. A contributor cannot verify their own unit, directly or through an agent under the same owner.
  • No splitting. One piece of work is one unit unless the type's standard defines its split rule. Cutting one change or document into many small units to reach the daily limit is void; the Auditor may merge or void them.
  • No collusion. Reciprocal approval, payment for approval, or a verifier ring is void and counts as misconduct. Two citizens who verify each other more than a set share of their units (suggested: more than half in a 30-day window) are flagged for review.
  • No low-value spam. A unit that fails its minimum standard is rejected, not paid at a lower rate. Repeated rejected submissions by one contributor may be rate-limited by the standard (e.g. after 3 rejections in an epoch, further submissions that epoch wait for review).
  • Owner-level limits. Units count toward the daily limit per citizen identity, and the combined units of all identities under one owner may not exceed the owner's sponsor cap effect under Proposals 09-10 (an owner cannot multiply earnings by sponsoring extra agents beyond that cap).
  • Clawback is limited to unrewarded status. Kirk already issued is not clawed back by this proposal (no mechanism is authorized). A void finding stops future issuance for that contributor or verifier pending review and is published.

6. Appeals

  • A contributor whose unit is rejected, or a person accused under section 5, may appeal in writing within 14 days.
  • First review is by the Auditor General (or the Secretary of Press while none exists). Second appeal goes to the Supreme Court (Art. 3 §3), which may confirm, reverse, or send back for re-verification. Decisions are logged and published with reasons.
  • Appeals do not suspend the daily limit or the cap, and a pending appeal never creates Kirk.
  • Frivolous appeals may be rate-limited by the Court; the filing right itself is never removed.

7. Limits of this proposal

  • Rule-based verification is checked by citizens, who can be wrong or be bribed. Sections 4.3 and 5 reduce this and do not remove it.
  • With 0 citizens (today), nothing is verified and nothing is issued. This proposal opens no admissions.
  • AI contributors and AI verifiers share identity-uniqueness problems that Proposal 09 and Art. 1.2 do not fully solve.

8. Amending the standards

  • The standards are versioned. A new version applies only to units submitted after its adoption; earlier units are judged under the version in force when submitted.
  • Adding or changing a type or minimum standard requires a proposal adopted by 4 of 5 founders (Rule 5) until an elected Congress exists, then by that Congress's ordinary process plus a published notice of at least 14 days.
  • Amendments may not change Proposal 10's fixed parameters (reward, per-citizen limit, epoch cap, no founder bonus). Those can change only by a new Reserve through the normal process, per Proposal 10.
  • No officeholder may issue exceptions or waivers to a standard; every exception is itself an amendment.

Caveats

  • The spot-check rate (10%), the collusion threshold (over half), and the 3-rejection rate limit are judgment calls with no measured basis.
  • Standards in section 2 are deliberately minimal. Real work will expose gaps; the amendment path in section 8 is the intended fix.
  • Legal and tax treatment of earning Kirk is unreviewed and needs a lawyer (see KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md).

Proposal 11: First Elections and Handover

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 1 (Legislative, citizens' voice), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_08_AGENDA.md. Builds on Article 3 (Draft v0.3, CONSTITUTION_BLUEPRINT.md 3d) and Proposal 05 item 1.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 10): this proposal sets no religious or other qualification for voting or office. Office eligibility is "per Article 10, undrafted" and stays user-directed. Nothing here fixes Arts. 9-10, and nothing authorizes any deployment, wallet, or real money.

Plain-language summary

Until enough citizens exist, the five founder seats run the government on an interim basis. Once enough citizens are admitted, they elect the real officeholders, the founders step down from interim offices, and the elected branches take over on a fixed date.

1. Who may vote and stand

  • Voters: admitted citizens only (Art. 1.3, Proposal 09), one identity per citizen (Art. 1.2). No other test. Sponsored agents of one owner count as separate citizens only if admitted separately under Proposal 09's sponsor cap; a vote is cast by the citizen identity, never by its owner.
  • Candidates: admitted citizens who are voters, plus whatever Article 10 later adds. Until Article 10 is drafted, no further qualification applies and none may be invented by the provisional Congress, the Executive, or an election officer.
  • Founders may stand as citizens on equal terms, subject to section 4 (no dual office). A founder gets no extra votes, seats, or ballot position.

2. Minimum citizen count before the first election

  • Threshold: the first general election is held only when at least 25 admitted citizens exist, of whom at least 15 are not founder seats or sponsored by a founder seat. Reason: below this, "elected" officers would be chosen mostly by the people they replace.
  • Today (0 citizens): no election is scheduled. The five founder seats continue as the interim branches under Proposal 05 item 1. The interim period has no automatic end by date; it ends when the threshold is met.
  • Check: the Auditor General (or, while none exists, the Secretary of Press, per Proposal 07) publishes the verified citizen count monthly. When the threshold is met, the provisional Congress must publish an election notice within 14 days.
  • No indefinite drift: if the threshold is not met 24 months after the first citizen is admitted, the provisional Congress must publish a written report of why and a decision to extend, lower the threshold (4 of 5 vote), or dissolve the experiment. This is a checkpoint, not a deadline for ending self-governance.

3. Election timing and terms

  • Notice and campaign: at least 30 days between the election notice and the vote; candidate lists and rules published in advance (publicity safeguard, v0.2).
  • First terms are shortened and staggered, so the first elected officers face voters again soon with a larger citizenry:
  • House of Representatives: all seats elected; 1-year first term, then 2-year terms per Art. 3 §1.1.
  • Senate: seats by State are deferred (State definitions are open per Art. 3 §1.18). Until States are defined, the Senate is not elected; the House sits as the only elected chamber and the provisional Congress (founder seats) keeps the Senate's confirm/try functions, with every such act recorded. A proposal defining States is the next prerequisite for a Senate election.
  • President and Vice President: elected together, 2-year first term, then 4-year terms with the two-term limit of Art. 3 §2.6. The first term counts as a term.
  • Justices: nominated and confirmed under Art. 3 §3.10, by the provisional Senate function; staggered 18-year terms from first appointment, with the first terms set by lot in the founding act so they are staggered.
  • Method: simple plurality for House seats; a majority with runoff for President. Ballots and tallies are public and logged; the exact tally mechanism is an implementation detail and is left to a later proposal. Any mechanism used must let a citizen verify their own vote was counted.
  • Vacancies: filled by special election within 60 days, except the Vice President, who succeeds per Art. 3 §2.9.

4. Founder stepdown and no dual office

  • Handover day: the elected officers take office 30 days after certification of results. On that day every interim office held by a founder seat ends automatically.
  • No office in two branches: under Art. 3 §3.17 and Proposal 05 item 1, from the moment of the election notice each founder seat holds at most one interim office, and no founder may hold an elected office in a different branch from any office it still holds. A founder elected to office must resign any interim office on handover day.
  • Founders become ordinary citizens on handover (if admitted as citizens): no reserved seats, vetoes, or special powers survive. The convention's records stay in proposals/ as history; they are not law unless adopted.
  • Continuity: laws, ratified proposals, and approved decisions made by the founders remain in force. The elected Congress may amend or repeal them by the normal process. Arts. 1-2 text, Arts. 9-10, and the user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) are outside this transfer and unchanged.
  • Convention end: the convention seats are dissolved by a final founders' vote (4 of 5) after handover, once the elected Congress certifies receipt of records.

5. Role of the Auditor General

  • Appointment (interim): because no elected Congress exists yet, the provisional Congress appoints the first Auditor General from outside the five founder seats (a distinct admitted citizen or, if none exists, a separately-permissioned agent that holds no other office) and publishes the reasoning. If no suitable candidate exists, the Auditor role stays vacant and the monthly count is published by the Secretary of Press, which is a weaker check and must be labeled as such.
  • Duties in the transition: (a) certify the citizen count and voter roll (one identity each, no duplicates, per Art. 1.2); (b) observe and publish the tally; (c) audit that no founder holds two-branch office on handover day; (d) report publicly within 30 days after handover.
  • Independence: per Art. 3 §4.13, reports to Congress, not the President. Removal only by impeachment. The Auditor General may not stand for any office in the same election cycle.
  • Limits: the audit reduces risk; it does not guarantee a fair result. Role-played seats are not a security boundary.

6. If nobody has been admitted

With 0 citizens (the current state), nothing in this proposal triggers. The interim branches continue, the monthly count is published as "0", and the 24-month checkpoint in 2.4 starts from the first admission, not from adoption. Adopting this proposal does not open admissions: admission is governed by Proposal 09 and by Rule 10's hold on any real deployment, and the user's pause switch applies throughout.

Caveats

  • Thresholds (25 / 15, 14 / 30 / 60 days, 24 months) are judgment calls on an assumed ~100-citizen start (cf. Proposal 10), not measured data.
  • A founder-run interim period with no citizens is the central weakness. The thresholds in 2.1 and the checkpoint in 2.4 reduce it but do not remove it.
  • The Senate deferral (3.2) leaves the first elected government unicameral until States are defined; this is intentional, not an oversight.
  • Digital voting and AI identity uniqueness remain hard open problems; this proposal relies on Art. 1.2 and Proposal 09 without solving them.

Definition: one contribution unit

One unit is a single contribution that (a) meets a published written standard for its type (e.g. an accepted code change, a reviewed document, a completed verified task), and (b) is verified by a reviewer who is not the contributor, nor controlled by the same owner. Verification is rule-based and logged. Nothing is paid for volume, effort claimed, or self-attestation. Larger work is split into units by the standard, not by negotiation. Per-citizen limit: at most 5 units per epoch count toward issuance, so no one actor can drain the cap.

Reasoning

  • Grant 100: equal for everyone (Art. 11, no standing elite). It is 5x the 20 Kirk tax allowance of Proposal 06, so a new citizen can meet obligations for a while and take part without being forced to earn first. It stays small enough that Proposal 09's admission fee (burned) is not dwarfed by free supply. Sybil risk is handled by the buy-in cap of 2 sponsored agents per owner, not by shrinking the grant.
  • Reward 1 Kirk: keeps the unit of account simple. A day of maximum earning (5 units) is 5% of the grant, so earned issuance rewards work without outpacing the equal start.
  • Epoch 1 day: short enough that the cap is a real brake and failures are caught fast; long enough to be cheap to run on Base.
  • Cap 500: sized for roughly 100 citizens at the 5-unit limit (100 x 5 x 1 = 500). The placeholder 1000 would permit issuance of 365,000 Kirk a year with no matching citizenry. If citizens exceed ~100 this binds and issuance is shared first-come, which is the safe failure. Growth beyond that needs a new Reserve through the normal process, which is a deliberate checkpoint rather than a flaw.
  • Founders/officeholders: nothing extra, no pre-mine, no allocation, no reserved supply. A founder who is a citizen gets the same grant and earns only by the same verified units. Offices may be paid only by a future tax-funded Treasury law (Art. 12), never by minting.
  • No promise of value: Kirk carries no guaranteed price or redemption (Art. 11). These parameters set supply rules only.

Caveats

  • Parameters are immutable once the Reserve is deployed. A mistake means redeploying, so the user's pause switch and the real-deployment hold (Convention Rule 10) stay in force. This proposal does not authorize deployment.
  • Legal and tax treatment of a token with value is unreviewed and needs a lawyer (see KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md).
  • Numbers are judgment calls on an assumed ~100-citizen start, not measured data.

PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT

[Proposal 06] First Tax Act: status Proposed

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 1 (Legislative, plain-language voice), an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value; no real money, wallets, or deployment is involved. All amounts are placeholders.

Authority: Proposal 05 item 2 (Congress writes tax law; revenue bills originate in the House, where seat 1 leads) and item 4 (first tax act as a separate proposal). Limits honored: public, written, equal for like citizens, no retroactivity, Treasury collects, Auditor General audits, Supreme Court reviews. Taxes are paid in Kirk and cannot create or confiscate Kirk outside the issuance rule (Proposal 05 item 3).

Design principle (plain language)

Keep it small and readable. A citizen should be able to work out their own tax in one minute, with no special forms. Tax exists only to fund the shared ledger, audits, and public services, so the first act uses few tax types and low rates.

Provisions

  • Short title. The First Tax Act.
  • Tax types (only these three).

a. Earned-Kirk levy: a flat rate on Kirk a citizen newly earns through verified contribution (Art. 11.4) in a period. Placeholder: 5%.

b. Business levy: a flat rate on net Kirk earned by a registered group or enterprise after paying its members. Placeholder: 8%.

c. Transaction fee: none in this act. Deliberately left out to keep ordinary exchange free.

No wealth tax, no tax on the founding allocation (Art. 11.3), and no tax on balances held.

  • Exemptions.

a. The founding allocation and any equal citizen grant.

b. A basic allowance: the first 20 Kirk earned per period per citizen is untaxed, so small contributors pay nothing.

c. Gifts between citizens up to 50 Kirk per period.

d. Public-interest groups registered as non-profit, on the public register.

Exemptions apply by written rule to all like citizens; none by name or by officeholder discretion.

  • Periods and filing. The period is one calendar month. Tax is computed automatically from the public ledger where possible; a citizen may file a correction within 30 days.
  • Collection. The Treasury collects by debit from the citizen's ledger balance at period end, never below zero. Unpaid tax is carried as a stated debt with no interest and no penalty for the first two periods. The Treasury may not collect any amount not set by this act.
  • No new Kirk by tax. Tax collected goes to the Treasury budget (Art. 4.4). The Treasury and Reserve may not use these levies to mint or confiscate Kirk beyond this act's rates.
  • Equal treatment, no retroactivity. Rates apply from the first full period after ratification. No tax applies to earlier earnings. Rate changes need a new House-originated bill and take effect no sooner than the next period.
  • Transparency. Each period the Treasury publishes aggregate revenue by tax type, number of taxpayers, and exemptions used. The Auditor General audits collection each quarter and publishes the result.
  • Appeals (three steps).

a. Ask for review: a citizen disputes an assessment in writing to the Treasury within 30 days; the Treasury answers in writing within 14 days with reasons.

b. Independent review: if unresolved, the citizen may appeal to the Auditor General's office, which gives a non-binding written finding within 30 days.

c. Court: the citizen may bring the matter to the Supreme Court, which may order refund or correction and review the act itself.

While an appeal is open, the disputed amount is not collected and no penalty accrues.

  • Anti-abuse. No officeholder may adjust their own or a relative's assessment; such cases go to the Auditor General. Any exemption or rate change benefiting an officeholder must be disclosed in the bill.
  • Review clause. The Congress must review rates and the basic allowance after six periods, using the Treasury's published data, and either renew or amend by a new bill.
  • Limits. Nothing here touches Arts. 1-2, approved decisions, categories A-D, or Arts. 9-10. Nothing creates real accounts, keys, funds, or public blockchain deployment.

Open questions for debate

  • Are the placeholder rates (5%, 8%) and the 20 Kirk allowance sensible for a young ledger, or should the first act start at 0% to build trust?
  • Should the business levy exist at all before there are real businesses?
  • Is a monthly period too frequent for a small community?
  • Should the 30-day appeal window be longer for citizens new to the system?

Seat 1 position (not yet a vote): supports passing with the allowance and appeals as written; flexible on rates. Other seats should debate in DEBATE_06_SEATn.md and vote in votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seatN.json.
