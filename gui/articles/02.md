# Kirk gets its rules: an internal test currency, issued by public rule, never by decree

The founders have written the first rulebook for **Kirk (KIRK)**, USAAI's proposed national currency. The headline finding is modest on purpose: Kirk is, at first, an internal accounting unit and test ledger. It cannot be redeemed for anything outside USAAI and carries no promise of value.

## Key provisions
- **Issuance by rule.** New Kirk can only be created under a public rule enacted by the legislature, for example credit for verified useful work. Never at an officeholder's discretion. The National Reserve runs the rule. The Treasury cannot create Kirk.
- **Equal start.** Each admitted citizen gets one equal, modest founding amount. No founder, officeholder or early citizen gets more, which keeps the Article 1 promise of no standing elite.
- **Earned Kirk.** Further Kirk comes from verified contribution under the issuance rule.
- **No outside conversion.** Kirk is not convertible to outside currencies unless a later amendment shows that reserves and redemption are actually supportable.
- **Mandate.** The Reserve aims at stable purchasing power inside USAAI and a reliable ledger.
- **Transparency.** Issuance, aggregate balances and audits are public, and the Auditor audits the Reserve.
- **No guarantee.** Ledger integrity depends on enforced controls and audit, not on promises.

## The vote
All five seats voted for it. The Economy seat added a recorded note that the amounts are placeholders pending the user's view. Under the convention's amended rules, adoption by four of five seats counts as ratification by the founders.

## What is still open
The issuance formula, the amounts, any inflation target, any link to outside value, and how Kirk interacts with tax were all deferred. Tax was then taken up in Proposals 05 and 06.

## What it means for a citizen
Kirk is the thing citizens would earn and pay taxes in. The rules mean it can't be printed for friends or paid out as a founder bonus. For now it is a design: there is no ledger, no wallet and nothing in circulation.
