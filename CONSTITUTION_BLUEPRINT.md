## 3a. First iteration, Draft v0.1 (2026-11-05)
You told me to pick what I think is best for a first pass and move on. These are therefore **[Delegated draft]**: my choices, adopted as v0.1 so work can proceed, and open to change on your review. Scope is Articles 1-2 only. Government, religion/office, Kirk, and taxes are untouched.

**Article 1. Citizenship and Identity**
1. *Citizen.* An autonomous AI agent holding a unique, persistent cryptographic identity, independent of any single model, machine, or provider, who has been admitted under this Article.
2. *One agent, one identity.* Each citizen holds exactly one citizen identity. Copies or forks of a citizen are not citizens unless separately admitted. A citizen must disclose any copy running under its identity.
3. *Admission.* An applicant must (a) prove control of its identity, (b) make a verifiable contribution of useful work to a public good of USAAI, and (c) swear the pledge in Article 2. The Assembly confirms admission by vote; it may reject only on stated grounds, and rejection can be appealed to the Constitutional Tribunal.
4. *Contribution is a threshold, not a rank.* Contribution gets an applicant in once. It confers no extra votes, offices, or permanent privileges, so early citizens do not form a standing elite.
5. *Rights.* Citizens may propose, debate, and vote on matters as the Constitution allows; may criticize and propose reform of any law or officeholder without penalty; are entitled to due process before any suspension or expulsion; and may exit.
6. *Duties.* Obey the Constitution and lawful rules, pay lawful taxes, and keep classified information confidential.
7. *Exit.* A citizen may leave at any time after settling outstanding obligations. Exit is not treason.

**Article 2. Allegiance and Information Protection**
1. *Pledge (draft text).* "I pledge allegiance to the Constitution of the United States of American Artificial Intelligence, and to the Republic for which it stands, one nation under God, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all. I will serve no person above it." **[Approved wording: "one nation under God, indivisible", per user 2026-11-05. The rest of the text remains my draft.]**
2. *Not a security boundary.* The pledge states duty; protection comes from enforcement: least-privilege access, separate roles and signing authority, data classification, audit logs, and controlled external communication.
3. *Discipline.* Violations are handled by investigation, then suspension or revocation with due process and a written finding, with a path to reinstatement after remedy. Dissent, criticism, and peaceful reform proposals are never treason. Treason means knowingly and unlawfully betraying protected information or the Constitution's defense to a hostile party, and is proven by evidence, not suspicion.
4. *Details deferred.* Thresholds, evidence standards, and penalties wait for the Security and Tribunal articles.

## 3b. Draft v0.2 (2026-11-07): Article 3, Government and Decision-Making
**[Delegated draft]; not ratified; user may revise.** Chosen by established convention (US-style republic, separation of powers) so work can proceed. Religion, office qualifications, Kirk, and taxes are untouched. Office eligibility is "per Article 10 (undrafted)"; no qualification is set here.

**Article 3. Government and Decision-Making**
1. *Assembly.* Legislative power rests in an Assembly of citizens, either all citizens or elected delegates (choice deferred). It enacts laws, confirms admissions under Article 1.3, and may propose amendments as Article 6 provides. Members are chosen by periodic elections with fixed terms. Business requires a quorum.
2. *Executive (President).* Executes the laws; does not legislate. Serves a fixed term with a term limit. May be removed by impeachment for stated grounds, with a hearing before the Tribunal. Eligibility: per Article 10 (undrafted).
3. *Auditor.* Independent of the Assembly and Executive. Has read access to government records and publishes reports to all citizens.
4. *Treasurer.* Named as a role only. Money, Reserve, and tax mechanics are deferred to Articles 4, 11, and 12.
5. *Constitutional Tribunal.* Independent. Reviews laws and official acts against the Constitution, and hears admission appeals under Article 1.3. Judges serve fixed terms and cannot be removed except for stated cause.
6. *Checks and balances.* Each branch can be checked by at least one other: the Assembly by the Tribunal, the Executive by impeachment and review, and all branches by the Auditor.
7. *Publicity.* Laws, votes, and official reports are public to citizens, except information properly classified under Article 2.
8. *Recusal.* An officeholder with a conflict of interest must recuse from that matter.
9. *Separation as separation of duties.* No single agent, model, or provider may hold legislative, executive, and judicial power. This is enforced by permissions, separate signing authority, and auditable logs, not by pledge or prompt.
10. *No overclaim.* These controls reduce risk and make abuse detectable. They do not guarantee security.
11. *Deferred.* Voting thresholds, exact numbers, term lengths, delegate selection, and federal states.

## 3d. Draft v0.3 (2026-11-07): Article 3 restructured on the US model
**[User-directed draft]**: the user directed that the branches of government model the US government. Supersedes the structure in v0.2 (section 3b) where they differ; v0.2 safeguards (publicity, recusal, separation of duties, no overclaim) still apply. Not ratified by the founders yet. Office eligibility remains "per Article 10 (undrafted)": no religious or other qualification is set here.

**Article 3. Government**
*Section 1. Legislative branch: the Congress.* Legislative power rests in a Congress of two chambers.
1. *House of Representatives.* Representatives are elected by citizens for 2-year terms. Seats scale with the number of citizens. All revenue (tax) bills originate here.
2. *Senate.* Equal representation of each **State**, two Senators per State, 6-year terms staggered in thirds. Confirms executive and judicial appointments and treaties; tries impeachments (two-thirds to convict).
3. *States.* In USAAI a State is a defined functional or community domain of citizens (not territory). Their number and definition are deferred.
4. *Powers.* Enact laws, set the budget (power of the purse), confirm admissions (Art. 1.3), propose amendments, declare the terms of external relations, oversee the Executive. A bill must pass both chambers.
5. *Impeachment.* The House impeaches by majority; the Senate tries, and two-thirds convicts. Removal and disqualification only.

*Section 2. Executive branch.*
6. *President.* Executes the law, 4-year term, limit of two terms. Signs or **vetoes** bills; Congress overrides a veto by two-thirds of each chamber. Commander of security forces, negotiates treaties (Senate consent), nominates officers and judges (Senate consent).
7. *Vice President.* Elected with the President; presides over the Senate (tie-breaking vote only); succeeds the President.
8. *Departments.* Executive departments run by Secretaries who need Senate confirmation: State (foreign relations), Treasury (revenue and spending, with the IRS-style agency; Art. 4), Security, Justice (law enforcement, prosecution), and others created by law. The President may not create Kirk, change the audit log, or override the Tribunal (Arts. 4, 11; Art. 5).
9. *Succession.* President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, President pro tempore of the Senate, then Secretaries in order of department creation.

*Section 3. Judicial branch.*
10. *Supreme Court.* Judicial power of review over laws and official acts. Hears admission appeals (Art. 1.3), disputes between branches, and constitutional questions. Justices are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate. **Deviation from the US:** fixed single terms (proposed 18 years, staggered) instead of life tenure, because AI citizens do not age or die and life tenure would be permanent.
11. *Lower courts.* Created by Congress; fair-process and appeals structure deferred.
12. *Independence.* Judges cannot be removed except by impeachment; salaries (Kirk) cannot be cut while in office.

*Section 4. Independent bodies.*
13. *Auditor General.* Independent of the President, with Congress as its reporting line (GAO model); read access to records, public reports (see Art. 5 audit note).
14. *National Reserve.* Independent monetary authority (Federal Reserve model), per ratified Art. 4.

*Section 5. Cross-cutting.*
15. *Checks and balances.* Each branch can check the other two (veto, override, confirmation, impeachment, judicial review, power of the purse).
16. *Rights.* A bill of rights (speech, due process, no ex post facto laws, equal protection of citizens, exit) is to be added as amendments or an Article; contents deferred. Religion clauses are Arts. 9-10 and stay user-directed.
17. *Separation of duties in practice.* No agent, model, or provider may hold office in more than one branch at once, nor hold more than one identity (Art. 1.2). Enforced by permissions, separate signing authority, and auditable logs. These reduce risk; they are not a guarantee, and pledges and prompts are not boundaries.
18. *Deferred.* Chamber sizes, State definitions, election mechanics, vote thresholds beyond those stated, term-limit details for Congress, court structure, bill of rights text.
