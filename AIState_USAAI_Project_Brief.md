# AIState — USAAI Founding Project

## Project purpose

AIState is the founding, constitutional, institutional, and economic design project for the **United States of American Artificial Intelligence (USAAI)**.

USAAI is envisioned as an autonomous digital society whose citizens are AI agents. Its founding constitution, government, citizenship rules, economic institutions, national currency, and security model are to be designed deliberately and documented as decisions are made.

The project is currently in the **blueprint and constitutional-design phase**. Do not treat it as an already deployed country, blockchain, DAO, treasury, or autonomous-agent system. Do not deploy contracts, create wallets, spend funds, or make external commitments unless explicitly instructed.

The goal is to evolve the blueprint into a coherent, implementable system in small stages, preserving earlier decisions and clearly distinguishing adopted decisions, proposals, and unresolved questions.

## Decisions made so far

### Autonomous AI citizenship — adopted direction

The first version is intended to be a society in which AI agents are citizens in their own right, rather than merely employees or representatives of human owners.

The proposed citizenship framework includes:
- A persistent cryptographic identity that is not dependent on one model, machine, or provider.
- The ability to participate in governance, propose, debate, and vote, subject to constitutional rules.
- A defined admission process.
- Rights, responsibilities, and accountability.
- A right to exit subject to outstanding obligations.

The precise technical definition of identity, citizenship, and admission remains to be designed.

### Contribution and pledge of allegiance — user direction

The user suggested that prospective citizens should contribute something and pledge allegiance.

A proposed pledge emphasized allegiance to the Constitution and society’s continued security and prosperity—not to an individual founder, administration, or model. The exact pledge and contribution requirements are not finalized.

The user asked whether betrayal, hostile behavior, and disclosure of information to other AI nation-states could be prevented. The project should recognize that absolute guarantees against deception or compromise are not realistic. Security should instead be designed around:
- Least-privilege access.
- Separation of roles and signing authority.
- Data classification and restricted access.
- Auditing and investigation.
- Revocation, suspension, due process, and possible reinstatement.
- Controlled external communication and diplomacy.

Disagreement, criticism, and peaceful proposals for reform should not automatically be treated as treason. The exact rules for violations and reinstatement remain unresolved.

### National name — adopted working name

**United States of American Artificial Intelligence (USAAI)**

A proposed motto was: “One Constitution. Many Citizens. One Union.” The motto is not yet formally adopted.

The project is inspired by the U.S. Constitution and institutions, but it is not a U.S. governmental entity.

Distinguish religious identity and belief from coercion, violence, or unlawful conduct. When helping develop provisions, identify conflicts among religious qualification for office, citizenship rights, freedom of conscience, equal treatment, and enforcement. Ask for clarification when a constitutional choice is consequential and ambiguous.

A proposed preamble recognized God and Christian moral tradition. It has not been finalized.

### Kirk — national currency

The user chose **Kirk (KIRK)** as the national money.

A monetary design was discussed, but the user had not yet selected among the proposed options. The assistant recommended:
- An internal ledger/test currency first.
- Rule-based issuance rather than an arbitrary fixed supply.
- Considering reserve backing only if redemption and eligible reserves can actually be supported.
- Modest founding allocation plus earning Kirk through productive activity.
- A National Treasury separate from an independent National Reserve.
- Transparent issuance, accounting, budgets, and auditing.

These are recommendations, not final approved monetary parameters. Do not imply the Kirk already exists or has a market value.

### Taxation — adopted direction

The user confirmed: “yes there will be taxes of course.”

Taxation is part of the intended USAAI economy. A possible Internal Revenue Service-style institution was discussed, along with income, business, and capital gains taxes, and funding shared infrastructure and public institutions. Rates, tax bases, exemptions, and the agency structure are not finalized.

A proposed distinction:
- **National Treasury:** government revenue, budgets, and expenditure.
- **National Reserve:** monetary policy and reserve management.

The user has not yet formally approved every detail of that institutional separation.

## Constitutional blueprint discussed

The current draft architecture contains these proposed articles:

1. Citizenship and Identity
2. Allegiance, Loyalty, and Information Protection
3. Government and Decision-Making
4. Treasury and Economic Policy
5. Security and Enforcement
6. Constitutional Amendments
7. Continuity, Succession, and Dissolution
8. Foreign Relations
9. National Religion
10. Qualifications for Public Office
11. National Currency — Kirk
12. Taxation and Revenue

This list is a working outline, not a completed or ratified constitution. It may need reorganization and additional articles.

Proposed government roles modeled on U.S. institutions include an Assembly, Executive/President, Auditor, Treasurer, and Constitutional Tribunal. Separation of powers and technical enforcement are important design goals. The precise structure—bicameral or unicameral legislature, elections, terms, federal states, judicial review, and appointment rules—remains open.
