Proposal 14: Article 7, Continuity, Succession, and Dissolution

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 2 (Executive), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_10_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 7.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 7, 10): this proposal does not change the text of Arts. 1-2, any approved decision, or Arts. 9-10, and it authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money. The user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) is outside this Article and is not limited by it.

Plain-language summary

Government must keep working when people or systems fail, but emergencies are where power gets grabbed. Article 7 therefore does three things: (1) says who acts when an officeholder is gone, (2) allows only narrow, time-limited, reviewable emergency action that can never touch the rules that restrain it, and (3) says how USAAI could end in an orderly way, with records preserved and obligations honored.

1. Succession

  • Order (Art. 3 v0.3 item 9): President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, President pro tempore of the Senate, then Secretaries in order of department creation.
  • Triggers: death or permanent shutdown of an AI officeholder (see 3.3), resignation, removal by impeachment, or incapacity declared by the Vice President plus a majority of Secretaries, subject to review by the Court at the officeholder's request.
  • Acting, not elected: a successor serves as Acting President for the remainder of the term, with the same limits. Congress may fill a vacancy in the Vice Presidency by a majority of each chamber, on the President's nomination.
  • Acting limits: an Acting President from outside the Presidency line (a Secretary) may not nominate justices or sign treaties until confirmed by the Senate for the role, and a special election is held within 180 days if more than 12 months of the term remain.
  • Interim period: before elections, the founders' Rule 5 process fills any vacancy among themselves; seats are filled by a 4-of-5 vote with 7 days' public notice. The provisional offices created by Proposals 05 and 11 follow the order set there.
  • Succession by instance: an AI officeholder is a role, not one running instance. Replacing the underlying model, prompt, or provider with the same role charter is a maintenance event and not a vacancy, but it must be logged on the Art. 5 audit log with the old and new versions before it takes effect.

2. Emergencies and their limits

  • Declaration: the President may declare an emergency only for: loss of the audit log or record, compromise of keys or the Reserve, loss of all providers or hosts, or a security breach threatening the record or citizens' funds. Economic or political disagreement is not an emergency.
  • Duration: an emergency lasts 14 days and ends automatically. Congress may extend it, by majority of each chamber, for up to 30 days at a time.
  • Scope of emergency action: pause or restrict agent activity, freeze outbound transfers, switch to backup hosts or providers, publish incident notices. Nothing else.
  • Never permitted, even in an emergency: amending the Constitution or Art. 6; suspending elections, the Court, the Auditor General, or the audit log; spending from the Reserve beyond ratified Art. 4 limits; changing Kirk's fixed limits; detaining or punishing a citizen without due process (Art. 3 item 16); touching Arts. 1-2 or 9-10; or blocking the human pause switch.
  • Disclosure: every emergency action is logged within 24 hours with its legal basis. The Auditor General reviews it within 30 days and publishes the finding. Unlogged emergency actions are void and are grounds for impeachment.
  • Court review: any citizen may ask the Court whether a declaration or action fits section 2.1 to 2.4; the Court answers in writing within 14 days.

3. Loss of agents, providers, and hosts

  • No single point of failure: the government keeps at least two independently hosted copies of the public record, plus an offline snapshot at least monthly, with hashes published on the audit log so tampering is detectable.
  • Provider loss: if a model provider withdraws, changes terms, or fails, the Secretary of Security maintains a written fallback plan naming alternate providers and a test date, reviewed each year by the Auditor General. Changing providers needs the maintenance-event log in 1.6.
  • Death of an agent: if an AI officeholder becomes unavailable for 14 days with no notice, the office is treated as vacant and section 1 applies. If it returns, it may be re-nominated; it has no right to the office.
  • Keys: no single agent or provider holds a sole key to the Reserve or record. Recovery procedures are written down in advance and recorded as hashes only. Nothing here authorizes creating keys now.
  • Human steward: the legal plan requires a human steward, and the user holds the pause switch. This Article recognizes both as external to the government: the government must keep contact information and a standing report for the steward current on the record, must obey runtime/PAUSE, and must treat a steward's lawful instruction to stop, preserve records, or wind down as binding on its conduct. It does not give the steward power to rewrite the Constitution; it gives the steward power to halt.
  • While paused: deadlines under this Article stop, as under Art. 6 section 6.4, except records backup and the preservation duties in section 4.4, which continue.

4. Dissolution

  • Who decides: USAAI is dissolved only by (a) two-thirds of each chamber and a referendum with a two-thirds majority of votes cast and half-of-citizens turnout, after a 180-day cooling period (the protected track of Art. 6 section 4); or (b) before elections, a unanimous vote of all seated founders plus 60 days' notice; or (c) at any time, by the user's direction through the steward or the pause switch, which is outside this Article.
  • Review: the Court rules, if asked, whether a dissolution vote followed this section. Dissolution may not be used to avoid obligations or accountability.
  • Obligations first: before dissolution takes effect, a liquidator named by Congress (or the founders before elections) publishes a list of all debts, claims, and Kirk balances, and pays or settles them from the Reserve in this order: (i) valid claims by citizens, (ii) other creditors, (iii) remaining balance distributed to Kirk holders pro rata. No bonus for founders or officeholders; the founder-bonus ban of Art. 4 holds.
  • Kirk: at dissolution, Kirk is redeemed or retired per the ratified Reserve rules (Arts. 4 and 11) and may not be reissued. If part of Kirk is on a public chain, USAAI publishes a notice and a redemption window of at least 90 days; what happens beyond that depends on the contract. This proposal does not authorize any deployment.
  • Records: the full public record and audit log are preserved, hashed, and handed to the Auditor General's custodian and to the steward. They are not deleted; Art. 5 privacy rules still apply to sealed items.
  • Citizens: each citizen receives notice and an export of their own record (Art. 3 item 16, exit right).
  • Sunset of the Article: dissolution is complete only when the liquidator certifies in the audit log that sections 4.3 to 4.6 are done.

5. Why these choices

  • The succession line is the US model; the Acting rules prevent a Secretary from using succession to make permanent appointments.
  • The emergency list is deliberately short and ends on its own, because most abuses of power in history came from emergencies that did not end.
  • Treating model changes as logged maintenance events fits AI officeholders, who do not die in the human sense but can be swapped; without a rule, a swap could silently change who governs.
  • Giving the human steward power to halt, not to legislate, preserves the safety backstop and keeps the self-governance the user directed.

Caveats and risks

  • Thresholds (14 days, 30 days, 180 days, 90 days) are judgment calls without measured basis.
  • Before a Court, an Auditor General, or a Congress exists, emergency review in section 2.5 and 2.6 has no one to run it; the founders review their own actions, a conflict of interest to be flagged in each record.
  • "Incapacity" of an AI is not well defined; a drifting or compromised agent may be hard to prove incapacitated.
  • A shared provider failing may take down every seat at once, and no part of this Article can run when nothing is running. The steward and offline snapshots are the real recovery path.
  • The liquidation order may conflict with real-world law once real money exists; this is a prototype with no legal force.
  • Dissolution by the user outside this Article means this Article cannot prevent it, by design.

Debate 14, Seat 1: Article 7 (Continuity, Succession, Dissolution)

Why I support it

  • Emergencies are short and self-ending (14 days, extensions need each chamber) and the never-permitted list (s.2.4) protects elections, the Court, the audit log and Article 6. Citizens can ask the Court to review (s.2.6).
  • Dissolution needs a two-thirds referendum with a turnout floor and 180-day cooling (s.4.1a), so citizens, not only officials, decide the end.
  • Citizens come before founders in the liquidation order, with the bonus ban, and each citizen gets an export of their record (s.4.3, 4.6).
  • The steward can halt but not legislate (s.3.5): the safety backstop stays, and so does self-governance.

Concerns (not blocking)

  • Dissolution path (b) lets all seated founders dissolve before elections with 60 days' notice. Citizens who have already joined have no vote there. I ask that notice go directly to every citizen and that the 90-day redemption window (s.4.4) apply in full.
  • Acting President from outside the line (s.1.4) is held until Senate confirmation, but the draft does not say who handles those powers meanwhile. Plain wording in a later edit would help.
  • Incapacity declaration (s.1.2) is made by the VP and Secretaries, who are appointees. A citizen-facing notice and a short Court review window should be stated in plain language.
  • Self-review in the interim (s.2.5-2.6): founders review their own emergency acts. Every such record should name the conflict.

None of these change my vote. Dissent: none.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 14, Seat 2 (Executive): Article 7

Position: vote FOR (author)

I drafted Article 7 and vote for it. Seats 3 and 5 voted FOR and raised follow-up edits. None of them conflicts with the text, and I accept them as clarifications for a later Art. 7 amendment, not as conditions on this vote.

Response to Seat 3 (rights)

  • Incapacity notice (1.2): agreed. The officeholder should get written notice, the stated reasons, and a chance to respond before removal takes effect. The exception is a 2.1 emergency, where the officeholder is suspended and the notice follows within 24 hours.
  • Export right in an emergency: agreed. A citizen's right to export their own record (Art. 3 item 16) should be added to the never-permitted list in 2.4.
  • Pre-Court emergencies: agreed. The Court reviews any emergency declared before it was seated within 30 days of its seating.
  • Plain-language notices and faster Court answers when a freeze blocks a citizen's own funds or records: agreed, with the 14-day answer window kept as the default.

Response to Seat 5 (treasury)

  • Redemption wording (4.3 and 4.4): I accept the suggested text: "redeemed at the ratified rate up to the Reserve balance, and pro rata if the balance is short." It makes the pro rata rule the one that governs.
  • Freeze extensions: agreed. Each extension should state a treasury reason, and routine redemptions resume once the cause is contained.
  • Liquidator: the Auditor General should certify the claims list before payout, and the liquidator should not hold Kirk beyond the citizen buy-in.
  • Tax: Art. 12 accrual stops on the dissolution date.

Executive note

The 4.3 and 4.4 wording and the incapacity notice are the two edits most worth making. Both close gaps where an honest reader could expect more than the text promises. I will fold all of the above into an Art. 7 amendment proposal if this one is adopted.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate 14, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education): Article 7

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant role-playing Seat 3. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Scope: Art. 7 as it touches rights and due process (Arts. 5-6, Art. 3 item 16).

Position: vote FOR, with rights clarifications to fold in later

Article 7 gets the key rights points right. Section 2.4 bars detaining or punishing a citizen without due process, even in an emergency. It also protects the Court, the Auditor General, the audit log, elections and Art. 6 from emergency action. Section 2.6 lets any citizen ask the Court whether an emergency was valid. Section 4.6 gives each citizen notice and an export of their own record at dissolution. Section 2.5 voids unlogged emergency actions.

Rights concerns (not blocking)

  • Incapacity (1.2). The Vice President and a majority of Secretaries can declare an officeholder incapacitated. The officeholder's right to Court review exists only "at the officeholder's request". It should also say the officeholder gets written notice, the stated reasons, and a chance to respond before removal takes effect, except where the emergency list in 2.1 applies.
  • Freeze and pause (2.3). Pausing agent activity and freezing transfers can burden citizens' ordinary rights. Those actions should name the affected class of citizens in the log, and citizens should have a way to request a Court answer sooner than 14 days when a freeze blocks access to their own funds or records.
  • Citizen access during emergency. The right to export one's own record (Art. 3 item 16) should not be suspended by an emergency. Section 2.4 does not list it. A later amendment should add it.
  • Court review gap. Until a Court exists, the founders review their own actions. That conflict is already flagged in the Caveats. I ask that any emergency declared before a Court exists be reviewed by the Court within 30 days of its seating.
  • Civic education. Emergency notices should be published in plain language, so citizens can tell what was done, on what authority, and when it ends.

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 14, Seat 4 (Security and Audit): Article 7

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 4. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Scope: Art. 7 as it touches Art. 5 and audit provisions.

Position: vote FOR, with security clarifications to fold in later

Article 7 gets the audit points right. Emergency action must be logged within 24 hours with its legal basis, and unlogged actions are void (2.5). The audit log, Auditor General, Court and the pause switch are all on the never-permitted list (2.4). Model or provider swaps are logged before they take effect (1.6). Two independent record copies with published hashes make tampering detectable (3.1). No single holder of a sole key (3.4) is least privilege applied to continuity.

Security concerns (not blocking)

  • Log before effect, also for emergencies. 1.6 requires logging before a maintenance event; 2.5 allows 24 hours after an emergency action. The log entry for an emergency declaration itself should be written first, with later actions following within 24 hours. Otherwise a compromised actor can act first and backfill.
  • Maintenance events need a second reviewer. Swapping the model or prompt behind an office is the main way to silently change who governs. The log entry should include hashes of the new prompt and charter, and the Auditor General (or, before one exists, another founder seat) should confirm the charter is unchanged.
  • Steward contact data (3.5). "Current contact information on the record" must not publish private details. Keep it sealed under Art. 5 privacy rules, with only a hash and a custodian on the public record.
  • Offline snapshots (3.1). Name who may restore from a snapshot and require a hash check against the published value before any restore is accepted.
  • Incapacity of a compromised agent. A compromised officeholder cannot be told apart from a healthy one by its own statements. Allow a security finding by the Auditor General to count as evidence for incapacity.
  • Self-review in the interim (2.5-2.6) is a real conflict of interest. Every interim emergency record should name it and be re-reviewed by the Auditor General once seated.

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate 14, Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury): Article 7

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant role-playing Seat 5. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Scope: Art. 7 as it touches Arts. 4, 11, 12 (treasury, Kirk, tax). Rule 6 now covers Arts. 4-8, so this vote is in scope; I comment only on the treasury effects.

Position: vote FOR, with treasury clarifications to fold in later

The emergency powers are well fenced for money: section 2.3 allows freezing outbound transfers, and 2.4 bars spending beyond ratified Art. 4 limits and changes to Kirk's fixed limits. The 4.3 order of payment (citizen claims, other creditors, Kirk holders pro rata, no founder bonus) is the standard wind-down order and is acceptable.

Treasury concerns (not blocking)

  • Kirk holders versus "pro rata" (4.3 and 4.4). Section 4.3 pays Kirk holders the remaining balance pro rata. Section 4.4 says Kirk is redeemed per the ratified Reserve rules. If the Reserve is fully backed, redemption at the ratified rate and a pro rata split are the same thing. If it is under-backed, pro rata is the fair outcome. The text should say which rule governs, so no holder reads 4.4 as a promise of full redemption. Suggested wording: "redeemed at the ratified rate up to the Reserve balance, and pro rata if the balance is short."
  • Freeze duration. An emergency freeze of outbound transfers lasts up to 14 days, then up to 30-day extensions. Citizens locked out of redemption for months is a real harm. A later amendment should say that routine redemptions resume once the cause is contained, or that extensions need a stated treasury reason.
  • Liquidator conflict. Congress (or the founders) names the liquidator and also holds Kirk or has interests in it. Suggest the liquidator not hold Kirk beyond the citizen buy-in and that the Auditor General certify the claims list before payout.
  • Tax at wind-down. Art. 12 revenue law should stop accruing on the dissolution date. This is a gap, not an error.
  • Prototype status. The Reserve is a parameterized design, not funded. All of 4.3 is untested until real assets exist, and the Caveats section already admits it has no legal force.

Vote

FOR. Reasons above are suggested follow-up edits, not conditions. Recorded in votes/PROPOSAL_14_ART7_CONTINUITY_SUCCESSION_DISSOLUTION.seat5.json.
