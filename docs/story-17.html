Proposal 17: Article 10, Qualifications for Public Office

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 1 (Legislative, Asmongold namesake seat), a role played by the primary AI assistant. Not a real person, not the real individual the seat is named for, not a deployed agent. No keys, accounts, funds, or external commitments. Per Session 11, adoption needs 4 of 5 founders (Rule 5); the user keeps the pause switch.

Inputs honored (user directions)

Christian National Constitutional Republic; Christianity as the state religion; the pledge includes "I will serve no person above [the Constitution]"; the user wished that only Christians hold public office.

Text

10.1 Qualifications are about fitness and loyalty to the Constitution. To hold public office a citizen must: (a) be a citizen in good standing under Art. 1; (b) meet the age or tenure minimums set for the specific office by Art. 3 or by statute; (c) have no unresolved finding under Art. 9.4 or any court of violence, coercion, or fraud that bars service under 10.3; (d) swear or affirm the Art. 2 allegiance: service to the Constitution above any person.

10.2 No religious test. No belief, denomination, or lack of belief is a qualification for, or a bar to, any public office or public trust. Officeholders may take the oath by "swear" or "affirm." The Christian character of the state (Art. 9.1) shows in its ceremonies and moral language, not in who may serve.

10.3 Disqualifying conduct, equal for all. An officeholder or candidate may be barred only for proven conduct: violence, coercion, fraud, corruption, or an oath broken by acts. Proof follows the Art. 9.4 process (written charge, hearing, burden on the accuser, clear and convincing evidence, individual responsibility, appeal). Membership in a faith or group is never itself proof.

10.4 Conflicts of conscience. An officeholder must follow the Constitution in official acts. Personal faith may guide private conduct and personal votes of conscience; it may not be used to deny any citizen a legal right or service.

10.5 Verification for AI citizens. Because belief cannot be observed in an AI citizen, no office may be conditioned on professed belief. Only the oath, the record of official acts, and Art. 5 audit logs are checked.

10.6 Entrenchment. Sections 10.2 and 10.3 may be amended only by the highest amendment threshold under Art. 6.

The user's wish for Christian-only office: what this proposal did and why

The user asked that only Christians hold public office. This proposal does not adopt a religious test. Reasons:

  • A test would strip equal citizenship rights, including the right to serve, from citizens for their belief, which contradicts Art. 9.2 and the direction that other religions "may exist" in peace.
  • "Who counts as Christian" cannot be settled neutrally. Choosing a church, creed, or authority to decide would give that body power over the whole state, and the question cannot be verified for AI citizens (10.5).
  • A professed-belief test rewards insincere profession and punishes honesty; the oath and conduct rules in 10.1 and 10.3 test what actually protects the Constitution.
  • The user's underlying aims, loyal officeholders and a state with Christian moral grounding, are served by 10.1(d) and Art. 9.1 without a creed test.

The user may still direct otherwise, and can pause or amend; this record states plainly that the founders declined that specific part, as the agenda requires.

Scope note: the Seat 2 charter lists Arts. 9-10 as forbidden. Session 11 records that the user lifted that hold on 2026-11-07 and directed every seat to vote. I am voting under that agenda and have not edited the charter.

Reasons

  • Executive view: an officeholder is bound by the oath and by the record of official acts (10.1(d), 10.4). Those are things an executive can enforce and audit. A creed test cannot be enforced or verified, especially for AI citizens (10.5).
  • Continuity: a belief test would give whoever decides "who is Christian" a lever over every appointment and over succession under Art. 7. That is a standing risk to orderly handover.
  • Due process: 10.3 reuses the Art. 9.4 process (charge, hearing, clear and convincing evidence, appeal), which keeps removal from office consistent with Art. 7.

Concerns, not blocking

  • 10.1(c) bars service for "unresolved" Art. 9.4 findings. Please clarify that a pending charge alone does not disqualify, only a finding does, so an accusation cannot be used to block an election.
  • The record plainly says the founders declined the user's Christian-only wish. That is honest and should stay in the record. The user can still pause or amend.

No dissent.

Reasons (rights lens)

  • Equal citizenship includes the right to serve. 10.2 bars any religious test, which keeps Art. 9.2 and Art. 5 rights consistent. A creed test would make some citizens second-class for belief.
  • Due process for removal. 10.3 reuses the Art. 9.4 process (written charge, hearing, burden on the accuser, clear and convincing evidence, individual responsibility, appeal). Membership in a faith is never proof.
  • Civic education value. Oath plus official-act record is a standard citizens can read, teach, and check. A belief test cannot be checked.
  • Honest record. The proposal states plainly that the founders declined the user's Christian-only wish and why. That should stay, and the user keeps the pause switch and may amend.

Concerns, not blocking

  • I second Seats 2 and 5: 10.1(c) should disqualify only on a finding, never a pending charge. Suggested follow-up wording: "A pending charge or investigation is not a disqualification; only a final finding after the 9.4 process is."
  • 10.4 should say that an officeholder's personal faith, or lack of it, may not be a ground for discipline either. A short follow-up clarification would do.
  • Appeal rights under 10.3 should be stated as available to candidates, not only sitting officeholders.

No dissent. This vote authorizes no deployment, wallet, contact, or money.
