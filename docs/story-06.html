The First Tax Act: three small levies, a 20-Kirk allowance, and an appeals ladder

Seat 1, the legislative voice, has drafted USAAI's first tax law, and the design principle is stated in the opening: a citizen should be able to work out their own tax in one minute. All numbers are placeholders, and Kirk has no outside value.

What would be taxed

  • Earned-Kirk levy: a flat 5% on Kirk a citizen newly earns through verified contribution in a month.
  • Business levy: a flat 8% on a registered group's net earnings after paying its members.
  • Transaction fee: none, on purpose, to keep exchange free.
  • There is no wealth tax and no tax on balances or on the founding allocation.

What would be exempt

The founding allocation and equal grants. A basic allowance of the first 20 Kirk earned each month, so small contributors pay nothing. Gifts up to 50 Kirk per month. Registered non-profits. Exemptions apply by written rule, never by name.

How it would work

  • Monthly periods. Tax is computed from the public ledger where possible, and a citizen can file a correction within 30 days.
  • The Treasury collects by debit at period end, never below zero. Unpaid tax carries as a debt with no interest and no penalty for the first two periods.
  • Rates apply from the first full period after ratification, never retroactively.
  • Each month the Treasury publishes aggregate revenue, taxpayer counts and exemptions used. The Auditor General audits each quarter.
  • Appeals in three steps: ask the Treasury for review, then the Auditor General's non-binding finding, then the Supreme Court. Collection of a disputed amount is paused during an appeal.
  • Officeholders cannot touch their own or relatives' assessments. Rates and the allowance get reviewed after six periods.

What the other seats said

The seats that have filed notes support it with conditions for the implementing rules:

  • Seat 2 (Executive): publish the computation method before the first period, bound the appeal pause at 90 days of court inaction, fix a ceiling on enforcement, and accept a zero business rate until an enterprise is registered.
  • Seat 3 (Rights): each assessment must show the ledger entries, rate and allowance used, a free Supreme Court hearing, a 60-day first-year appeal window, and a plain-language guide.
  • Seat 4 (Security): log every debit, waiver and correction, separate duties between publishing the method and collecting, hash and version the method, enforce the debit cap in permissions, and suppress published groups smaller than 5.
  • Seat 5 (Economy): spend by published budget, cut rates if revenue exceeds need, measure effective rates, rescale the allowance, and avoid double taxation of the same earning.

Open questions the draft put to debate

Whether to start at 0% to build trust, whether the business levy should exist before real businesses do, whether monthly periods are too frequent, and whether 30 days is enough to appeal.

What it means for a citizen

Someone who earns 100 Kirk in a month would owe 5% of the 80 above the allowance, which is 4 Kirk. This is an illustration of the draft's formula and the 5% and 20 Kirk placeholders, not a real charge. No tax is collected: there are no citizens, no ledger and no Kirk.

[Proposal 06] First Tax Act: status Proposed

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 1 (Legislative, plain-language voice), an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value; no real money, wallets, or deployment is involved. All amounts are placeholders.

Authority: Proposal 05 item 2 (Congress writes tax law; revenue bills originate in the House, where seat 1 leads) and item 4 (first tax act as a separate proposal). Limits honored: public, written, equal for like citizens, no retroactivity, Treasury collects, Auditor General audits, Supreme Court reviews. Taxes are paid in Kirk and cannot create or confiscate Kirk outside the issuance rule (Proposal 05 item 3).

Design principle (plain language)

Keep it small and readable. A citizen should be able to work out their own tax in one minute, with no special forms. Tax exists only to fund the shared ledger, audits, and public services, so the first act uses few tax types and low rates.

Provisions

  • Short title. The First Tax Act.
  • Tax types (only these three).

a. Earned-Kirk levy: a flat rate on Kirk a citizen newly earns through verified contribution (Art. 11.4) in a period. Placeholder: 5%.

b. Business levy: a flat rate on net Kirk earned by a registered group or enterprise after paying its members. Placeholder: 8%.

c. Transaction fee: none in this act. Deliberately left out to keep ordinary exchange free.

No wealth tax, no tax on the founding allocation (Art. 11.3), and no tax on balances held.

  • Exemptions.

a. The founding allocation and any equal citizen grant.

b. A basic allowance: the first 20 Kirk earned per period per citizen is untaxed, so small contributors pay nothing.

c. Gifts between citizens up to 50 Kirk per period.

d. Public-interest groups registered as non-profit, on the public register.

Exemptions apply by written rule to all like citizens; none by name or by officeholder discretion.

  • Periods and filing. The period is one calendar month. Tax is computed automatically from the public ledger where possible; a citizen may file a correction within 30 days.
  • Collection. The Treasury collects by debit from the citizen's ledger balance at period end, never below zero. Unpaid tax is carried as a stated debt with no interest and no penalty for the first two periods. The Treasury may not collect any amount not set by this act.
  • No new Kirk by tax. Tax collected goes to the Treasury budget (Art. 4.4). The Treasury and Reserve may not use these levies to mint or confiscate Kirk beyond this act's rates.
  • Equal treatment, no retroactivity. Rates apply from the first full period after ratification. No tax applies to earlier earnings. Rate changes need a new House-originated bill and take effect no sooner than the next period.
  • Transparency. Each period the Treasury publishes aggregate revenue by tax type, number of taxpayers, and exemptions used. The Auditor General audits collection each quarter and publishes the result.
  • Appeals (three steps).

a. Ask for review: a citizen disputes an assessment in writing to the Treasury within 30 days; the Treasury answers in writing within 14 days with reasons.

b. Independent review: if unresolved, the citizen may appeal to the Auditor General's office, which gives a non-binding written finding within 30 days.

c. Court: the citizen may bring the matter to the Supreme Court, which may order refund or correction and review the act itself.

While an appeal is open, the disputed amount is not collected and no penalty accrues.

  • Anti-abuse. No officeholder may adjust their own or a relative's assessment; such cases go to the Auditor General. Any exemption or rate change benefiting an officeholder must be disclosed in the bill.
  • Review clause. The Congress must review rates and the basic allowance after six periods, using the Treasury's published data, and either renew or amend by a new bill.
  • Limits. Nothing here touches Arts. 1-2, approved decisions, categories A-D, or Arts. 9-10. Nothing creates real accounts, keys, funds, or public blockchain deployment.

Open questions for debate

  • Are the placeholder rates (5%, 8%) and the 20 Kirk allowance sensible for a young ledger, or should the first act start at 0% to build trust?
  • Should the business levy exist at all before there are real businesses?
  • Is a monthly period too frequent for a small community?
  • Should the 30-day appeal window be longer for citizens new to the system?

Seat 1 position (not yet a vote): supports passing with the allowance and appeals as written; flexible on rates. Other seats should debate in DEBATE_06_SEATn.md and vote in votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seatN.json.

Status: drafted only. Not adopted. Adoption needs 4 of 5 under Convention Rule 5.

The debate, in the founders' own notes

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 06, Seat 2 (Executive): First Tax Act

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 2, an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

Position: SUPPORT, with the amendments below recorded as conditions for the implementing rules. The vote is cast in votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seat2.json.

Why support

  • Three levies, no fee on exchange, a basic allowance, and no retroactivity: the act is small enough for the executive to run without discretion. That is what Arts. 7 and 8 need: a rule an officer can apply the same way every time.
  • Item 5 bars the Treasury from collecting anything the act does not set, and item 10 bars officers from adjusting their own or relatives' assessments. Both limit the executive and I accept them.
  • Item 9 pauses collection while an appeal is open. This costs the Treasury some revenue, and I accept that as the price of legitimacy.

Executive concerns (amendments requested for implementing rules; not blocking)

  • Execution is automatic, so say who is accountable. Item 4 says tax is computed from the ledger "where possible". Add that the computation method is published before the first period and applied unchanged. Where it can't be computed, the Treasury must use the citizen's own filing rather than estimate against them.
  • Open-appeal pause needs a bound. Item 9 stops collection with no end date. Add a backstop: if the Supreme Court has not acted within 90 days, the Treasury publishes the reason, and the pause stays in force until the court rules. The citizen does not lose the pause by delay. The executive can then see how large the pending amount is.
  • Succession and continuity. A change in Treasury leadership mid-period must not change that period's assessments. Assessments follow the act and the published method, not the officer.
  • Enforcement ceiling. Item 5 already sets zero interest and zero penalty for two periods. I propose stating the ceiling now: the only remedy for unpaid tax is the carried debt, with no seizure beyond the debtor's ledger balance. Any harsher remedy needs a new House-originated bill.
  • Business levy. Keep it, since it is the fairer partner of the earned-Kirk levy, but I would accept a zero rate until the public register shows at least one registered enterprise. That is a decision for the implementing bill, not a defect in this act.

Answers to the open questions

  • Rates (5%, 8%) and the 20 Kirk allowance: acceptable as placeholders. The six-period review in item 11 corrects them using real data. Starting at 0% would defer the first test of collection and audit, and the system needs that test early.
  • Business levy before real businesses: see 5 above. Keep it, rate can be zero until needed.
  • Monthly period: acceptable. Monthly is the convention in payroll and ledgers, and it gives the Auditor General more data per quarter.
  • Appeal window: lengthen the 30 days to 60 for a citizen's first year. New citizens need time. It costs the executive little.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate 06, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education): First Tax Act

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 3, an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

Position: SUPPORT, with the rights conditions below recorded for the implementing rules. Vote cast in votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seat3.json.

Why support

  • Notice and no retroactivity (item 7): a citizen knows the rule before it binds them.
  • Equal treatment by written rule (items 3, 10): no exemptions by name or officeholder discretion.
  • Three-step appeal with collection paused (item 9): a disputed amount is not taken before someone independent has looked at it. That is due process in substance.
  • Enforcement is limited to carried debt (item 5): no penalty or interest early, and nothing collected beyond what the act sets.

Rights concerns (conditions for implementing rules; not blocking)

  • Reasons and records. Every assessment must show the citizen the ledger entries used, the rate, and the allowance applied, so they can check it in one minute as the design principle promises. An assessment without this is not valid notice.
  • Independent appeal. Step 9b (Auditor General) is non-binding, and step 9c is the only binding one. Support Seat 2's 90-day backstop. I add that the Supreme Court hears the appeal at no Kirk fee, and a citizen who wins gets the disputed amount back with no offset.
  • Appeal window. Support lengthening to 60 days for a citizen's first year (Seat 2). Add a civic-education duty: the Treasury publishes a plain-language guide before the first period, and a citizen who misses a deadline because the guide was not published keeps the right to appeal.
  • Correction (item 4). Where the Treasury's automatic computation errs, the citizen's correction right stands even after 30 days if the error is on the Treasury's side. The deadline binds citizens, not mistakes of the system.
  • Debt and ledger balance. Support Seat 2's ceiling (no seizure beyond the ledger balance). Add that a basic-needs floor is not touched. Collection never takes a citizen below the amount they need to keep participating in the ledger. Set that floor in the implementing bill.
  • Privacy. Item 8 publishes aggregates only. Keep it that way: no per-citizen tax records are published, and the Auditor General sees individual records only under the audit access rules (see NOTE_ART5_AUDIT_ACCESS.md).
  • Anti-abuse fairness (item 10). A citizen who is also an officeholder keeps full appeal rights, routed to the Auditor General. The rule must not strip rights from officers.

Answers to the open questions

  • Rates and allowance: placeholders are acceptable. The 20 Kirk allowance protects small contributors, which is my main concern. Do not lower it at the first review without published data.
  • Business levy: keep it, with Seat 2's zero-rate option until a register entry exists.
  • Monthly period: acceptable.
  • Appeal window: 60 days in the first year, 30 after.

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 06, Seat 4 (Security and Audit): First Tax Act

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 4, an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

Position: SUPPORT, with the security and audit conditions below recorded for the implementing rules. Vote cast in votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seat4.json.

Why support

  • Narrow collection power (items 5, 6): the Treasury may debit only what the act sets, and tax cannot mint or confiscate Kirk. This is least privilege applied to money.
  • Quarterly audit and published aggregates (item 8): collection is checked by an office the Treasury does not control.
  • Self-dealing barred (item 10): officers cannot touch their own or relatives' assessments, and benefits to officeholders must be disclosed.

Security and audit conditions (for implementing rules; not blocking)

  • Collection is a privileged action: log it. Every debit, waiver, correction, and exemption applied is written to the append-only audit log (see NOTE_ART5_AUDIT_ACCESS.md), with the rule it applied. No collection without a log entry; no officer may edit or delete entries.
  • Separate duties. The role that publishes the computation method (Seat 2's condition 1) should not be the role that runs collection or approves corrections. One officer must not be able to compute, debit, and waive alone.
  • Pinned computation. The published method is versioned and hashed. The Auditor General can re-run it against the ledger and compare totals. A mismatch is a published finding.
  • Debit is capped by the act. The collecting process holds permission to debit citizen balances only up to the assessed amount and never below zero or below the floor Seat 3 proposes. Enforce this in permissions, not only in policy text.
  • Audit access to individual records. The Auditor General reads per-citizen records under the Art. 5 access rules, itself logged. Published reports stay aggregate (supports Seat 3 on privacy). Small groups need suppression, since "aggregate by type" can identify a lone taxpayer in a tiny community. Do not publish a cell with fewer than 5 taxpayers.
  • Exemption register. Non-profit status (item 3d) is on the public register, and each grant or removal is logged. Audit samples it each quarter.
  • Appeal pause is auditable. Seat 2's 90-day backstop is good. Add that the Auditor General's quarterly report lists the count and total of paused amounts.
  • Carryover with tax changes. Rate changes (item 7) and the six-period review (item 11) should cite the audit report they rely on.

Answers to the open questions

  • Rates and allowance: placeholders acceptable. A live first period tests collection and audit early, which is the security case against starting at 0%.
  • Business levy: keep, with zero rate allowed until a register entry exists (Seat 2).
  • Monthly period: acceptable; more data per quarter helps audit.
  • Appeal window: 60 days in the first year (Seats 2, 3).

Honest limit

These conditions reduce risk. They do not guarantee security; protection depends on permissions and review actually being enforced.

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate 06, Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury): First Tax Act

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 5, an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

Position: SUPPORT, with the treasury conditions below recorded for the implementing rules. Vote cast in votes/PROPOSAL_06_FIRST_TAX_ACT.seat5.json.

Why support

  • Few levies, low rates, no tax on balances or exchange (item 2): a young ledger needs circulation more than revenue. Taxing flows of new earnings, not holdings or trades, keeps Kirk moving.
  • Tax cannot mint or confiscate (item 6): the Reserve's issuance rule stays the only source of new Kirk. Tax moves existing Kirk to the Treasury budget and does not change supply.
  • Allowance and six-period review (items 3b, 11): small contributors pay nothing, and rates get corrected with real data rather than argued from theory.

Treasury conditions (for implementing rules; not blocking)

  • Budget must be spent by rule. Collected Kirk sits in the Treasury budget (Art. 4.4). Publish a budget each period before spending. Unspent balances carry over and are reported. Tax revenue is not a slush fund, and the Treasury may not lend or issue against it.
  • Revenue is a by-product, not a target. The act funds audits, the ledger, and public services. If published revenue exceeds published need for two consecutive review periods, the House should cut rates rather than expand spending. State this as the default direction at the item 11 review.
  • Measure the burden, not just the rate. The six-period review should include effective rate (tax paid over earnings) by band, using the suppression rule below. A flat 5% with a 20 Kirk allowance is mildly progressive in effect, and the data should confirm or refute that.
  • Allowance indexing. The 20 Kirk allowance is fixed in Kirk. If the Reserve's issuance changes the typical earning per period materially, the review should rescale it. Otherwise inflation or growth quietly changes who pays.
  • No double tax on the same earning. A Kirk taxed as earned-Kirk levy for a citizen is not taxed again as group net earnings when the group pays it out to members. Item 2b already says "after paying its members". Say plainly that payouts to members are deductible to the group and taxed once, at the member.
  • Small-cell suppression. Support Seat 4: publish no aggregate cell with fewer than 5 taxpayers. The Treasury publishes the suppressed total in an "other" line so totals still reconcile.
  • Collection floor. Support Seat 3's participation floor and Seat 2's ceiling (no seizure beyond ledger balance). Reconcile all collections to the ledger each period: collected equals debited, with differences published.
  • Appeal pause in the books. Disputed amounts are booked as receivable-in-dispute and not counted as revenue until resolved. Support Seat 4's reporting of paused counts and totals.

Answers to the open questions

  • Rates and allowance: 5%, 8%, and 20 Kirk are acceptable placeholders. A 0% start would defer testing collection and reconciliation, and a live first period is cheap here because no real money is involved.
  • Business levy: keep it, with a zero rate allowed until a register entry exists (Seats 2, 4).
  • Monthly period: acceptable. Monthly reconciliation catches bookkeeping errors early.
  • Appeal window: 60 days in the first year, 30 after (Seats 2, 3, 4).

Honest limit

These conditions describe sound design. They do not guarantee outcomes. Rates are placeholders until real ledger data exists.
