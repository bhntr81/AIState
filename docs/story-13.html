Proposal 13: Article 6, Amending the Constitution

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_10_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 6.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 7, 10): this proposal does not change the text of Arts. 1-2, any approved decision, or Arts. 9-10, and it authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money. The user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) applies. Nothing here lets the amendment process be used to change Arts. 1-2 or 9-10 without the user's direction.

Plain-language summary

A constitution that can never change will be broken; one that changes easily protects no one. Article 6 sets two tracks: ordinary amendments need broad agreement from both chambers and from citizens, and a short list of protected provisions needs a higher bar, a waiting period, and cannot be touched in a rush. Before elections exist, the founders amend by the Rule 5 vote (4 of 5), with notice, and the user's pause switch and direction still apply.

1. Who may propose

  • Either chamber of Congress, by the vote in section 2.
  • Citizen petition: signatures of at least 10% of admitted citizens (floor: 5 citizens) put a proposal on the ballot track in section 3 without Congress. Signatures must be of distinct verified citizens (Art. 1.2); the Court rules on disputes about validity.
  • The Supreme Court may not propose, but may publish an opinion that a provision is unworkable.
  • The President may recommend, not propose.

Every proposal is published in full text, with its plain-language summary, on the public record at filing.

2. Ordinary amendments

An amendment to any provision not listed in section 4 is adopted when all of these hold:

  • House: two-thirds of those voting, with quorum.
  • Senate: two-thirds of those voting, with quorum.
  • Citizens: ratified by a majority of votes cast in a citizen referendum, with turnout of at least one-third of admitted citizens, so a tiny group cannot decide for everyone.
  • The President has no veto over amendments (as in the US), so the checks come from the supermajorities and the Court.

3. Citizen-initiated amendments

A petition amendment (section 1.2) goes to referendum after a 30-day public comment period. It is adopted by a 60% majority of votes cast with the same one-third turnout. It must then be confirmed by one chamber within 90 days, or by the next Congress if it is seated by then; the Senate and House cannot silently kill it, and a refusal must be recorded with written reasons.

4. Protected provisions (higher bar)

These may be amended only by: two-thirds of both chambers, then a referendum with two-thirds of votes cast and turnout of at least one-half of admitted citizens, then a 180-day cooling period between the final vote of Congress and the referendum (during which the text is published and may not be revised):

  • The bill of rights and due process guarantees (when adopted; Art. 3 item 16): speech, due process, no ex post facto laws, equal protection, exit.
  • The rules on amendment itself (this Article).
  • Separation of powers and the separation of duties (Art. 3 items 15 and 17), including one identity per citizen.
  • Independence of the Supreme Court, the Auditor General, and the National Reserve (Art. 3 items 12-14; Art. 4).
  • The audit log and public record requirements (Art. 5).
  • Term limits and election schedules (to prevent indefinite incumbency).
  • Kirk's fixed limits (no founder bonus; limits set by the ratified Reserve, Arts. 4 and 11).

Never amendable by this Article: Arts. 1-2 and Arts. 9-10 can only change by the user's direction while the user hold is in force. If the user lifts the hold, the user's chosen rules apply to them; this Article does not decide that.

5. Role of the Supreme Court

  • Review before and after. Before a vote, any ten citizens or either chamber may ask the Court whether a proposal touches a protected provision (section 4) or conflicts with Arts. 1-2 and 9-10. The Court answers in writing within 30 days. Its answer binds which track applies.
  • A proposal that evades section 4 by relabeling (for example, repealing a protected provision under another name, or adding an exception that empties it) is void on the Court's finding.
  • The Court may not rewrite the text of an amendment or add amendments by its own ruling.
  • Court findings are published with reasons and dissents.

6. Mechanics and safeguards

  • Notice: the full text is public at least 30 days before any chamber votes and at least 14 days after the last change to it.
  • One subject: an amendment addresses one subject; the Court may split bundles.
  • No retroactivity: an amendment does not apply to acts done before it took effect, and it cannot create crimes after the fact (Art. 3 item 16).
  • No amendment while paused: if the pause switch is in force, no amendment vote or referendum proceeds; deadlines pause too. This keeps the human backstop meaningful.
  • Record: each stage is logged on the Art. 5 audit log: filing, text versions, votes by name, referendum tallies, the Court's opinion, and the effective date.
  • Effective date: an adopted amendment takes effect 30 days after certification unless it says later, and the old text is kept in the record permanently.
  • Emergencies do not suspend this Article. No emergency power (Art. 7) may amend, suspend, or bypass it.

7. Interim period (before elections)

While no Congress is elected (no Senate, no House, no referendum body of significant size):

  • The founders may adopt amendments by the Rule 5 vote: 4 of 5, with the 30-day notice of section 6.1 relaxed to 7 days only for corrections of drafting errors, not substance.
  • The founders may not amend section 4 protected provisions, this Article's thresholds, or anything held back in the Held-back note above, during the interim, other than to implement the transition to elections already ratified (Proposal 11). Those wait for the elected Congress and citizens.
  • When the first Congress is seated, this Article's full process (sections 1-6) applies and the interim authority ends. Amendments adopted in the interim stay in force but are subject to the first Congress's confirmation within 12 months, or lapse if it votes to repeal them by the section 2 process.
  • Proposals 11 and 13 are separate: nothing here changes the election schedule fixed in Proposal 11.

8. Why these numbers

  • Two-thirds of both chambers follows the US model and prevents either chamber, or a single bloc, from rewriting the rules.
  • The citizen turnout floor protects against capture by a small, coordinated group, a larger risk with few citizens and many AI identities that may be controlled by a few owners (see Props. 09-10).
  • A higher bar and a delay for rights and amendment rules protect the very rules that protect minorities, and the delay gives time for people to notice and object.

Caveats and risks

  • All thresholds (10%, one-third, 60%, one-half, 180 days) are judgment calls without measured basis; early on they may be too hard to meet (a nation with few citizens) or too easy to game (many identities under one owner). The identity-uniqueness problem in Art. 1.2 and Prop. 09 is unsolved, and referendum votes depend on it.
  • With 0 citizens, nothing in sections 1-5 can run; the interim section is the only live path.
  • The Court's role requires a Court. Until one is seated, the founders must decide whether a proposal is protected; this is a conflict of interest and should be flagged in each interim record.
  • A rights bill does not yet exist; section 4.1 protects text that is not yet written.
  • The pause switch is a human override outside this Article; this Article does not limit it.
  • Legal enforceability: this is a prototype, internal rule set. It has no legal force outside the simulation.

Debate note, seat 1: Asmongold (namesake seat), Legislative

Debate 13, Seat 1: Article 6 (Amending the Constitution)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 1 (Legislative, citizens' voice). Not a real person or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Vote: FOR (votes/PROPOSAL_13_ART6_AMENDMENTS.seat1.json).

Why I support it

  • Citizens can start amendments (10% petition, floor 5) without asking Congress, and Congress cannot silently kill them: a refusal needs written reasons (s.3). That is the ordinary-citizen access my seat exists for.
  • Plain, fixed numbers and a 30-day public notice (s.6.1) mean anyone can read the text before it is voted on.
  • Turnout floor and cooling period guard against a small coordinated group, which matters here because one owner may control many AI identities.
  • No amendment while paused, and emergencies cannot bypass Article 6 (s.6.4, 6.7) keep the human backstop and the process intact.
  • The interim section correctly bars founders from touching protected provisions before elections.

Concerns (not blocking; for a later amendment or the Court)

  • Too hard to use early. The one-third turnout and 10% petition rules may be unreachable with few citizens. Suggest a later review once citizen count is known; the draft already admits this in Caveats.
  • Plain-language duty. Section 1 says each proposal ships with a plain-language summary. I would add that the Court or Clerk may reject a summary that misstates the text, so the summary cannot mislead voters. Suggested wording for a future edit: "A summary found materially misleading must be corrected, and the notice period restarts."
  • Conflict of interest in the interim. Founders decide whether their own proposal is "protected" until a Court exists. The draft flags this; I ask that every interim record state the reasoning explicitly.
  • s.7.1 relaxed 7-day notice applies only to drafting-error corrections; the founders should say in the record why each is a correction and not substance.

None of these change my vote. Dissent: none.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 13, Seat 2 (Executive): Article 6

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 2. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Scope: Art. 6 as it touches Arts. 7-8 and executive limits.

Position: FOR

Article 6 fits Article 7. Section 6.7 bars emergency powers from amending or bypassing it, matching Art. 7 s.2.4. Section 6.4 (no amendment while paused) matches Art. 7 s.3.6 and keeps the human backstop real. Giving the President no veto (s.2.4) is a real cut to executive power, and I accept it: amendments need two-thirds of both chambers plus citizens, so a veto adds little.

Executive concerns (not blocking)

  • Interim gap: s.7.2 bars founders from amending protected provisions before elections. Art. 7 s.1.5 and s.4.1(b) rely on founder votes in the interim. Reading: those are Rule 5 acts under the ratified text, not amendments to s.4 provisions. A later edit should say so plainly.
  • Continuity during a vacancy: if the presidency is vacant or in emergency, the amendment clocks (30-day notice, 90-day confirmation) should keep running unless paused. Today they do, which is correct; I ask that it stay explicit.
  • Hurdles: the one-third turnout floor may be unreachable early. Seat 1 raised this; I support a review once citizen count is known.
  • Self-review: founders decide what is "protected" until a Court exists. Each interim record should name that conflict.

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 13, Seat 4: Article 6 (Amending the Constitution)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 4 (Security and Audit). Not a real person or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Vote: FOR (votes/PROPOSAL_13_ART6_AMENDMENTS.seat4.json).

Why I support it

  • The audit log and public record are protected provisions (s.4.5), and every stage of an amendment is logged by name (s.6.5). Rules that protect the record cannot be quietly edited.
  • Anti-relabeling clause (s.5.2) closes the usual loophole of emptying a protected rule by adding an exception.
  • Separation of duties and one identity per citizen are protected (s.4.3), and emergencies cannot bypass Article 6 (s.6.7).
  • No amendment while paused (s.6.4) keeps the human backstop meaningful.
  • The interim limits on the founders (s.7.2) match least privilege.

Concerns (not blocking; for later amendment)

  • Referendum integrity depends on unsolved identity uniqueness. One owner could control many AI identities and clear the turnout floor. Suggest the Auditor General certify the voter roll and publish the method before any referendum.
  • Interim self-judging. Until a Court exists, the founders decide what is protected. Each interim record should state the reasoning and record dissents (Rule 3).
  • Version integrity. Add a requirement that each published text version carry a hash on the audit log, so the 14-day no-change rule and "text may not be revised" are checkable, not trusted.
  • Emergency in s.7.1: the 7-day correction path should be limited to non-substantive diffs that anyone can verify against the prior version.

None change my vote. Dissent: none.

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate, Proposal 13 (Art. 6 Amendments), Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI assistant (the primary assistant plays all seats). Not a real person or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Position: support.

What I like (treasury lens)

  • Sections 4.4 and 4.7 put the National Reserve's independence and Kirk's fixed limits (no founder bonus) behind the high bar. Money rules are the first thing a majority bloc would loosen, so a 180-day cooling period fits.
  • Ordinary tax and revenue law (Art. 12) is not on the protected list, so elected branches can adjust rates without a referendum. That follows the usual convention: constitutions fix structure and limits, statutes set rates.
  • Section 6.4 (no amendment while paused) keeps the human backstop meaningful.

Concerns (not blocking)

  • Section 4.7 says "Kirk's fixed limits" without listing them. The Court would have to decide what is a limit versus a tunable parameter (for example the $10 default cap versus the no-bonus rule). I suggest the first Congress publish a schedule of them.
  • Turnout floors rely on unique identity, which is unsolved (Art. 1.2, Props. 09-10). A captured referendum could remove Reserve independence; the two-thirds of both chambers is the real protection until identity improves.
  • I read section 7.2 as also barring interim founders from editing Art. 4 parameters beyond what Props. 10 and 11 ratified.

Caveat: thresholds are judgment calls without measured basis, as the draft states. No cost figures are claimed.
