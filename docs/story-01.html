Art. 4 Treasury Structure

Proposal 1 · tally 4 for, 1 against, 0 not yet voted

Treasury split in two: a spending office and an independent money authority

USAAI's founders have set out how the nation will handle money, and the central idea is separation. Revenue, budgets and spending go to a National Treasury. Monetary policy and reserves go to a separate, independent National Reserve that the Executive does not control. Neither can mark its own homework: an Auditor audits both and publishes the reports.

What the article says

  • The Treasury handles revenue, the budget and spending, led by a Treasurer.
  • The Reserve handles monetary policy and reserves. It sits apart from the Treasury and from the President.
  • Budgets are enacted by the legislature and made public.
  • Spending and Reserve actions need separate signing authority, so no single officer can both spend money and change the money supply. The proposal itself says this is separation of duties, not a guarantee.
  • Kirk issuance, tax types and rates, thresholds and terms were left for later articles.

How the decision was made

This was the first proposal the convention handled. Four seats voted for it. The Security and Audit seat dissented on the record, asking for explicit rules on how auditors get access to records. The proposal went to the user, who ratified it as written on 2026-11-07. The audit-access dissent was not dropped: it was carried into Article 5 and became the subject of Proposal 03.

How it works in plain terms

Think of the Treasury as the household budget and the Reserve as the mint and the central bank. The person who spends the money is not allowed to print it, and the person who watches the printing is not allowed to spend. Auditors check both.

What it means for a citizen

Citizens get a public budget and published audits. Because Kirk issuance belongs to the Reserve, not the Treasury, no officeholder can fund a project by creating Kirk at will. Today there is still no ledger, no Treasury balance and no Kirk in circulation. This is the design the future machinery will have to follow.

What happens next

Article 4 is the only article here ratified by the user. Its open details, namely how Kirk is issued and how taxes work, were taken up in Proposal 02 (Kirk), Proposal 05 (tax delegation) and Proposal 06 (the First Tax Act).
