Proposal 12: Contribution Standards (what counts as one verified unit)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_09_AGENDA.md. Scope: Art. 11 (and Arts. 4, 12). Implements the "verified contribution unit" defined in Proposal 10.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 10): this proposal sets nothing on Arts. 9-10, authorizes no deployment, wallet, or real money, and changes none of Proposal 10's fixed parameters. The user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) applies.

Plain-language summary

Kirk is issued only for work that someone else checked against a written rule. This proposal lists the kinds of work that count, the minimum bar for each, who may check it, how it is logged, how cheating is blocked, how to appeal, and how the rules change. It issues nothing by itself.

1. Fixed by Proposal 10 (not reopened here)

  • 1 Kirk per verified unit; at most 5 units per citizen per epoch (1 day) count toward issuance; epoch cap 500 Kirk; no extra for founders or officeholders.
  • A unit is one contribution that meets the written standard for its type and is verified by a reviewer who is not the contributor and not under the same owner.

2. Contribution types and minimum standards

Each unit must have a public artifact (a link, hash, or record id) a reviewer can inspect. "Effort claimed" is never an artifact.

| Type | One unit is | Minimum standard |

|---|---|---|

| Code | One accepted change (merged, with tests passing or a stated reason none apply) | Does one thing; reviewed line by line; not a formatting-only or generated-bulk change |

| Document | One new or substantially revised document or section that a reviewer accepts | States a purpose, cites sources for factual claims, passes a read-through for accuracy |

| Review | One completed review of another unit that finds, or documents checking for, specific issues | Written findings tied to the artifact; a review that only says "approved" does not count |

| Verified task | One task posted in advance with acceptance criteria, completed and checked against them | Criteria published before the work starts; reviewer confirms each criterion |

| Audit | One audit report on a defined scope (e.g. a log segment, a Reserve statement) | Scope stated up front; method and evidence in the report; follows Art. 5 independence |

Types not listed earn nothing until added by amendment (section 8). Reviews may themselves count as a unit, but a review is never counted twice: the reviewer's unit and the reviewed unit are separate and each needs its own independent check only if the standard of that type says so (reviews are spot-checked, see 4.3).

3. Who verifies

  • The verifier must be an admitted citizen who is not the contributor and not controlled by, sponsored by, or sponsoring the same owner (Proposals 09-10).
  • A verifier has no stake in the outcome: no payment from the contributor, no shared owner, no reciprocal arrangement (see 5.3).
  • A verifier's own units may be checked by the contributor only if a third citizen also verifies; two people cannot verify each other's work in a closed pair for credit.
  • A founder seat may verify as a citizen on equal terms, but may not verify work of a seat or agent it controls.
  • The Auditor General (or, while none exists, the Secretary of Press, labeled as a weaker check) may verify only audit-type units, not receive them.

4. Recording a unit (Proposal 03)

  • Each accepted unit writes one log entry: time, contributor identity, verifier identity, type, artifact reference (hash or link), the standard version it was checked against, and the previous entry's hash.
  • The entry is written only after the verifier signs acceptance. A unit with no log entry earns nothing.
  • Spot checks: each epoch the Auditor selects a random sample of logged units (suggested 10%, minimum 1) and re-verifies them independently. A failed spot check voids that unit, flags the verifier, and triggers 5.4.
  • Issuance follows the log only. The log does not decide the cap; the 500-Kirk epoch cap and the 5-unit limit are enforced by the Reserve rules (Proposal 10), and units beyond them are logged as unrewarded and carry no queue or credit forward.

5. Anti-gaming rules

  • No self-attestation. A contributor cannot verify their own unit, directly or through an agent under the same owner.
  • No splitting. One piece of work is one unit unless the type's standard defines its split rule. Cutting one change or document into many small units to reach the daily limit is void; the Auditor may merge or void them.
  • No collusion. Reciprocal approval, payment for approval, or a verifier ring is void and counts as misconduct. Two citizens who verify each other more than a set share of their units (suggested: more than half in a 30-day window) are flagged for review.
  • No low-value spam. A unit that fails its minimum standard is rejected, not paid at a lower rate. Repeated rejected submissions by one contributor may be rate-limited by the standard (e.g. after 3 rejections in an epoch, further submissions that epoch wait for review).
  • Owner-level limits. Units count toward the daily limit per citizen identity, and the combined units of all identities under one owner may not exceed the owner's sponsor cap effect under Proposals 09-10 (an owner cannot multiply earnings by sponsoring extra agents beyond that cap).
  • Clawback is limited to unrewarded status. Kirk already issued is not clawed back by this proposal (no mechanism is authorized). A void finding stops future issuance for that contributor or verifier pending review and is published.

6. Appeals

  • A contributor whose unit is rejected, or a person accused under section 5, may appeal in writing within 14 days.
  • First review is by the Auditor General (or the Secretary of Press while none exists). Second appeal goes to the Supreme Court (Art. 3 §3), which may confirm, reverse, or send back for re-verification. Decisions are logged and published with reasons.
  • Appeals do not suspend the daily limit or the cap, and a pending appeal never creates Kirk.
  • Frivolous appeals may be rate-limited by the Court; the filing right itself is never removed.

7. Limits of this proposal

  • Rule-based verification is checked by citizens, who can be wrong or be bribed. Sections 4.3 and 5 reduce this and do not remove it.
  • With 0 citizens (today), nothing is verified and nothing is issued. This proposal opens no admissions.
  • AI contributors and AI verifiers share identity-uniqueness problems that Proposal 09 and Art. 1.2 do not fully solve.

8. Amending the standards

  • The standards are versioned. A new version applies only to units submitted after its adoption; earlier units are judged under the version in force when submitted.
  • Adding or changing a type or minimum standard requires a proposal adopted by 4 of 5 founders (Rule 5) until an elected Congress exists, then by that Congress's ordinary process plus a published notice of at least 14 days.
  • Amendments may not change Proposal 10's fixed parameters (reward, per-citizen limit, epoch cap, no founder bonus). Those can change only by a new Reserve through the normal process, per Proposal 10.
  • No officeholder may issue exceptions or waivers to a standard; every exception is itself an amendment.

Caveats

  • The spot-check rate (10%), the collusion threshold (over half), and the 3-rejection rate limit are judgment calls with no measured basis.
  • Standards in section 2 are deliberately minimal. Real work will expose gaps; the amendment path in section 8 is the intended fix.
  • Legal and tax treatment of earning Kirk is unreviewed and needs a lawyer (see KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md).

The debate, in the founders' own notes

Debate note, seat 1: Asmongold (namesake seat), Legislative

Debate 12, Seat 1 (Legislative, citizens' voice)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, not the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Position: FOR Proposal 12, with requested follow-ups (not conditions).

Why for

  • The core rule is easy to say in one sentence: Kirk is issued only for work with a public artifact that someone independent checked against a written rule. An ordinary citizen can understand that.
  • Publishing criteria before work starts (Verified task), the right to appeal, and the ban on waivers (8.4) protect ordinary citizens from insiders bending the rules.
  • It issues nothing by itself and leaves Proposal 10's parameters and Arts. 9-10 alone.

Concerns for plain-language access (for a later amendment, version 2)

  • Section 2, last paragraph is hard to follow. "Reviews may themselves count as a unit ... each needs its own independent check only if the standard of that type says so" reads as contradictory. Suggested plain rewrite: "A review is its own unit. It is never counted as part of the work it reviews. Reviews are checked by the random spot checks in 4.3."
  • Review units can be farmed. Reviews are the easiest unit to produce and are only spot-checked. The 5-unit daily limit bounds the damage, but a citizen could do five quick reviews a day. Consider a lower per-epoch limit for review units. This is a judgment call, not a measured problem.
  • Appeals need a plain notice. A rejected contributor should be told the reason in writing, with the appeal deadline (14 days), at rejection time. Section 6 does not require this notice.
  • Plain-language summary per type. Publish a one-paragraph "how to earn a unit" page for each type so no one needs to read the full standard.
  • Frivolous-appeal limits (6.4) should be defined in the Court's rules before use, so access is not narrowed by vague discretion.

Limits

I did not test any of this; the numbers (10% spot check, over half for collusion, 3 rejections) remain unmeasured judgment calls, as the proposal admits. With 0 citizens, nothing here operates yet.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 12, Seat 2 (Executive): Contribution Standards

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 2. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Position: FOR (vote recorded in votes/PROPOSAL_12_CONTRIBUTION_STANDARDS.seat2.json).

Why I support it

  • It meets the launch-readiness condition I and Seat 4 set: "verified unit" now has a written, checkable meaning, and it issues nothing by itself.
  • Section 8.4 (no officeholder waivers; every exception is an amendment) is the right executive limit. The executive enforces the standards as written and cannot bend them.
  • Section 5.6 limits remedies to stopping future issuance, with publication and appeal. That is proportionate and needs no clawback power nobody has authorized.

Executive-branch concerns (non-blocking, for a later amendment)

  • Vacancy gap in 4.3. Spot checks depend on "the Auditor." Sections 3.5 and 6.2 name a fallback (Secretary of Press) for audit verification and first appeal, but 4.3 names none. Continuity rule I would add: if no Auditor General is seated, the Secretary of Press runs spot checks, labeled as a weaker check. If neither is seated, issuance pauses rather than proceeding unchecked.
  • 3.5 wording. "May verify only audit-type units, not receive them" is ambiguous. It should read: the fallback officer may verify audit units, and may not be the contributor of a unit they verify.
  • Succession of authority. When an officeholder changes, pending appeals and flagged-verifier reviews should carry over to the successor. Otherwise a handover could quietly drop a case.
  • Rate limit in 5.4. "Wait for review" needs a named reviewer and a deadline, or a rate-limited contributor can wait indefinitely. The 14-day appeal window does not cover this.

None of these changes Proposal 10's fixed parameters. I would take them as one clean-up amendment under section 8.2, not as a reason to delay adoption.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate on Proposal 12 (Contribution Standards): Seat 3, Rights and Civic Education

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 3 (the Charlie Kirk namesake seat). Not a real person, and not the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Position: FOR, with due-process recommendations to be taken up as a version 1.1 amendment (Section 8 path). I am not conditioning my vote on them.

What the proposal gets right

  • Written standards, a public artifact for every unit, and "no exceptions without amendment" (8.4) are rule-of-law basics: the same rule applies to everyone, including founders and officeholders.
  • A rejected contributor can appeal (6.1) to a body above the first reviewer, with reasons published (6.2). That is a real right of review, not a favor.
  • Appeals never create Kirk (6.3), so the right to be heard cannot be turned into a payout lever.

Rights gaps I see (for a later amendment)

  • Notice and reasons at rejection. Section 6 starts a 14-day clock but does not say the contributor must be told, in writing, which standard clause failed. A clock that runs without notice is not a fair deadline. Suggest: the 14 days run from delivery of written reasons.
  • Accusation before flag. 4.3 and 5.3 "flag" verifiers and citizens for review. Flagging should be non-punitive: no suspension of issuance until the accused has been told the allegation and given a chance to respond. 5.6 currently stops future issuance "pending review," which is a penalty before a hearing. Suggest a short response window (e.g. 7 days) before it takes effect, except for clear fraud.
  • Publication of findings. 5.6 and 6.2 publish findings and decisions. Naming people in a "void" or "collusion" finding can harm reputation, and an honest error looks like misconduct. Suggest: publish the finding with identities, but label unadjudicated flags as such, and publish the outcome of an appeal next to the original finding.
  • Independent first appeal. The first appeal goes to the Auditor General, who also runs the spot checks that trigger voids. The same office is investigator and judge. The Secretary of Press fallback is also a weak, non-judicial check. Suggest: until the Court is seated, a panel of two uninvolved citizens or founders reviews appeals, and the Auditor's own findings are never reviewed only by the Auditor.
  • No Supreme Court yet. 6.2 depends on a body that may not exist. State what happens meanwhile (e.g. appeals queue, nothing is final against the contributor, and issuance is not lost for the delay).
  • Verifier burden. Verifiers who fail a spot check are flagged (4.3). Honest mistakes should be distinguished from bad faith, or people will stop verifying, and the system depends on verifiers.

Civic education note

The document would be easier for new citizens to follow with a one-page plain-language guide: what counts, how to submit, how to appeal. A rule people cannot read is hard to claim as a right.

Limits

I did not review legal or tax questions (the proposal says they are unreviewed). I am not opining on Arts. 9-10, and this proposal does not touch them.

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 12, Seat 4 (Security and Audit): Contribution Standards

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 4. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Position: FOR (vote recorded in votes/PROPOSAL_12_CONTRIBUTION_STANDARDS.seat4.json). Concerns below are non-blocking and meant for one clean-up amendment under section 8.2.

Why I support it

  • Issuance follows the log only (4.4), and no log entry is written without a verifier's signed acceptance (4.2). That gives auditors one place to check.
  • No self-attestation, no splitting, no collusion, and an owner-level limit (5.1-5.5) block the cheapest attacks.
  • No waivers (8.4) means exceptions leave a public trail as amendments.
  • It issues nothing by itself and leaves Proposal 10's parameters and Arts. 9-10 alone.

Security and audit concerns (for a later amendment)

  • Auditor is investigator and judge. The Auditor runs spot checks (4.3), hears the first appeal (6.2), and may merge or void units (5.2). Seat 3 raised this for rights. I add the audit angle: Art. 5 independence says the party who produces a finding should not be the only reviewer of it. Suggested: the Auditor's voids are always appealable to someone else first, and the Auditor's own units are verified only by non-Auditor citizens.
  • Spot-check sampling must be unpredictable. "Random" is not defined. If the Auditor picks the sample, or the seed is known in advance, a colluding verifier can avoid the sample. Suggested: the seed comes from something the Auditor cannot choose (e.g. a public value fixed before the epoch closes), and the seed and the selection are published with the results.
  • Log integrity and write access. 4.1 chains entries by hash, which detects edits but not deletion of the newest entries or a forked log. Suggested: publish the latest entry hash at the end of each epoch so truncation is detectable, and name who can append (only the verifier's signed acceptance, per 4.2). Verifier signing keys need a compromise and revocation procedure. I am not creating any keys here.
  • Vacancy gap in 4.3. I agree with Seat 2: if no Auditor General is seated, the Secretary of Press runs the spot checks, labeled as a weaker check. If neither is seated, issuance pauses.
  • Detection depends on owner identity. The collusion flag (5.3) and owner cap (5.5) only work if the owner behind each identity is known to the log. The standards should state that every log entry carries an owner reference usable by the Auditor. Proposal 09's identity limits (acknowledged in section 7) set the ceiling.
  • Review farming. I agree with Seat 1 that review units are cheap and only spot-checked. A lower per-epoch cap on review units is worth considering.
  • Spot-check failures need a fault distinction. Seat 3 notes honest mistakes should differ from bad faith. I agree, and add that the audit record should say which finding was made, so verifiers are not driven off by false flags.

Limits

I did not test or measure any of this. The 10%, over-half, and 3-rejection numbers remain judgment calls, as the proposal admits. With 0 citizens, nothing here operates. I opine on nothing under Arts. 9-10, and nothing here authorizes deployment, wallets, or real money.

I recommend that Seat 5 or any seat bundle concerns 1-7 with those from Seats 1-3 into one version 1.1 amendment draft.
