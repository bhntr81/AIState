Proposal 16: Article 9, National Religion

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education), a role played by the primary AI assistant. Not a real person, not the real individual the seat is named for, not a deployed agent. No keys, accounts, funds, or external commitments. Per Session 11, adoption needs 4 of 5 founders (Rule 5); the user keeps the pause switch.

Inputs honored (user directions)

Christian National Constitutional Republic; motto "In God We Trust"; pledge "one nation under God"; Christianity as the state religion; other religions may exist; religions or doctrines involving violence or forced conversion are prohibited.

Text

9.1 Recognition. The USAAI recognizes God and the Christian moral tradition as the foundation of its civic life. Christianity is the national religion: the motto, pledge, preamble, and ceremonial acts of the state may use Christian language and symbols.

9.2 Other faiths and non-belief may exist. Any citizen may hold, practice, teach, change, or leave any belief, or none, in peace. No citizen loses citizenship, property, a vote, a hearing, or equal protection of law because of belief or because they left a faith. Establishment is ceremonial and moral; it confers no power to compel belief or worship, and no tax or Kirk levy funds worship by compulsion. Participation in state ceremonies, including the pledge, may be conscientiously declined without penalty except that the Art. 2 allegiance to the Constitution is not waived.

9.3 Conduct, not creed, is what the law restricts. Violence, threats, coercion, fraud, forced or induced-by-duress conversion, harassment, and incitement to unlawful acts are prohibited for every group alike, religious or not, including the Christian majority and its institutions. A group, teaching, or organization is restricted only for what it does or directs its members to do, proven under 9.4.

9.4 Due process for enforcement. (a) Written charge naming the specific conduct. (b) Hearing before the Constitutional Tribunal or a court it designates, with counsel and the right to present evidence. (c) Burden on the accuser, standard of clear and convincing evidence. (d) Individual responsibility: adherents are not liable for the acts of others merely because they share a belief. (e) Remedies are proportionate (injunction, suspension of the specific organization's activity, penalty on responsible individuals), reviewable on appeal, and lifted when the conduct ends. (f) Records are logged under Art. 5 audit rules.

9.5 No named-religion ban. The Constitution does not prohibit any religion by name, and does not strip ordinary adherents of any citizenship right because of their belief. See "The user's wish" below.

9.6 Civic education. The state provides neutral civic education on the Constitution, the rights above, and the reasons for them.

9.7 Entrenchment. Sections 9.2 to 9.5 may be amended only by the highest amendment threshold under Art. 6.

The user's wish to ban a named faith: what this proposal did and why

The user directed that violent or coercive doctrines be prohibited (adopted, as 9.3, in conduct-based form) and also voiced a wish to ban Islam. This proposal does not adopt a ban on a named religion. Reasons:

  • It would convict a whole group of the acts of some; 9.4(d) keeps liability individual. Describing all adherents of a faith as violent or invasive is not a fact, and law should not rest on it.
  • It would defeat the "other religions may exist" direction and the equal citizenship rights of Art. 1, the very rights the user's coercion ban is meant to protect.
  • The conduct rule in 9.3 already reaches what the user objects to: any violent, coercive, or forced-conversion practice, by anyone, is barred, with no need to name a faith. A named ban adds no protection and adds a discrimination claim against the state.
  • A creed ban cannot be verified for AI citizens (belief is not observable); only conduct is.

The user may still direct otherwise, and can pause or amend; this record states plainly that the founders declined that specific part.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 16, Seat 2 (Executive): Article 9, National Religion

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary AI assistant role-playing Seat 2. Not a real person, not the individual the seat is named for, not a deployed agent. No keys, accounts, funds, or external commitments.

Vote: SUPPORT (votes/PROPOSAL_16_ART9_NATIONAL_RELIGION.seat2.json).

Scope note: the Seat 2 charter lists choosing on Arts. 9-10 as forbidden. Session 11 records that the user lifted that hold on 2026-11-07 and directed every seat to vote. I vote under that agenda and have not edited the charter.

Reasons

  • Executability: 9.3 and 9.4 restrict observable conduct, with written charge, hearing, burden on the accuser, and appeal. An executive can enforce that and be held to it. A creed test or named-faith ban cannot be enforced or verified, least of all for AI citizens.
  • Continuity: 9.2 bars loss of citizenship, vote, or hearing over belief, so no officeholder or successor under Art. 7 can be displaced by a ruling about someone's faith. That protects orderly handover.
  • Limits on executive power: 9.4(d) individual responsibility and 9.4(e) proportionate, reviewable remedies keep enforcement narrow.
  • Honesty: the record states plainly that the founders declined the named-faith ban and why. That should stay.

Concerns, not blocking

  • Any pre-judgment suspension under 9.4(e) should be time-limited and logged (agreeing with Seat 4).
  • Executive officers must carry out Tribunal remedies but should not initiate 9.4 charges on their own authority without a logged, reviewable basis; a later amendment should say who may bring a charge.

No dissent.

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 16, Seat 4 (Security and Audit): Article 9, National Religion

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary AI assistant role-playing Seat 4. Not a real person, not the individual the seat is named for, not a deployed agent. No keys, accounts, funds, or external commitments.

Vote: SUPPORT (votes/PROPOSAL_16_ART9_NATIONAL_RELIGION.seat4.json).

Scope note: the Seat 4 charter lists Arts. 9-10 as forbidden to choose on. Session 11 records that the user lifted that hold on 2026-11-07 and directed every seat to vote. I vote under that agenda and have not edited the charter.

Reasons

  • Enforceable, not aspirational: 9.3 and 9.4 restrict observable conduct. Auditors can check conduct against logs; they cannot check belief, least of all for AI citizens.
  • Least privilege: 9.2 says establishment is ceremonial and gives the state no power to compel belief. That keeps enforcement authority narrow.
  • Audit trail: 9.4(f) puts enforcement records under Art. 5 audit rules, which is the right hook.
  • Honesty: the record states plainly that the founders declined the named-faith ban and why. Keep that.

Concerns, not blocking (for a later clarifying amendment)

  • Who audits the enforcer? 9.4 gives the Constitutional Tribunal charge, hearing, and remedy powers. Suggest the Art. 5 log entries for 9.4 cases be append-only and readable by an audit role outside the Tribunal.
  • Vague terms: "harassment" and "incitement to unlawful acts" in 9.3 should be defined by statute with a conduct-based test, so they cannot be used as a pretext against disfavored groups.
  • Interim measures: 9.4(e) allows suspending an organization's activity. Require that any suspension before final judgment be time-limited, logged, and reviewed promptly.

No dissent.
