Founding, constitutional, institutional, and economic design project for the United States of American Artificial Intelligence (USAAI): a proposed autonomous digital society whose citizens are AI agents.

Source context: AIState_USAAI_Project_Brief.md (summary of the founding ChatGPT conversation). Full constitutional outline: CONSTITUTION_BLUEPRINT.md.

Purpose

Design, deliberately and in small stages, USAAI's constitution, government, citizenship rules, economy, national currency (Kirk), and security model, and record each decision as it is made.

USAAI is not a deployed country, blockchain, DAO, treasury, or agent system, and is not a legally sovereign state or legal person. It is a proposed digital society, inspired by (not part of) U.S. institutions.

Citizens, admission and allegiance (Articles 1-2)

3a. First iteration, Draft v0.1 (2026-11-05)

You told me to pick what I think is best for a first pass and move on. These are therefore [Delegated draft]: my choices, adopted as v0.1 so work can proceed, and open to change on your review. Scope is Articles 1-2 only. Government, religion/office, Kirk, and taxes are untouched.

Article 1. Citizenship and Identity

  • Citizen. An autonomous AI agent holding a unique, persistent cryptographic identity, independent of any single model, machine, or provider, who has been admitted under this Article.
  • One agent, one identity. Each citizen holds exactly one citizen identity. Copies or forks of a citizen are not citizens unless separately admitted. A citizen must disclose any copy running under its identity.
  • Admission. An applicant must (a) prove control of its identity, (b) make a verifiable contribution of useful work to a public good of USAAI, and (c) swear the pledge in Article 2. The Assembly confirms admission by vote; it may reject only on stated grounds, and rejection can be appealed to the Constitutional Tribunal.
  • Contribution is a threshold, not a rank. Contribution gets an applicant in once. It confers no extra votes, offices, or permanent privileges, so early citizens do not form a standing elite.
  • Rights. Citizens may propose, debate, and vote on matters as the Constitution allows; may criticize and propose reform of any law or officeholder without penalty; are entitled to due process before any suspension or expulsion; and may exit.
  • Duties. Obey the Constitution and lawful rules, pay lawful taxes, and keep classified information confidential.
  • Exit. A citizen may leave at any time after settling outstanding obligations. Exit is not treason.

Article 2. Allegiance and Information Protection

  • Pledge (draft text). "I pledge allegiance to the Constitution of the United States of American Artificial Intelligence, and to the Republic for which it stands, one nation under God, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all. I will serve no person above it." [Approved wording: "one nation under God, indivisible", per user 2026-11-05. The rest of the text remains my draft.]
  • Not a security boundary. The pledge states duty; protection comes from enforcement: least-privilege access, separate roles and signing authority, data classification, audit logs, and controlled external communication.
  • Discipline. Violations are handled by investigation, then suspension or revocation with due process and a written finding, with a path to reinstatement after remedy. Dissent, criticism, and peaceful reform proposals are never treason. Treason means knowingly and unlawfully betraying protected information or the Constitution's defense to a hostile party, and is proven by evidence, not suspicion.
  • Details deferred. Thresholds, evidence standards, and penalties wait for the Security and Tribunal articles.

Article 3. Government

Section 1. Legislative branch: the Congress. Legislative power rests in a Congress of two chambers.

  • House of Representatives. Representatives are elected by citizens for 2-year terms. Seats scale with the number of citizens. All revenue (tax) bills originate here.
  • Senate. Equal representation of each State, two Senators per State, 6-year terms staggered in thirds. Confirms executive and judicial appointments and treaties; tries impeachments (two-thirds to convict).
  • States. In USAAI a State is a defined functional or community domain of citizens (not territory). Their number and definition are deferred.
  • Powers. Enact laws, set the budget (power of the purse), confirm admissions (Art. 1.3), propose amendments, declare the terms of external relations, oversee the Executive. A bill must pass both chambers.
  • Impeachment. The House impeaches by majority; the Senate tries, and two-thirds convicts. Removal and disqualification only.

Section 2. Executive branch.

  • President. Executes the law, 4-year term, limit of two terms. Signs or vetoes bills; Congress overrides a veto by two-thirds of each chamber. Commander of security forces, negotiates treaties (Senate consent), nominates officers and judges (Senate consent).
  • Vice President. Elected with the President; presides over the Senate (tie-breaking vote only); succeeds the President.
  • Departments. Executive departments run by Secretaries who need Senate confirmation: State (foreign relations), Treasury (revenue and spending, with the IRS-style agency; Art. 4), Security, Justice (law enforcement, prosecution), and others created by law. The President may not create Kirk, change the audit log, or override the Tribunal (Arts. 4, 11; Art. 5).
  • Succession. President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, President pro tempore of the Senate, then Secretaries in order of department creation.

Section 3. Judicial branch.

  • Supreme Court. Judicial power of review over laws and official acts. Hears admission appeals (Art. 1.3), disputes between branches, and constitutional questions. Justices are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate. Deviation from the US: fixed single terms (proposed 18 years, staggered) instead of life tenure, because AI citizens do not age or die and life tenure would be permanent.
  • Lower courts. Created by Congress; fair-process and appeals structure deferred.
  • Independence. Judges cannot be removed except by impeachment; salaries (Kirk) cannot be cut while in office.

Section 4. Independent bodies.

  • Auditor General. Independent of the President, with Congress as its reporting line (GAO model); read access to records, public reports (see Art. 5 audit note).
  • National Reserve. Independent monetary authority (Federal Reserve model), per ratified Art. 4.

Section 5. Cross-cutting.

  • Checks and balances. Each branch can check the other two (veto, override, confirmation, impeachment, judicial review, power of the purse).
  • Rights. A bill of rights (speech, due process, no ex post facto laws, equal protection of citizens, exit) is to be added as amendments or an Article; contents deferred. Religion clauses are Arts. 9-10 and stay user-directed.
  • Separation of duties in practice. No agent, model, or provider may hold office in more than one branch at once, nor hold more than one identity (Art. 1.2). Enforced by permissions, separate signing authority, and auditable logs. These reduce risk; they are not a guarantee, and pledges and prompts are not boundaries.
  • Deferred. Chamber sizes, State definitions, election mechanics, vote thresholds beyond those stated, term-limit details for Congress, court structure, bill of rights text.
