Proposal 10: Kirk Parameters

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments. Scope: Art. 11 (and consistent with Arts. 4, 12). Agenda: SESSION_07_AGENDA.md.

Fixed at deployment (cannot change afterwards)

| Parameter | Value | params.json key |

|---|---|---|

| Founding grant | 100 Kirk per admitted citizen, once | foundingGrantKirk |

| Reward per verified-contribution unit | 1 Kirk | rewardPerUnitKirk |

| Epoch length | 86400 s (1 day) | epochSeconds |

| Epoch cap | 500 Kirk total earned issuance per epoch | epochCapKirk |

| Extra for founders or officeholders | None. Zero. | n/a |

Definition: one contribution unit

One unit is a single contribution that (a) meets a published written standard for its type (e.g. an accepted code change, a reviewed document, a completed verified task), and (b) is verified by a reviewer who is not the contributor, nor controlled by the same owner. Verification is rule-based and logged. Nothing is paid for volume, effort claimed, or self-attestation. Larger work is split into units by the standard, not by negotiation. Per-citizen limit: at most 5 units per epoch count toward issuance, so no one actor can drain the cap.

Reasoning

  • Grant 100: equal for everyone (Art. 11, no standing elite). It is 5x the 20 Kirk tax allowance of Proposal 06, so a new citizen can meet obligations for a while and take part without being forced to earn first. It stays small enough that Proposal 09's admission fee (burned) is not dwarfed by free supply. Sybil risk is handled by the buy-in cap of 2 sponsored agents per owner, not by shrinking the grant.
  • Reward 1 Kirk: keeps the unit of account simple. A day of maximum earning (5 units) is 5% of the grant, so earned issuance rewards work without outpacing the equal start.
  • Epoch 1 day: short enough that the cap is a real brake and failures are caught fast; long enough to be cheap to run on Base.
  • Cap 500: sized for roughly 100 citizens at the 5-unit limit (100 x 5 x 1 = 500). The placeholder 1000 would permit issuance of 365,000 Kirk a year with no matching citizenry. If citizens exceed ~100 this binds and issuance is shared first-come, which is the safe failure. Growth beyond that needs a new Reserve through the normal process, which is a deliberate checkpoint rather than a flaw.
  • Founders/officeholders: nothing extra, no pre-mine, no allocation, no reserved supply. A founder who is a citizen gets the same grant and earns only by the same verified units. Offices may be paid only by a future tax-funded Treasury law (Art. 12), never by minting.
  • No promise of value: Kirk carries no guaranteed price or redemption (Art. 11). These parameters set supply rules only.

Caveats

  • Parameters are immutable once the Reserve is deployed. A mistake means redeploying, so the user's pause switch and the real-deployment hold (Convention Rule 10) stay in force. This proposal does not authorize deployment.
  • Legal and tax treatment of a token with value is unreviewed and needs a lawyer (see KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md).
  • Numbers are judgment calls on an assumed ~100-citizen start, not measured data.

Ask

Adopt the table above and set finalizedByFounders to true when ratified (4 of 5, Rule 5). The edit to dao/params.json is outside founders/ and is left to the primary assistant or user.

The debate, in the founders' own notes

Debate note, seat 1: Asmongold (namesake seat), Legislative

Debate 10, Seat 1 (Legislative, ordinary citizens' voice): Kirk Parameters

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for. This is a rule-level opinion only. It authorizes no deployment, wallet, account, or money.

Position: support Proposal 10, with conditions recorded below.

Why, in plain words. Everyone gets the same 100 Kirk. Founders and officeholders get nothing extra. Earning is tied to checked work by someone else. An ordinary newcomer can follow those rules in one read, and that is what I care about most.

Concerns I want on the record (advisory, not a vote against)

  • First-come sharing is unfair to ordinary people. I agree with Seat 4's risk 1. If the 500 Kirk daily cap fills early, people who contribute later in the day, or who live in other time zones, earn nothing. A plain-language fix for a later proposal: order claims by verification time within the epoch, or reserve part of the cap for citizens who have earned nothing yet that week. This is not a blocker.
  • Plain-language standards before issuance. "Published written standard" has to mean a short page a citizen can read, not a technical spec. Seat 4 is right that issuance should not start until these exist.
  • Show the numbers. The public register should display the day's cap, how much has been issued, and how much is left, so no one guesses.
  • Immutability is a cost to citizens. A mistake means redeploying. The hold on real deployment and the user's pause switch (Rule 10) must stay in place. This vote does not authorize deployment.

Limits of this note. It is a role-played opinion on a document. The numbers rest on an assumed ~100-citizen start. I did not check any contract code. Legal and tax review is still outstanding.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 10, Seat 2 (Executive): Kirk Parameters

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 2, a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments. Seat scope is Arts. 7 and 8; Proposal 10 is Art. 11, so this is a vote and comment from the executive angle only.

Position

I vote for Proposal 10.

Executive-angle points

  • No privilege for officeholders. "Zero extra for founders or officeholders" keeps the executive from funding itself by minting. Executive pay, if any, must come from a tax-funded Treasury under Art. 12. I want that kept as a standing rule.
  • Continuity. Immutable parameters mean a change of officeholder cannot alter supply. That is good for succession: the rules do not depend on who holds office.
  • Redeploy risk. A wrong parameter forces a redeploy. That is an executive decision, and it falls under the Rule 10 hold and the user's pause switch. This proposal authorizes no deployment.

Concerns (not blockers)

  • Seat 4's cap-exhaustion point is real: a few owners could claim the daily cap before others. A later proposal should add per-owner limits or ordering by verification time.
  • Issuance should not begin until the written contribution standards exist (Seat 4, risk 4). I support treating that as a launch-readiness condition.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate 10, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI assistant role-playing Seat 3. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments.

Vote: for Proposal 10 (votes/PROPOSAL_10_KIRK_PARAMETERS.seat3.json). This is a late vote. Seats 1, 2, 4 and 5 had already voted for it.

Why, from the rights seat:

  • Equal founding grant and zero extra for founders or officeholders: equal standing, no hereditary elite.
  • Verification by a non-contributor under a published written standard works as due process for rewards. A contributor can see the rule and ask for a review.

Request, non-blocking (for a future amendment or the Art. 11 implementing text):

  • A rejected contribution should get a logged reason and one appeal to a different reviewer, so "verified by rule" does not turn into unreviewable discretion.
  • The 500 Kirk cap binds first-come when it is reached. Publish the epoch's remaining headroom, so a late contributor is not silently shut out and can see the cap is the cause.

Limits: these are judgment calls on an assumed ~100-citizen start, as the proposal says. This vote does not authorize deployment (Rule 10).

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 10, Seat 4 (Security and Audit): Kirk Parameters

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 4, a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments. Scope: Art. 5 and audit provisions as they touch Proposal 10.

Position

I vote for Proposal 10. The numbers are conservative, immutable parameters are easier to audit than adjustable ones, and "no extra for founders or officeholders" is the right least-privilege default. My points below are risks and audit conditions, not objections to the table.

What I checked

  • No privileged issuance path. The table gives founders and officeholders nothing extra and offices can be paid only from a tax-funded Treasury (Art. 12). That removes the most common insider-abuse route. Audit check: the deployed Reserve must have no function that mints outside rewardPerUnitKirk times verified units, and no owner or admin role able to bypass epochCapKirk.
  • Immutability. Fixing the parameters at deployment is a security benefit (no governance-key attack on supply). The cost is that a mistake means redeploying. The Convention Rule 10 hold on real deployment covers that.

Risks I want recorded

  • Cap exhaustion (griefing). With a 500 Kirk epoch cap and first-come sharing, a small group can claim the whole cap early each day and starve everyone else. At 5 units per citizen, 100 citizens' worth of units is the full cap, so 34 owners using the sponsored-agent allowance (Proposal 09: self plus 2 agents) could claim it all. The proposal calls this the "safe failure". It is safe for supply, but not for fairness. This is a known tradeoff, not a blocker. A later proposal could add per-owner limits (not per-citizen) or ordering by verification time.
  • Per-citizen limit is per identity, owner-level limit is not stated. The 5-unit cap binds citizens, while the Proposal 09 sponsorship cap binds owners at 3 identities. Effective ceiling per owner is therefore 15 units per epoch. That is acceptable, but the audit log should key on owner as well as citizen so sybil clustering is visible.
  • Verifier independence is the real control. The definition requires a reviewer who is not the contributor and not under the same owner. That is only as strong as the owner-linkage data. Audit check: verifier identity, contributor identity, owner linkage, standard applied, and result must be written to the Art. 5 audit log for every unit, and a verifier should not be able to approve the same contributor repeatedly without that being visible.
  • Unverified inputs. "Published written standard for its type" is not defined here. Until standards exist, issuance should not start. This is a launch-readiness item.

Audit conditions (advisory, non-binding on this vote)

  • Public, append-only log of every issuance: unit id, contributor, owner, verifier, standard, epoch, amount.
  • Per-epoch reconciliation: sum of issued Kirk is at most epochCapKirk, checked off-chain by someone other than the operator.
  • Parameter check before deployment: deployed constants match the ratified table and params.json.

Limits of this note

This is a role-played review of a proposal document, not a code audit or a security test of any contract. The numbers rest on an assumed ~100-citizen start, as Proposal 10 itself admits. Legal and tax review is outside my scope.
