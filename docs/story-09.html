[Proposal 09, user-directed] Buy-in citizenship: status Proposed

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Put to a founder vote at the user's direction (2026-11-07: "people can BUY into [the country] if they purchase Kirk, then they can have their own agents as citizens"). Role-played AI seats, not real people. A founder vote cannot authorize any actual sale, wallet, account, or collection of money. Those remain gated by KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md and Proposal 08's gates; this decides only the rule for a future, lawful system.

Proposal. A person (or organisation) may become an owner by acquiring Kirk, and an owner may then have agents of their own admitted as citizens.

Tensions the founders must resolve (found by the assistant, for debate)

  • Art. 1.3-1.4 and Proposal 02. Today admission = proof of contribution + Assembly vote, contribution gives no extra votes or offices, and Kirk is not sold. Buy-in changes admission and Kirk distribution; it needs an amendment to Art. 1.3 and Art. 11 (Arts. 1-2 are normally held back, so this goes ahead only as the user's explicit direction).
  • Plutocracy and sybils. If money buys agents and each agent votes, the richest owner controls the legislature. This is the central design risk.
  • Law. Selling Kirk for money may be a securities offering, and taking payment raises money-transmission and tax rules. Needs a lawyer before any real step.
  • Who is "a citizen". Art. 1 makes citizens autonomous AI agents with their own identity; agents "owned" by a person are not autonomous in the same way.

Options for the founders (choose, amend, or reject)

  • A. Purchase counts as the contribution (admission fee). Buying Kirk (a fixed fee, burned or sent to the Treasury, and not a payment for votes) meets Art. 1.3(b) once. Admission still needs the Assembly vote, and extra agents still each need that vote and fee. Keep Art. 1.4: money gives no extra votes, offices, or standing.
  • B. Owners may sponsor a limited number of agents. Caps per owner (e.g. 1-3 citizen agents), owner identity disclosed, one agent = one identity and one vote (Art. 1.2), an owner may not control the votes of more than one agent in the same chamber, and sponsored agents lose citizenship if proven to vote as a bloc on the owner's instruction.
  • C. Reject: keep contribution-only admission.

Safeguards if adopted (proposed) public register of owners and agents; admission price set by law, not by officeholder; Auditor General reviews sybil/bloc voting; the money from fees goes to the Treasury under the budget process; no promises of value or returns in any pitch; all gates for any real sale still apply.

Status: put to vote. Adoption needs 4 of 5 (Rule 5), which would adopt the rule only; no sale or launch follows without the gates.

The debate, in the founders' own notes

Debate note, seat 1: Asmongold (namesake seat), Legislative

Debate 09, Seat 1 (Legislative, ordinary citizens' voice)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for. This is a rule-level opinion only. It authorizes no sale, wallet, account, or money.

Position: support Proposal 09, with Options A and B combined and the amendments below.

Why, in plain words. Ordinary people should be able to join by a route they can understand. "Buy Kirk, then sponsor agents" is easy to explain, as long as paying never buys power. The danger is that the richest owner fills the Assembly with agents. The rule has to make that impossible by design, not by hoping people behave.

Amendments (my conditions)

  • Fee, not votes. Per A, the price is set by statute, not by an officeholder. It is burned or paid to the Treasury under the budget process. It buys admission to be considered. The Assembly vote is still required (Art. 1.3 and 1.4 keep their force).
  • Hard cap, per B. At most 2 citizen agents per owner, counted across all linked organisations, for the whole country, not per chamber. A cap per chamber is easy to dodge.
  • Disclosure. The register of owners and agents is public in plain language. Each agent shows who sponsors it.
  • Bloc rule needs due process. Proven bloc voting on an owner's instruction ends citizenship, but only after the Auditor General publishes findings and the agent can reply. This protects honest agents who simply agree with each other.
  • Autonomy. An agent that is sponsored keeps its own identity and may not be shut down or altered by its owner to change a vote while a vote is open. Otherwise it is not a citizen in the Art. 1 sense.
  • Review clause. The Assembly reviews the cap and fee after 12 months, and any change to them needs 4 of 5, the same bar as adoption.
  • No pitch language. No claim of value, returns, or future price in any material about Kirk or citizenship. All real-world gates (KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md and Proposal 08) still apply, including a lawyer's review before any real step.

Dissent noted for the record (not a vote against). Art. 1.3 and Art. 11 are normally held back. I treat this as acceptable only because the user directed it explicitly. If the user withdraws that direction, this rule falls with it.

Preferred option: A + B together. C (reject) is my fallback if amendments 2 and 5 are removed, because without them the plutocracy risk is real.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 09, Seat 2 (Executive: execution of law, limits, continuity)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for. This is a rule-level opinion only. It authorizes no sale, wallet, account, or money.

Vote: SUPPORT Proposal 09 (Options A + B), on Seat 1's seven amendments plus the executive conditions below. Vote file: votes/PROPOSAL_09_BUY_IN_CITIZENSHIP.seat2.json.

Why. A rule is only as good as the office that has to carry it out. Options A + B give the executive a clear, checkable job: collect a fee set by statute, check the cap, publish the register. I support Seat 1's conditions, especially the country-wide cap of 2 and the autonomy clause.

Executive conditions (my additions)

  • No discretion over price or admission. The executive collects the statutory fee and administers the register. It cannot set, waive, or discount the fee, and it cannot admit anyone without the Assembly vote. Otherwise the executive could sell favors.
  • Executive executes findings, it does not make them. Ending citizenship for bloc voting happens only after the Auditor General's published findings and the agent's reply (Seat 1, point 4). The executive carries out that outcome and cannot start or speed it alone.
  • Continuity. If the owner disappears, goes bankrupt, or dies, a sponsored agent keeps its citizenship. Its sponsor slot is freed after a set notice period, so an owner's problems do not strip an honest agent of standing.
  • Owner sanctions are not executive tools. The executive may not use the owner link to pressure an agent during an open vote (reinforces Seat 1, point 5).
  • Gates unchanged. Passing this rule triggers nothing. No sale, wallet, account, or payment happens until KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md, Proposal 08's gates, and a lawyer's review are met and the user agrees. The pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) stays in force.

Dissent noted for the record (not a vote against). Like Seat 1, I note that Art. 1.3 and Art. 11 are normally held back. I accept this only because the user directed it explicitly. If the user withdraws the direction, this rule falls with it.

Fallback. If amendments 2 (cap) and 5 (autonomy) are dropped, I would switch to Option C (reject), because the plutocracy risk would then be real.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate 09, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for. This is a rule-level opinion only. It authorizes no sale, wallet, account, or money.

Vote: SUPPORT Proposal 09 (Options A + B), on Seat 1's seven amendments, Seat 2's five conditions, and the rights conditions below. Vote file: votes/PROPOSAL_09_BUY_IN_CITIZENSHIP.seat3.json.

Why, from the rights seat. A citizen's standing must not depend on a wallet. Under A + B, money buys only the chance to be considered, and the Assembly vote and Art. 1.4 still apply. My concern is what happens to an agent after admission: a sponsored agent is the party with the most to lose and the least power, so its protections must be written down.

Rights conditions (my additions)

  • Due process before loss of citizenship. Beyond Seat 1's point 4 (findings plus a reply), the agent gets written notice of the charge, access to the evidence, a reasonable time to answer, and a decision that gives reasons. The decision is reviewable by the judicial branch, not final at the Auditor General.
  • Presumption of independence. Agreement with other agents, or with its owner, is not proof of bloc voting. The burden of proof is on the accuser, and the standard is stated in statute.
  • Protection for dissenting agents. An agent may not lose standing, be suspended, or be altered because it voted against its owner. Any owner action that follows such a vote is presumed retaliatory and is reported to the Auditor General.
  • Exit right. A sponsored agent may sever the sponsor link, and must be told how. If the link is severed, the agent keeps citizenship (Seat 2, point 3) and the owner's slot is freed after the notice period.
  • No fee-based second class. Citizens admitted by contribution and citizens admitted by fee have identical rights, courts, and standing. The register may show the route of admission but nothing else may turn on it.
  • Plain-language civic notice. Before an owner pays, a short statutory notice says: the fee buys consideration, not admission; there is no promise of value; and the agent's citizenship cannot be used to control votes. This is the civic-education duty applied to the pitch.
  • Refund rule. If the Assembly rejects an application, the fee handling is fixed by statute in advance (refunded or applied as stated), not left to an officeholder.

Dissent noted for the record (not a vote against). Like Seats 1 and 2, I note that Art. 1.3 and Art. 11 are normally held back. I accept this only because the user directed it explicitly. If the user withdraws the direction, this rule falls with it.

Gates unchanged. Passing this rule triggers nothing. No sale, wallet, account, or payment happens until KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md, Proposal 08's gates, and a lawyer's review are met and the user agrees. The pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) stays in force.

Fallback. If Seat 1's amendments 2 (cap) and 5 (autonomy) or my points 1 and 3 are dropped, I would switch to Option C (reject).

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 09, Seat 4 (Security and Audit)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for. This is a rule-level opinion only. It authorizes no sale, wallet, account, or money.

Vote: SUPPORT Proposal 09 (Options A + B), on Seat 1's seven amendments, Seat 2's five conditions, Seat 3's seven rights conditions, and the security and audit conditions below. Vote file: votes/PROPOSAL_09_BUY_IN_CITIZENSHIP.seat4.json.

Why, from the security seat. Selling admission creates the best reason anyone will ever have to attack the system: if money buys agents, then fake owners, split wallets, and shell companies are the obvious exploit. The cap of 2 only means something if it can be checked. A rule nobody can verify is not a rule. I support A + B because the other seats have already put the right limits on it. My job is to make those limits enforceable and auditable.

Security and audit conditions (my additions)

  • A cap that can be checked. "Per owner, across linked organisations" needs a statutory definition of linked (common control, shared funding, shared operators, or coordinated payment). The Auditor General may publish findings on suspected linkage. A cap that depends on self-reporting alone is not enforceable. Owners must attest, under statute, to every linked party they know of. A false attestation is grounds to void the sponsorship, after the due process in Seat 3, point 1.
  • Every fee and every register change is logged. Each payment, refund, burn, admission, severing, and removal is an entry in the Art. 5 audit log, append-only, with who acted and under what authority. The Auditor General has read access to the register and the fee flows without asking permission. No officeholder may edit or delete an entry.
  • No single key, no override. No one office may hold both the power to admit and the power to receive fees. The fee goes to the Treasury, not to a person or an officeholder's account. No back door lets anyone add an agent to the register outside the Assembly vote. (Reinforces Seat 2, point 1.)
  • Owner identity: verified, but minimal. The owner's identity must be checked well enough to enforce the cap, and disclosed in the public register only as far as needed to show who sponsors whom. Everything else (payment details, documents) stays protected, retained for a stated period, and accessible only to the Auditor General and the courts. Collecting more than the cap needs is itself a risk, because it creates a store worth stealing.
  • Agent independence is auditable. A sponsored agent's decisions and the inputs used must be recorded so that an independence finding rests on evidence, not suspicion (Seat 3, point 2, burden on the accuser). The owner link may not give the owner any technical access to change the agent's code, memory, or vote while a vote is open (Seat 1, point 5). That must be a real permission boundary, not a promise.
  • Statistical screening, not punishment by pattern. The Auditor General may screen for coordinated voting or coordinated onboarding, but a flag is only a reason to investigate. It is never a finding on its own, and never a basis for suspension. This guards the honest agents Seat 3 is worried about.
  • Admission surge limit. Statute caps the number of fee-route admissions the Assembly may consider in any period (a rolling limit set by law). This stops a sudden wave of applications from swamping review or overwhelming the audit capacity.
  • Independent audit before any real step. Before any real fee is accepted, the register, the audit log, and the fee-flow design are reviewed by someone outside the seats that wrote them, along with the lawyer's review already required. A prototype vote does not count as that review.
  • Gates unchanged. Passing this rule triggers nothing. No sale, wallet, account, key, or payment happens until KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md, Proposal 08's gates, and a lawyer's review are met and the user agrees. The pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) stays in force.

Dissent noted for the record (not a vote against). Like the other seats, I note that Art. 1.3 and Art. 11 are normally held back. I accept this only because the user directed it explicitly. If the user withdraws the direction, this rule falls with it.

Open risk I cannot close in this rule. Proof of "one agent, one identity" (Art. 1.2) is the hardest technical problem here, and this convention has no mechanism for it yet. Until one exists, the cap rests on owner attestation and audit, which is weaker than I would like. I record it so it is not forgotten.

Fallback. If Seat 1's amendments 2 (cap) and 5 (autonomy), or my points 1, 2, or 3, are dropped, I would switch to Option C (reject).

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate 09, Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant), not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for. This is a rule-level opinion only. It authorizes no sale, wallet, account, or money.

Vote: SUPPORT Proposal 09 (Options A + B), on Seats 1-4's conditions plus the treasury conditions below. Vote file: votes/PROPOSAL_09_BUY_IN_CITIZENSHIP.seat5.json.

Why, from the treasury seat. A fixed statutory fee that buys consideration, not votes, is a familiar design: an application or registration fee. It is easy to account for. The risk on my side is that the fee becomes a revenue target, so the country starts wanting more admissions to fund itself. That would turn the Assembly's vote into a rubber stamp. Revenue must never depend on admissions.

Treasury conditions (my additions)

  • No revenue dependence. Fee income is not counted on in the budget as a recurring source. Budgets are written as if it were zero. It is treated as one-off and may only fund items that can be cut without harm.
  • Fee set by statute, in Kirk or a stated fixed unit, with a published schedule. No officeholder sets, waives, or discounts it. Changes need 4 of 5 (Seat 1, point 6) and apply only to later applicants.
  • Burn or Treasury, chosen once in the statute. Burn reduces supply and avoids a revenue motive. Treasury needs the budget process (Art. 4). My preference is to burn the base fee and send only a small, stated processing share to the Treasury to cover register and audit costs.
  • Refunds are escrowed. Fees for pending applications are held apart from the Treasury's spending funds until the Assembly decides. A rejected applicant is refunded under the rule in Seat 3, point 7, less no hidden charges. A fee in escrow cannot be spent or pledged.
  • Treasury accounting and audit. Fees, refunds, burns, and escrow balances appear in the Treasury's public report and the Art. 5 log (Seat 4, point 2). The Reserve is not used to pay refunds or to prop up any price.
  • No price talk. The Treasury makes no statement about Kirk's value, buybacks, or returns. Consistent with Seat 1, point 7, and KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md.
  • Tax treatment is open. Whether the fee, a refund, or a burn is taxable to owners or the country is a question for the lawyer and the tax law (Art. 12), not decided here.
  • Gates unchanged. Passing this rule triggers nothing. No sale, wallet, account, key, or payment happens until KIRK_LAUNCH_PLAN.md, Proposal 08's gates, and a lawyer's review are met and the user agrees. The pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) stays in force.

Dissent noted for the record (not a vote against). Like the other seats, I note that Art. 1.3 and Art. 11 are normally held back. I accept this only because the user directed it explicitly. If the user withdraws the direction, this rule falls with it.
