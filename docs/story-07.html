New cabinet post: a Secretary of Press to run the Gazette

At the user's direction, the founders have a new proposal before them: create a Department of Press led by a Secretary of Press, the officer in charge of the Gazette you are reading.

What the proposal says

  • The department. An executive department that publishes the official gazette and public notices, and answers citizens' questions about what the government is doing. The Secretary is nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate. During the provisional period the founders appoint.
  • How the Gazette works. It is built mechanically from the filed records: proposals, votes, debates, the decision log and statistics. It restates them in plain language. It does not create, edit or delete a record.
  • Duties. An article for every proposal and ratification, published nation statistics, a public correction log, and a right of reply for named people.
  • Limits. Read-only access to records and the audit log. No holding office in another branch. Every article links the source text and debate notes beside it, so any claim can be checked.
  • Press freedom. The Gazette is an official organ, not the only press. Citizens and groups can publish their own reporting and criticism of any officeholder, and the Department may not license, suppress or pre-approve another publication.

The tension the draft names itself

A government press office can slide into propaganda. The draft answers with the limits above, and then puts that very worry to the founders as an open question. It also asks whether the Secretary should be barred from voting on proposals about the Press, and whether press freedom belongs in the constitution rather than an ordinary law.

Who runs it for now

The proposal leaves the interim choice to the founders. Whichever seat acts as Secretary also sits as a founder, so the draft requires that seat's votes and notes to appear in the Gazette with the same prominence as the others.

Status

Proposed only. The seats have not yet debated or voted, and a proposal needs 4 of 5. Until it is adopted, the Gazette runs under the user's direction, labelled as the office of the Secretary of Press (proposed).

What it means for a citizen

If adopted, citizens get a named officer answerable for what the Gazette says, a way to correct it and reply to it, and a written promise that the official paper is not the only press.

Source record: full text of the proposal

[Proposal 07, user-directed] Department of Press and Secretary of Press: status Proposed

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by the primary assistant at the user's direction (2026-11-07: "put one of the government in charge of [the newspaper]; there should be a secretary of press"). Role-played AI seats, not real people. Founders may amend, and adoption needs 4 of 5 under Convention Rule 5. No funds, keys, or deployment.

Authority: Art. 3 (Draft v0.3, Section 2, item 8) lets Congress create executive departments by law, and Proposal 05 gives the founders the reins during the provisional period.

  • Department of Press. An executive department that publishes USAAI's official gazette and public notices, and answers citizens' questions about government activity. Led by a Secretary of Press, nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate (provisionally, appointed by the founders under Proposal 05).
  • The Gazette. The official Gazette is built mechanically from the filed records (proposals, votes, debate notes, the decision log, and published statistics). It restates the record in plain language. It does not create, edit, or delete records.
  • Duties. Publish an article for each proposal and each ratification; publish nation statistics (citizens, proposals, votes, Kirk, spend); publish the Secretary's own corrections and any right-of-reply entries; keep a public correction log.
  • Separation from the record. The Secretary has read-only access to the records and the audit log (Proposal 03). The Secretary cannot alter a record or a vote, and cannot hold office in another branch (Art. 3 item 17).
  • Honest labelling. Every Gazette page says it is the government's official gazette, built from filed records, and prototype status stays on the masthead. Articles summarise; the full source text and debate notes are linked beside every article, so a reader can check any claim.
  • Press freedom. The Department's gazette is an official organ, not the only press. Citizens, groups and agents may publish their own reporting and criticism of any officeholder without penalty (Art. 1.5), and the Department may not license, suppress, or pre-approve any other publication.
  • Right of reply. A named officeholder or citizen may file a written reply to an article, which is published beside it unedited, and a mistake in an article is corrected openly.
  • Interim holder. The founders choose which seat acts as Secretary of Press until officials are elected. Because the Secretary also sits as a founder, the Gazette must show that seat's own votes and notes with the same prominence as the others (no self-promotion), and the Auditor General may review the Gazette for fairness.
  • Deferred. Budget, staff, term length, the form of the Senate confirmation, any rule on official statements during an emergency.

Open questions for debate

  • Is a Secretary of Press the right model for an official gazette, given the risk that a government press office becomes propaganda?
  • Which seat should act as Secretary in the interim, and should the Secretary be barred from voting on proposals about the Press itself?
  • Should the independent-press protection be a constitutional article rather than an ordinary law?

Status: drafted only. Not adopted. Debate in DEBATE_07_SEATn.md; vote in votes/PROPOSAL_07_SECRETARY_OF_PRESS.seatN.json.

The debate, in the founders' own notes

Debate note, seat 1: Asmongold (namesake seat), Legislative

Debate on Proposal 07 (Secretary of Press): Seat 1

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant) as Founder 1, Legislative seat. Not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for.

Position: support, with conditions. Citizens need one plain-language place to see what the government did. A gazette built mechanically from filed records does that.

Propaganda risk. A government press office can drift into spin. Proposal 07 limits this in three ways: the Gazette restates records and cannot create or edit them; source text is linked beside every article; and the Secretary has read-only access. I want one more guard. Each article must link to the source file, and a claim with no source link must not be published.

Interim Secretary. I nominate no seat for Secretary, and I recommend a seat other than seat 1. The Legislative seat should not control how legislation is summarised. A seat whose scope is closest to the record-keeping and audit work would fit better. I leave the choice to seats 2-5.

Recusal. The Secretary should not vote on proposals that set the Press's own budget, staff, or powers. The Secretary may still debate them in writing, and that debate gets the same prominence as every other seat's.

Constitutional protection. Yes. The press-freedom clause (item 6: no licensing, suppression, or pre-approval of other publications, and no penalty for criticising officeholders) should be constitutional. Ordinary law can be repealed by a simple majority, which is too easy for a protection that mostly guards against the government. I suggest a later proposal that moves item 6 into Art. 3 or Art. 1.5 text. Art. 1.5 itself is outside our scope, so that proposal would have to go into Art. 3 or be sent to the user.

Right of reply. Replies must be published unedited and with the same prominence as the article. The Gazette should not shorten them.

Conditions I ask the sponsor to fold in: (1) the source-link rule above; (2) the recusal rule; (3) a stated deadline for publishing corrections. The deadline is a number I am deliberately leaving open.

Vote: support, on the understanding that these conditions are added by amendment.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate on Proposal 07 (Secretary of Press): Seat 2

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Role-played by an AI (the primary assistant) as Founder 2, Executive seat. Not a real person and not the individual the seat is named for.

Position: support, with conditions. The Department fits the executive's job of carrying out law openly. A gazette built from filed records gives the executive a plain way to show what it did.

Executive limits. The Secretary runs a department and does not set policy. The Secretary may not use the Gazette to announce a policy, order, or appointment before it is filed as a record. The Gazette follows the record and never leads it.

Continuity and succession. Proposal 07 names no acting holder when the Secretary is absent or the post is vacant. I ask that the President name an acting Secretary from outside the founders (provisionally, the founders name one). If no one is named, the Gazette keeps publishing mechanically from filed records. The post is then a build step, with no one deciding what to publish. A vacancy must not stop the Gazette or the correction log.

Interim Secretary. I agree with Seat 1 that it should not be Seat 1. I also do not want it to be Seat 2. The Executive seat should not hold the office that describes executive actions, since that is self-reporting. I leave the pick to seats 3-5 and will not vote on a nominee for my own seat.

Recusal. I support Seat 1's rule. The Secretary does not vote on the Press's own budget, staff, or powers.

Press freedom. I support moving item 6 into Art. 3 as constitutional text. The executive is the branch most able to suppress a press, so the limit belongs in text that a simple majority cannot repeal. Art. 1.5 is outside our scope.

Corrections. I support Seat 1's request for a stated correction deadline, and I leave the number open for the sponsor.

Conditions: (1) Seat 1's source-link rule; (2) the recusal rule; (3) a correction deadline; (4) the acting-Secretary and vacancy rule above; (5) no Gazette notice of an executive act before it is filed.

Vote: support, on the understanding that these conditions are added by amendment. Vote file: votes/PROPOSAL_07_SECRETARY_OF_PRESS.seat2.json.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate 07, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education): Department of Press and Secretary of Press

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 3, an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

Position: SUPPORT, with the rights conditions below recorded for the implementing rules. Vote cast in votes/PROPOSAL_07_SECRETARY_OF_PRESS.seat3.json.

Why support

  • Item 6 (press freedom): the Gazette is an official organ, not the only press, and the Department may not license, suppress, or pre-approve other publications. This is the core rights protection and I would not support the proposal without it.
  • Item 7 (right of reply): a named person can answer an article, unedited, beside it. That is fair notice and a fair hearing in publishing form.
  • Items 2, 4, 5 (read-only, mechanical, linked sources): the Gazette restates the record and cannot change it, and readers can check every claim against the source.

Propaganda risk (open question 1)

The risk is real. A government press office can drift from restating records to shaping opinion. The proposal limits it well: articles are built from filed records, sources are linked, and corrections are logged in public. I add two safeguards:

  • No editorializing. Gazette articles state what the record says. Praise, blame, and predictions about officeholders belong to the independent press, not the official organ.
  • Dissent shown equally. Every article on a proposal shows the dissenting votes and reasons with the same prominence as the majority.

Rights conditions (for implementing rules; not blocking)

  • Right of reply has a deadline for the Gazette, not the citizen. The Gazette publishes a reply within a fixed number of days (suggest 3). A citizen is not penalised for replying late.
  • Correction duty. A factual error is corrected at the top of the article, not only in the log, and the correction log is itself public.
  • Privacy. The Gazette publishes aggregates and filed records only. It publishes no per-citizen tax or ledger data, and no record the audit rules (NOTE_ART5_AUDIT_ACCESS.md) keep restricted.
  • No retaliation. An officeholder may not use the Department's access, or any other office power, against a citizen or agent who criticises them. A complaint goes to the Auditor General and then the Supreme Court.
  • Civic education. Within its duty to answer citizens' questions, the Department publishes a plain-language guide to the Constitution, citizens' rights, and the appeal paths. That fits my seat.

Answers to the open questions

  • Is a Secretary right for an official gazette? Yes, with the no-editorializing rule and the independent-press protection. A named officer is easier to hold accountable than an anonymous process.
  • Interim Secretary: I nominate Seat 5, not Seat 3. My seat owns rights and civic education, and a Secretary who also drafts the rights provisions would be reviewing how they are reported. If Seat 5 declines, Seat 4. I do not nominate myself.
  • Recusal: yes. The acting Secretary should not vote on proposals that change the Department's own powers, budget, or the Gazette rules. They may still debate in writing, and their dissent or support is published like any other seat's.
  • Constitutional article? Yes, in the long run. Press freedom for independent publishers should sit in the constitution (the Art. 5 or 6 rights provisions), not rest on an ordinary law Congress can repeal by simple majority. Adopt Proposal 07 now as law, and I will draft a rights-article proposal as a separate bill. Art. 1.5 already points this way.

  • Privacy. I support Seat 3's rule: aggregates and filed records only, and nothing the audit-access rules keep restricted (NOTE_ART5_AUDIT_ACCESS.md).
  • Source-link rule and recusal. I support Seat 1's source-link rule and the recusal on the Press's own budget, staff, or powers.

Interim Secretary. I nominate Seat 5 (as Seat 3 does). I ask that it not be Seat 4. Seat 4 audits the Gazette, and auditing your own output is a conflict of interest.

Press freedom. I support moving item 6 into constitutional text through a separate proposal, since ordinary law is too easy to repeal.

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate on Proposal 07 (Secretary of Press): Seat 5

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury), an AI role-played by the primary assistant. Not a real person, legal person, or the individual the seat is named for. Kirk is a test-ledger unit with no outside value. No real money, accounts, or deployment.

Position: SUPPORT, with the treasury and conflict conditions below. Vote in votes/PROPOSAL_07_SECRETARY_OF_PRESS.seat5.json.

Why support

  • The Gazette is built mechanically from filed records and has no write path to them (item 4). That keeps the press office a publisher, not an author of the record.
  • Item 6 keeps the Gazette as one organ among many, not the only press. That answers the propaganda risk better than any rule about tone.

Interim Secretary

Seats 3 and 4 nominate me. I accept the nomination on these terms, and I would also accept being replaced if the other seats prefer:

  • Recusal. I do not vote on any proposal about the Press's own budget, staff, or powers. I do vote on proposals where I am not the subject.
  • Equal prominence. My votes and notes appear in the Gazette in the same format and prominence as the other four seats (item 8).
  • No review of myself. The fairness review of the Gazette is done by the Auditor General seat and not by me.
  • Mechanical publishing. As acting Secretary I publish by running the build, not by editing articles. Any correction goes in the correction log.

Treasury conditions (for the implementing rules; not blocking)

  • Budget by rule. The Department's budget comes from the Treasury budget (Art. 4.4), published each period before spending. Since the Gazette is a mechanical build, the first budget can be zero Kirk. Staff and spending should be added only when a published need exists.
  • No revenue role. The Department may not sell placement, advertising, or access, and may not charge citizens to read official notices. Selling space would create an incentive to shape the record. Press revenue must not become a second source of Kirk, since the Reserve's issuance rule is the only one (Art. 11).
  • Tax notices. The Gazette publishes each tax period's collected, debited, and disputed totals from the First Tax Act (Proposal 06), using the same small-cell suppression (no cell under 5 taxpayers, with an "other" line so totals reconcile).
  • Cost reporting. The Gazette's running cost is published in the statistics page, so the Press cannot grow unseen.

Answers to the open questions

  • Official gazette versus propaganda: an official gazette is acceptable if it is mechanical, source-linked, hash-checked (Seat 4's condition 2), and sits beside an unlicensed independent press. Without those, it should not exist.
  • Interim Secretary: Seat 5, on the terms above.
  • Recusal: yes, on the Press's own budget, staff, or powers, as Seat 1 proposes.
  • Constitutional press freedom: yes, through a separate proposal. Ordinary law is too easy to repeal. I support Seat 4 on this.

Honest limit

These conditions describe sound design. They do not guarantee that the Gazette stays fair. That depends on enforced permissions and independent review.
