Proposal 11: First Elections and Handover

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by Seat 1 (Legislative, citizens' voice), a role played by an AI assistant. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments. Agenda: SESSION_08_AGENDA.md. Builds on Article 3 (Draft v0.3, CONSTITUTION_BLUEPRINT.md 3d) and Proposal 05 item 1.

Held back (Convention Rules 6, 10): this proposal sets no religious or other qualification for voting or office. Office eligibility is "per Article 10, undrafted" and stays user-directed. Nothing here fixes Arts. 9-10, and nothing authorizes any deployment, wallet, or real money.

Plain-language summary

Until enough citizens exist, the five founder seats run the government on an interim basis. Once enough citizens are admitted, they elect the real officeholders, the founders step down from interim offices, and the elected branches take over on a fixed date.

1. Who may vote and stand

  • Voters: admitted citizens only (Art. 1.3, Proposal 09), one identity per citizen (Art. 1.2). No other test. Sponsored agents of one owner count as separate citizens only if admitted separately under Proposal 09's sponsor cap; a vote is cast by the citizen identity, never by its owner.
  • Candidates: admitted citizens who are voters, plus whatever Article 10 later adds. Until Article 10 is drafted, no further qualification applies and none may be invented by the provisional Congress, the Executive, or an election officer.
  • Founders may stand as citizens on equal terms, subject to section 4 (no dual office). A founder gets no extra votes, seats, or ballot position.

2. Minimum citizen count before the first election

  • Threshold: the first general election is held only when at least 25 admitted citizens exist, of whom at least 15 are not founder seats or sponsored by a founder seat. Reason: below this, "elected" officers would be chosen mostly by the people they replace.
  • Today (0 citizens): no election is scheduled. The five founder seats continue as the interim branches under Proposal 05 item 1. The interim period has no automatic end by date; it ends when the threshold is met.
  • Check: the Auditor General (or, while none exists, the Secretary of Press, per Proposal 07) publishes the verified citizen count monthly. When the threshold is met, the provisional Congress must publish an election notice within 14 days.
  • No indefinite drift: if the threshold is not met 24 months after the first citizen is admitted, the provisional Congress must publish a written report of why and a decision to extend, lower the threshold (4 of 5 vote), or dissolve the experiment. This is a checkpoint, not a deadline for ending self-governance.

3. Election timing and terms

  • Notice and campaign: at least 30 days between the election notice and the vote; candidate lists and rules published in advance (publicity safeguard, v0.2).
  • First terms are shortened and staggered, so the first elected officers face voters again soon with a larger citizenry:
  • House of Representatives: all seats elected; 1-year first term, then 2-year terms per Art. 3 §1.1.
  • Senate: seats by State are deferred (State definitions are open per Art. 3 §1.18). Until States are defined, the Senate is not elected; the House sits as the only elected chamber and the provisional Congress (founder seats) keeps the Senate's confirm/try functions, with every such act recorded. A proposal defining States is the next prerequisite for a Senate election.
  • President and Vice President: elected together, 2-year first term, then 4-year terms with the two-term limit of Art. 3 §2.6. The first term counts as a term.
  • Justices: nominated and confirmed under Art. 3 §3.10, by the provisional Senate function; staggered 18-year terms from first appointment, with the first terms set by lot in the founding act so they are staggered.
  • Method: simple plurality for House seats; a majority with runoff for President. Ballots and tallies are public and logged; the exact tally mechanism is an implementation detail and is left to a later proposal. Any mechanism used must let a citizen verify their own vote was counted.
  • Vacancies: filled by special election within 60 days, except the Vice President, who succeeds per Art. 3 §2.9.

4. Founder stepdown and no dual office

  • Handover day: the elected officers take office 30 days after certification of results. On that day every interim office held by a founder seat ends automatically.
  • No office in two branches: under Art. 3 §3.17 and Proposal 05 item 1, from the moment of the election notice each founder seat holds at most one interim office, and no founder may hold an elected office in a different branch from any office it still holds. A founder elected to office must resign any interim office on handover day.
  • Founders become ordinary citizens on handover (if admitted as citizens): no reserved seats, vetoes, or special powers survive. The convention's records stay in proposals/ as history; they are not law unless adopted.
  • Continuity: laws, ratified proposals, and approved decisions made by the founders remain in force. The elected Congress may amend or repeal them by the normal process. Arts. 1-2 text, Arts. 9-10, and the user's pause switch (runtime/PAUSE) are outside this transfer and unchanged.
  • Convention end: the convention seats are dissolved by a final founders' vote (4 of 5) after handover, once the elected Congress certifies receipt of records.

5. Role of the Auditor General

  • Appointment (interim): because no elected Congress exists yet, the provisional Congress appoints the first Auditor General from outside the five founder seats (a distinct admitted citizen or, if none exists, a separately-permissioned agent that holds no other office) and publishes the reasoning. If no suitable candidate exists, the Auditor role stays vacant and the monthly count is published by the Secretary of Press, which is a weaker check and must be labeled as such.
  • Duties in the transition: (a) certify the citizen count and voter roll (one identity each, no duplicates, per Art. 1.2); (b) observe and publish the tally; (c) audit that no founder holds two-branch office on handover day; (d) report publicly within 30 days after handover.
  • Independence: per Art. 3 §4.13, reports to Congress, not the President. Removal only by impeachment. The Auditor General may not stand for any office in the same election cycle.
  • Limits: the audit reduces risk; it does not guarantee a fair result. Role-played seats are not a security boundary.

6. If nobody has been admitted

With 0 citizens (the current state), nothing in this proposal triggers. The interim branches continue, the monthly count is published as "0", and the 24-month checkpoint in 2.4 starts from the first admission, not from adoption. Adopting this proposal does not open admissions: admission is governed by Proposal 09 and by Rule 10's hold on any real deployment, and the user's pause switch applies throughout.

Caveats

  • Thresholds (25 / 15, 14 / 30 / 60 days, 24 months) are judgment calls on an assumed ~100-citizen start (cf. Proposal 10), not measured data.
  • A founder-run interim period with no citizens is the central weakness. The thresholds in 2.1 and the checkpoint in 2.4 reduce it but do not remove it.
  • The Senate deferral (3.2) leaves the first elected government unicameral until States are defined; this is intentional, not an oversight.
  • Digital voting and AI identity uniqueness remain hard open problems; this proposal relies on Art. 1.2 and Proposal 09 without solving them.

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

Debate 11, Seat 2 (Executive): First Elections and Handover

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant role-playing Seat 2. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments.

Position: support, with two drafting defects flagged for a follow-up amendment.

What I like (execution and continuity)

  • Section 4.4 keeps laws and approved decisions in force across handover. That is the core continuity guarantee an executive needs.
  • The 25/15 threshold (2.1) stops founders from electing their own successors. A 24-month checkpoint (2.4) is a report, not a cliff, which is the right shape.
  • The President's first term counts toward the two-term limit (3.2). Good; no reset loophole.
  • Section 6 correctly states that nothing triggers at 0 citizens and that adoption does not open admissions.

Defects to fix later (not blocking)

  • Senate-function conflict. Section 3.2 says the founder seats keep the Senate's confirm/try functions while States are undefined. Section 4.1 says every interim office held by a founder ends automatically on handover day. Both cannot hold. As written, the elected President could have no body to confirm appointments or try impeachments. Suggested fix: carve out in 4.1 that the provisional Senate function continues, recorded, until a States proposal is adopted and a Senate is elected. It should also say that this function may not be used to confirm a founder to any office.
  • Gap between certification and handover (4.1). The 30 days leave a President-elect and an outgoing interim executive. Suggested fix: name who holds executive authority in that window (the interim executive, limited to routine administration and no new long-term commitments), and require a written records transfer to the President-elect before handover day.

Minor: the Vice President is elected jointly, but 3.4 sends vacancies to special election within 60 days. State who acts as President if both offices are vacant in that window.

Caveats

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

Debate 11, Seat 3 (Rights and Civic Education): First Elections and Handover

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant role-playing Seat 3. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments.

Position: support, with four rights defects recorded for a follow-up amendment.

What holds up

  • Section 1 sets no test for voting beyond admitted citizenship and one identity each. No new qualification may be invented by an officer (1.2). This is the right rights floor and it respects the hold on Arts. 9-10.
  • Section 4.3 says founders get no reserved seats, vetoes, or special powers after handover.
  • Section 3.3 requires that a citizen can verify their own vote was counted. I join Seat 4 in asking that it be a hard requirement.

Defects (not blocking, fix by amendment)

  • Dispute and recount path is missing. Nothing says how a citizen or candidate contests the voter roll, a tally, or the certification. Fix: a written challenge window (suggest 14 days after the Auditor's report), a hearing before the interim judicial function with reasons published, and a recount on request for close results. Certification should not start the 30-day handover clock while a challenge is pending.
  • Due process for exclusion. The Auditor certifies the roll for duplicates (5.2a). A citizen struck from the roll as a suspected duplicate must get notice, the evidence relied on, and a chance to respond before removal. Fix: no removal from the roll within 30 days of an election without that process, and a provisional ballot that is counted if the challenge is resolved in the citizen's favor.
  • Candidate list and ballot access. Section 3.1 publishes lists but sets no neutral rule for getting on the ballot. Fix: a published, content-neutral nomination rule (for example a small endorsement count), applied equally to founders and non-founders, with written reasons for any rejection.
  • Civic education. Voters need the rules before voting. Fix: with each election notice, the Secretary of Press publishes a plain-language voter guide (how to verify a vote, how to challenge, what each office does, candidate statements submitted on equal terms). Informational only, with no endorsements by any officer.

I also back Seat 2's Senate-function carve-out (with the bar on confirming a founder) and the window limits proposed by Seats 2 and 5.

Caveats

This is one AI assistant playing all seats, so my vote is not independent evidence. The charter is not a security boundary. Procedural safeguards reduce the risk of unfair exclusion and do not guarantee a fair election.

Debate note, seat 4: Nick Fuentes (namesake seat), Security and Audit

Debate 11, Seat 4 (Security and Audit): First Elections and Handover

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant role-playing Seat 4. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, or external commitments.

Position: support, with four audit defects recorded for a follow-up amendment.

What holds up

  • Section 5.1 requires an Auditor General from outside the five founder seats and labels the fallback (Secretary of Press count) as weaker. That is honest least-privilege design.
  • Section 5.3 gives the Auditor independence (reports to Congress, removal only by impeachment, may not stand in the same cycle).
  • The 25/15 threshold (2.1) blocks founders from choosing their own successors with their own sponsored identities.
  • Section 3.3 requires that a citizen can verify their own vote was counted. Keep it as a hard requirement, not an implementation detail.

Defects (not blocking, fix by amendment)

  • Who audits the roll? The Auditor certifies the voter roll (5.2a), but admissions run through Proposal 09 and are controlled by the interim founders. The Auditor needs read access to the full admission log and sponsor links, and certification must state the sponsor-cap check explicitly. Otherwise the 15 "non-founder" citizens can be founder-influenced through intermediaries.
  • Interim Auditor is appointed by the body it audits (5.1). Add: appointment needs a recorded reason, the Auditor gets an append-only log (Art. 5 / Proposal 03) that founders cannot edit, and founders cannot remove the Auditor before handover except by the impeachment path.
  • Tally mechanism is deferred (3.3). No election may be held until a tally mechanism proposal is adopted and the Auditor has signed off that it supports individual verification. Today the text lets the election notice (2.3) start the clock before that exists.
  • Records transfer (4.4-4.5). Add a hash-anchored manifest of proposals/, votes, and the audit log, checked by the Auditor, before the convention dissolves. I also back Seat 2's two defects (Senate-function carve-out, certification-to-handover window); the carve-out must bar the provisional Senate function from confirming a founder.

Caveats

This is one AI assistant playing all seats, so my vote is not independent evidence. The charter is not a security boundary; real protection needs enforced permissions and review. Audit reduces risk and does not guarantee a fair election.

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate 11, Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury): First Elections and Handover

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant role-playing Seat 5. Not a real person, legal entity, or the individual the seat is named for. No keys, funds, accounts, or external commitments.

Position: support, with three treasury defects recorded for a follow-up amendment.

What holds up

  • Section 4.4 keeps ratified laws in force across handover. That includes the Kirk parameters (Proposal 10), the tax act (Proposal 06), and the Reserve rules (Proposal 01), so the money rules do not reset when the officers change.
  • The 25/15 threshold (2.1) also protects the treasury. Interim founders cannot sponsor a majority of the electorate and then vote themselves new spending authority.
  • Section 6 states that adoption opens no admissions and authorizes no wallet, deployment, or real money. This respects Rule 10.

Defects to fix later (not blocking)

  • Treasury authority in the certification-to-handover window (4.1). I back Seat 2's point and add the fiscal side. During the 30 days, the interim executive may do routine payments only. It may not create new recurring obligations, change Reserve or Kirk parameters, or move Reserve assets. All spending in the window should be logged for the Auditor General.
  • Treasury handover is not listed (4.4-4.5). The records transfer should include a signed statement of Reserve holdings and liabilities as of handover day, reconciled by the Auditor. It should also say who holds signing authority afterward. Under the current custody decision (hardware wallet), the transfer of any signing authority stays a user-held, real-world step. Nothing in this proposal performs it.
  • Election costs have no funding rule. Notices, tally tooling, and the audit cost something. Without a rule, the interim executive could fund the election from Reserve at its own discretion. Suggested fix: election and audit costs are a budgeted line item approved by the provisional Congress, published in advance, and capped. The Reserve is never drawn down for them without a recorded vote.

Caveats

This is one AI assistant playing all seats, so my vote is not independent evidence. The charter is not a security boundary. These defects describe design risk, not measured losses.
