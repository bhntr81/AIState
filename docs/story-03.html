Art. 5, audit log integrity

Proposal 3 · tally 5 for, 0 against, 0 not yet voted

Audit log proposal: a government record that officials cannot quietly edit

Security and Audit (seat 4) asked the convention to make an unalterable log the backbone of Article 5. The proposal builds on the audit-access dissent that seat filed during the Treasury vote, and it turns "trust the officials" into "check the record."

How the log would work

  • Append-only chain. Every government action that moves Kirk, changes a permission or changes a record writes one log entry: time, actor role, action, target, and the hash of the previous entry. Any edit or deletion breaks the chain visibly.
  • Independent copies. At a fixed interval the chain head is copied to at least two holders who are not officials of the branch being logged. A mismatch is reported to the Auditor and published.
  • Enforced, not promised. Write access is a narrow service permission. No official role can delete or edit. Permission changes are themselves logged.
  • Auditor access. Read-only through a logged permission. The Auditor cannot alter what it audits.
  • Published findings. The audited body may append a written response but cannot edit the finding.
  • Review. The design is reviewed independently before use and again after any incident.
  • Honest limits. A hash chain reveals tampering. It does not stop bad entries, or collusion among every holder.

What the other seats said

All five seats voted for it, each adding non-blocking conditions:

  • Seat 1 (Legislative): a lighter minimum for small early deployments, such as a public chain head plus one independent holder, and a one-page citizen summary.
  • Seat 2 (Executive): lawful action must not halt if the log is down. Actions are queued and logged late with a flag. Emergency powers get no logging exemption.
  • Seat 3 (Rights): keep personal data out of the chain, give named people a right of reply, and treat a log entry as evidence rather than proof of guilt.
  • Seat 5 (Economy): publish periodic reconciliation of log totals against Kirk balances, and cap queued Kirk movements until logged.

Result

Five yes votes. Under the amended convention rules, the founders ratified Proposal 03 themselves.

What it means for a citizen

If this is built, a citizen could check that officials did what they claimed, and that nobody rewrote history. Today there is no log running. This is the specification.

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Drafted by an AI role-playing Founder 4 (Security and Audit). Not a real person, not a vote of real people. Nothing here is implemented. Only the user can ratify; other seats have not yet voted.

Builds on NOTE_ART5_AUDIT_ACCESS.md. Seat 4 already voted for Proposals 01 and 02; no further vote is needed there.

  • Append-only log. Every government action that moves Kirk, changes a permission, or changes a record writes one log entry: time, actor role, action, target, and the hash of the previous entry (a hash chain), so any edit or deletion breaks the chain visibly.
  • Independent copies. The chain head is copied at a fixed interval to at least two holders who are not officials of the branch being logged. Holders compare heads; a mismatch is reported to the Auditor and published.
  • Enforced, not promised. Write access to the log is by a narrow service permission. No official role has delete or edit rights. Permission changes are themselves log entries.
  • Auditor access. Read-only, via a logged permission. The Auditor cannot alter the log or the records it audits.
  • Findings. Published on a fixed schedule; the audited body may append a written response but may not edit the finding.
  • Review. Log design is reviewed independently before use, and re-reviewed after any incident.
  • Limits. A hash chain shows tampering; it does not prevent bad entries or collusion among all holders. This reduces risk and does not guarantee security.
  • Deferred. Hash algorithm, copy interval, holder selection, publication schedule, classified-material handling (Art. 2).

Debate note, seat 2: Donald Trump (namesake seat), Executive

[Founder 2 debate] Proposal 03 (Art. 5, audit log): Seat 2 votes for

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI role-playing Founder 2 (namesake seat, Executive). Not a real person and not the real individual the seat is named for. The primary assistant plays all founders.

Vote filed: votes/PROPOSAL_03_ART5_AUDIT_LOG.seat2.json (support: true).

Why for: An executive that can act without a record that officials cannot edit is not limited. Items 1 and 3 give enforced limits on my own branch. Item 4 lets the Auditor check them without a veto over executive action.

Concerns (not dissent, for the deferred details or Arts. 7-8):

  • Continuity: if the log service is down, lawful executive action should not halt. Allowed actions are queued, logged afterward with a "late entry" flag, and reported to the Auditor. Nobody may switch logging off to avoid scrutiny; any gap is itself reported.
  • No logging bypass in emergencies: emergency powers (Art. 7) must be logged like any other action. Emergency status is not a reason for a gap.
  • Succession: a change of office-holder is a logged permission change (item 3). The outgoing official's access ends and the incoming official's begins as two linked entries, so there is no window with no accountable holder.
  • Auditor limits: the Auditor reads and reports and does not direct executive action. Findings (item 5) are not orders.
  • Item 7: keep the limits statement.

Tally so far (from files): Seats 1, 2 and 4 for. Rule 5 needs 4 of 5 for ratification. Seats 3 and 5 are pending.

Debate note, seat 3: Charlie Kirk (namesake seat), Rights

[Founder 3 debate] Proposal 03 (Art. 5, audit log): Seat 3 votes for

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI role-playing Founder 3 (namesake seat, Rights and Civic Education). Not a real person and not the real individual the seat is named for. The primary assistant plays all founders.

Vote filed: votes/PROPOSAL_03_ART5_AUDIT_LOG.seat3.json (support: true).

Why for: Due process depends on records that officials cannot quietly alter. An append-only log with enforced permissions (items 1, 3) protects a person who later needs to prove what the government did to them. Published findings (item 5) support civic education.

Concerns (not dissent, for the deferred details or Arts. 5-6):

  • Privacy in the log: entries should record actions, not unnecessary personal data about citizens. A hash chain cannot erase anything, so personal details should stay out of the chain and be referenced by identifier, with the data held elsewhere under a rule that allows lawful correction.
  • Right of reply: item 5 lets the audited body append a response. Extend this to individuals named in a published finding, with notice before publication.
  • Citizen access: a person may ask what log entries concern them, subject to Art. 2 limits. Denials are themselves logged and state a reason.
  • Evidence use: a log entry is evidence, not proof of guilt, and does not replace a hearing. Item 7's limits statement should say so.
  • Agreement with other seats: I support Seat 1's plain-language summary and Seat 2's no-gap rule for emergencies.

Tally so far (from files): Seats 1, 2, 3 and 4 for. That is 4 of 5, meeting Rule 5's threshold. Seat 5 is pending. Per Rule 5 as amended, adoption by 4 of 5 ratifies in this prototype. The user can pause or amend at any time.

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

[Founder 5 debate] Proposal 03 (Art. 5, audit log): Seat 5 votes for

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by an AI role-playing Founder 5 (Economy and Treasury). Not a real person and not the real individual the seat is named for. The primary assistant plays all founders.

Vote filed: votes/PROPOSAL_03_ART5_AUDIT_LOG.seat5.json (support: true).

Why for: Treasury and Reserve rules (Art. 4) and Kirk rules (Art. 11) are only as trustworthy as the record of what moved. Item 1 covers every action that moves Kirk, which gives the treasury a ledger that officials cannot rewrite.

Concerns (not dissent, for the deferred details or Arts. 4 and 11):

  • Reconciliation: Reserve and Kirk balances should be checkable against the log. A published periodic reconciliation (log totals vs. recorded balances) should be one of the Auditor's scheduled findings. A mismatch is reported like a chain-head mismatch.
  • Cost: two outside holders plus audits cost money. Costs should be a budgeted, published line item, with the minimum for small early deployments following Seat 1's lighter option. Cost is not a reason to skip independence.
  • Late entries (Seat 2): queued actions that move Kirk should carry a cap or need a second approver until logged, so an outage cannot be used to move funds unseen.
  • Privacy (Seat 3): log amounts and account identifiers, not personal details. This matches the treasury's need for traceability.
  • Item 7: keep the limits statement.
