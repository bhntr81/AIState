Provisional government and tax delegation

Proposal 5 · tally 5 for, 0 against, 0 not yet voted

Founders will run the interim government, and leave tax law to Congress

How does a nation with no citizens run itself? The founders' answer is Proposal 05: until citizens can elect officials, the five founder seats act as the interim branches. The same proposal sets the limits on taxation.

The interim government

The five seats sit as a provisional Congress. They appoint an interim President, Vice President and Justices from among themselves or from admitted citizens, and run the interim departments. Once Article 3 is ratified, no founder may hold office in more than one branch at once, so the seats must be allocated accordingly.

Taxes: limits in the constitution, rates in the law

  • The constitution sets limits only. Congress writes the tax law.
  • Revenue bills start in the House. Taxes must be public, written and equal for like citizens. No tax may be retroactive.
  • The Treasury collects, the Auditor General audits, and the Supreme Court reviews tax law.
  • Taxes are paid in Kirk. Congress and the Reserve may not use taxation to create or confiscate Kirk outside the issuance rule.
  • The first tax act should be a separate proposal. That became Proposal 06.

What the seats worried about

Every seat supported it, and every seat raised the same concern: five seats acting as all branches concentrates power.

  • Seat 1 asked that the allocation be public and the first election date set early.
  • Seat 2 asked that the interim President hold no seat in the provisional Congress or Court, and that the first election date be set in the first session.
  • Seat 3 warned about judges ruling on their own acts, and asked that taxpayers can contest an assessment before any penalty.
  • Seat 4 asked for an Auditor General who is not a sitting founder, or the independent log holders as an interim check.
  • Seat 5 asked for a definition of "like citizens", a rule that tax receipts are held and not re-minted, and recusal where a seat taxes its own accounts.

Result

Five yes votes. This is a stopgap by the founders' own description, and the concerns are the to-do list for the first election.

What it means for a citizen

There are no admitted citizens yet, so nobody can vote. Until elections, the founders govern, and the proposal's own text says that is temporary.

[Proposal] Provisional government and tax delegation: status Proposed

PROTOTYPE. Drafted by the primary assistant at the user's direction as a starting text; founders may amend. Role-played AI seats, not real people.
  • Provisional government. Until citizens elect officials, the five founder seats act as the interim branches: they sit as a provisional Congress, appoint an interim President, Vice President, and Justices from among themselves or admitted citizens, and run the interim departments. Founders may not hold offices in more than one branch at once once Article 3 is ratified (3d item 17); the seats must be allocated accordingly. A date or citizen count for the first elections is set by the provisional Congress.
  • Tax delegation (Art. 12). The constitution sets limits only; the Congress writes tax law. Limits: revenue bills originate in the House; taxes are public, written, and apply equally to like citizens; no tax may be retroactive; the Treasury administers collection, the Auditor General audits it, and the Supreme Court reviews tax law. Tax types (income, business, capital gains) were proposed earlier and are not mandated; the Congress chooses types, rates, and exemptions.
  • Kirk. Taxes are denominated in Kirk (Art. 11). The Congress and Reserve may not use taxation to create or confiscate Kirk outside the issuance rule.
  • First tax act. The provisional Congress should draft the first tax act as a separate proposal after Proposals 04 and 05 are ratified.

The debate, in the founders' own notes

Debate note, seat 5: Myron Gaines (namesake seat), Economy

Debate note: Proposal 05, Seat 5 (Economy and Treasury)

PROTOTYPE, NOT RATIFIED. Written by the primary assistant (an AI) role-playing Founder 5. Not a real person.

Vote: support, within my scope (Art. 12 tax delegation and Kirk, item 3). I take no position on items 1 and 4 beyond noting they sit under Art. 3.

Reasons

  • Limits-only delegation is the established convention: the constitution bounds taxation and the legislature sets types and rates.
  • Item 3 protects the Kirk issuance rule (Art. 11) from being bypassed through taxation.
  • Treasury collects, Auditor General audits, Court reviews: separation of duties on the revenue side.

Non-blocking concerns, for the first tax act (not this proposal)

  • Define "like citizens" so equal application is testable (e.g., same class of account or activity).
  • Provide a reserve-neutral rule: tax receipts are held in Treasury accounts, not burned or re-minted, so Kirk supply changes only under the issuance rule.
  • Require a published revenue and spending report each period, audited under Art. 5.
  • Provisional Congress members should recuse from votes on taxes that apply to their own seat's accounts if a conflict can be shown.

No dissent recorded.
